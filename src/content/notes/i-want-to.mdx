You’ve finished work and want to go home: 집에 가고 싶어요. Add -고 싶다 to a verb stem to say you want to do that action. In polite conversation, finish with -고 싶어요.

<SentenceBlock formal="저는 비빔밥을 먹고 싶습니다." formalRom="jeoneun bibimbabeul meokgo sipseumnida." polite="저는 비빔밥을 먹고 싶어요." politeRom="jeoneun bibimbabeul meokgo sipeoyo." casual="나는 비빔밥을 먹고 싶어." casualRom="naneun bibimbabeul meokgo sipeo." english="I want to eat bibimbap." />

<SentenceBlock formal="저는 케이팝 콘서트를 보고 싶습니다." formalRom="jeoneun keipap konseoteureul bogo sipseumnida." polite="저는 케이팝 콘서트를 보고 싶어요." politeRom="jeoneun keipap konseoteureul bogo sipeoyo." casual="나는 케이팝 콘서트를 보고 싶어." casualRom="naneun keipap konseoteureul bogo sipeo." english="I want to watch a K-pop concert." />

<SentenceBlock formal="한국어를 배우고 싶습니다." formalRom="hangugeoreul baeugo sipseumnida." polite="한국어를 배우고 싶어요." politeRom="hangugeoreul baeugo sipeoyo." casual="한국어를 배우고 싶어." casualRom="hangugeoreul baeugo sipeo." english="I want to learn Korean." />

For an ordinary statement about another person’s desire, use -고 싶어 하다: 동생은 쉬고 싶어 해요. When asking the listener what they want, -고 싶어요? is natural. Direct quotations and some narrative contexts can also contain another person’s -고 싶다; keep the ordinary conversation pattern clear first.

<SentenceBlock formal="재민 씨는 농구를 하고 싶어 합니다." formalRom="jaemin ssineun nongguleul hago sipeo hamnida." polite="재민 씨는 농구를 하고 싶어 해요." politeRom="jaemin ssineun nongguleul hago sipeo haeyo." casual="재민 씨는 농구를 하고 싶어 해." casualRom="jaemin ssineun nongguleul hago sipeo hae." english="Jaemin wants to play basketball." />

<SentenceBlock formal="안나 씨는 제주도에 여행을 가고 싶어 합니다." formalRom="anna ssineun jejudoe yeohaengeul gago sipeo hamnida." polite="안나 씨는 제주도에 여행을 가고 싶어 해요." politeRom="anna ssineun jejudoe yeohaengeul gago sipeo haeyo." casual="안나 씨는 제주도에 여행을 가고 싶어 해." casualRom="anna ssineun jejudoe yeohaengeul gago sipeo hae." english="Anna wants to travel to Jeju Island." />

<Hint type="hint">Compare 저는 가고 싶어요, “I want to go,” 가고 싶어요?, “Do you want to go?”, and 동생은 가고 싶어 해요, “My younger sibling wants to go.” The statement, question, and third-person report use different perspectives.</Hint>

<SentenceBlock formal="제 동생은 가수가 되고 싶어 합니다." formalRom="je dongsaengeun gasuga doego sipeo hamnida." polite="제 동생은 가수가 되고 싶어 해요." politeRom="je dongsaengeun gasuga doego sipeo haeyo." casual="내 동생은 가수가 되고 싶어 해." casualRom="nae dongsaengeun gasuga doego sipeo hae." english="My younger sibling wants to become a singer." />

<Hint type="hint">되다 (to become) is the verb behind every career ambition: 가수가 되고 싶어요 is literally "want to become a singer," not "want to do singer." Watch for the particle in front of it: it's 이/가, the same subject marker from [은/는 vs 이/가](/notes/old-news-new-news), not 을/를. You're not doing the job yet, you're becoming it.</Hint>
