The rule itself you already know by feel: consonant-ending stems take -습니다 (statement) or -습니까? (question); vowel-ending stems take -ㅂ니다 or -ㅂ니까?. What's been missing is when a real person actually reaches for it.

<SentenceBlock formal="무엇을 합니까?" formalRom="mueoseul hamnikka?" polite="뭐 해요?" politeRom="mwo haeyo?" casual="뭐 해?" casualRom="mwo hae?" english="What are you doing?" />

<SentenceBlock formal="수지 씨는 어디에 있습니까?" formalRom="suji ssineun eodie isseumnikka?" polite="수지 씨는 어디에 있어요?" politeRom="suji ssineun eodie isseoyo?" casual="수지 씨는 어디에 있어?" casualRom="suji ssineun eodie isseo?" english="Where is Suji?" />

<SentenceBlock formal="이 모자는 얼마입니까?" formalRom="i mojaneun eolmaimnikka?" polite="이 모자는 얼마예요?" politeRom="i mojaneun eolmayeyo?" casual="이 모자는 얼마야?" casualRom="i mojaneun eolmaya?" english="How much is this hat?" />

<Hint>This is the register for job interviews, formal presentations, news broadcasts, the military, and speaking to a boss or someone significantly senior for the first time. Everyday friendships, even respectful ones, mostly live in polite -요 speech. Formal isn't "more polite than polite," it's a different social mode: distance and structure rather than warmth.</Hint>

<Hint>You'll read this register far more than you'll speak it. Train announcements, product packaging, official notices, and museum placards are almost always written in -습니다/-ㅂ니다, because it reads as neutral and authoritative rather than personal, exactly the tone a sign is going for.</Hint>
