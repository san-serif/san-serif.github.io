The neighborhood bakery used to open at six, but now it opens later: 六時に開かなくなりました. なくなる describes a change into a state where something no longer happens.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="今[いま]はあまり食[た]べなくなりました。" romaji="Ima wa amari tabenaku narimashita." english="I don't eat much anymore these days." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="今[いま]はあまり泣[な]かなくなりました。" romaji="Ima wa amari nakanaku narimashita." english="I don't cry much anymore these days." />

<Hint type="hint">なくなる describes a gradual fade, not a sudden choice, closer to "I've stopped doing this somewhere along the way" than "I decided to quit." ことにする, by contrast, always names a deliberate decision, a useful pair to keep distinct.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="町[まち]が明[あか]るくなって、夜[よる]が怖[こわ]くなくなりました。" romaji="Machi ga akaruku natte, yoru ga kowaku nakunarimashita." english="The town got brighter, and nights stopped being scary." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="車[くるま]が多[おお]くなって、空気[くうき]が綺麗[きれい]じゃなくなりました。" romaji="Kuruma ga ooku natte, kuuki ga kirei ja nakunarimashita." english="There got to be more cars, and the air stopped being clean." />

<Hint type="hint">These sentences chain two なる clauses together, one positive change causing a negative one, 明るくなって(became bright)、怖くなくなりました(stopped being scary), a common way to describe cause and effect unfolding over time.</Hint>
