どう means "how," asking for an opinion, a method, or a state, not a number or a name the way ど-question words like どこ or どの do.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="新[あたら]しい仕事[しごと]はどうですか。" romaji="Atarashii shigoto wa dou desu ka." english="How's the new job?" />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="このプロジェクトをどう思[おも]いますか。" romaji="Kono purojekuto o dou omoimasu ka." english="What do you think about this project?" />

<Hint>どう思いますか is the standard way to ask someone's opinion, literally "how do you think." English needs a different verb, "think," Japanese just reuses どう plus 思う, the same "think" verb from Give and Take.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="この漢字[かんじ]をどう読[よ]みますか。" romaji="Kono kanji o dou yomimasu ka." english="How do you read this kanji?" />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="このコストの問題[もんだい]をどうすればいいですか。" romaji="Kono kosuto no mondai o dou sureba ii desu ka." english="What should be done about this cost problem?" />

<Hint>どうすればいい (literally "if [I] do how, is [it] good") is the fixed way to ask "what should I do about this." It shows up constantly as a complete phrase, worth learning as one chunk rather than building it fresh every time.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="木村[きむら]、デザートはどう。" romaji="Kimura, dezaato wa dou." english="Kimura, how about dessert?" />

<Hint>Dropped down to casual speech, どう alone becomes a quick way to offer something, closer to "how about it?" than a real question about quality. どうですか offers the same thing more politely, and いかがですか is the version reserved for someone senior, offering a guest tea or a boss a drink.</Hint>
