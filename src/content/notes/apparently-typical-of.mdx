A friend says the restaurant is apparently closed, then points to a dish that feels very Japanese. らしい can report something you heard or describe something as typical of its kind; the word before it tells you which job it has.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="難[むずか]しくて大変[たいへん]らしいです。" romaji="Muzukashikute taihen rashii desu." english="Apparently it's difficult and rough." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="よく知[し]りませんが、30年[さんじゅうねん]働[はたら]いているらしいですよ。" romaji="Yoku shirimasen ga, sanjuunen hataraite iru rashii desu yo." english="I don't know the details, but apparently they've worked there for thirty years." />

<Hint type="hint">そうだ reports something you heard directly and fairly recently; らしい carries a slightly vaguer, more secondhand feel, "from what I gather," often used when you're not totally sure of the source or the details.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="ああ、北海道[ほっかいどう]らしいラーメンですね。" romaji="Aa, Hokkaidou rashii raamen desu ne." english="Ah, that's such a Hokkaido-style ramen." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="ああ、京都[きょうと]らしい建物[たてもの]ですね。" romaji="Aa, Kyouto rashii tatemono desu ne." english="Ah, that's such a Kyoto-style building." />

<Hint type="hint">Attached straight to a noun with no verb involved, X らしい means "very X," embodying everything typical or expected of that category, 北海道らしい, "quintessentially Hokkaido," a specific compliment about fitting an image perfectly.</Hint>
