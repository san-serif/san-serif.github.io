から見ると and からすると introduce a standpoint or evidence used to make a judgment: “from X, it looks like…” にしたら frames the matter from a person's perspective, often imagining how the situation feels to them. These are close to にとって from the N3 lesson, but add a stronger sense of judging from evidence or stepping into someone else's position.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="データから見[み]ると、この地域[ちいき]では電車[でんしゃ]が一番[いちばん]便利[べんり]です。" romaji="Deeta kara miru to, kono chiiki de wa densha ga ichiban benri desu." english="Looking at the data, trains are the most convenient in this area." />

から見ると presents an evaluation from a particular angle, such as data, a profession, or a set of values. The literal “looking from” does not need to be visual: 経済の面から見ると, “from an economic perspective.”

<JSentenceBlock furigana="空[そら]の様子[ようす]からすると、午後[ごご]は雨[あめ]になりそうです。" romaji="Sora no yousu kara suru to, gogo wa ame ni narisou desu." english="Judging from the sky, it looks like it will rain this afternoon." />

からすると often draws an inference from clues or evidence. It can overlap with から見ると, but the “judging from” reading is especially natural when the speaker is using a sign or fact to reach a conclusion.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="初[はじ]めて日本[にほん]に来[き]た人[ひと]にしたら、漢字[かんじ]は難[むずか]しいでしょう。" romaji="Hajimete Nihon ni kita hito ni shitara, kanji wa muzukashii deshou." english="From the perspective of someone visiting Japan for the first time, kanji must be difficult." />

にしたら takes a person as its reference point: “for them / from their perspective.” It can show empathy or acknowledge that the same situation may feel different to someone else.

The noun + 上 form adds “from the standpoint of” or “in terms of”: 経済上の理由, “for economic reasons”; 安全上問題がある, “there is a problem from a safety standpoint.” It is common in formal compounds and reports. Unlike からすると, it often names a domain or criterion rather than evidence used to make an inference.

ところを見ると draws an inference from an observable clue: 雲の様子を見ると, “judging from the clouds.” からして can also mean “judging from,” but may emphasize that even the first or most basic evidence points to a conclusion. と考えられる is a cautious formal “it can be considered / is thought to be.” 確かに acknowledges that a point is certainly true, often before adding a qualification.

<Hint type="insight">In real life, からすると is common when giving a cautious assessment from available clues: 症状からすると、風邪かもしれません, “Judging from the symptoms, it might be a cold.” It leaves room for uncertainty; the phrase identifies the basis for the inference instead of presenting it as a proven fact.</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">Use にとって to say what matters or is useful for someone; use にしたら to imagine the situation from their side; use から見ると / からすると to name the angle or evidence supporting your own judgment.</Hint>
