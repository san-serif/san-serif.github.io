を marks the direct object of a verb, the thing being acted on. It also has a second job with verbs of movement, marking the space something travels through rather than a destination.

### を: The Direct Object

<JSentenceBlock furigana="漢字[かんじ]を書[か]きます。" romaji="Kanji o kakimasu." english="I write kanji." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="日本語[にほんご]のニュースを聞[き]きます。" romaji="Nihongo no nyuusu o kikimasu." english="I listen to Japanese news." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="明日[あした]安[やす]いホテルを探[さが]しましょう。" romaji="Ashita yasui hoteru o sagashimashou." english="Let's look for a cheap hotel tomorrow." />

<Hint>を is the most ordinary job this particle does, marking whatever noun the verb is directly doing something to. It shows up constantly paired with a noun+する verb too, 発表をします (to give a presentation), 予約をします (to make a reservation), the noun takes を before する turns it into an action.</Hint>

### を: Passing Through

<JSentenceBlock furigana="この道[みち]をまっすぐ行[い]ってください。" romaji="Kono michi o massugu itte kudasai." english="Please go straight along this road." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="次[つぎ]の角[かど]を右[みぎ]に曲[ま]がってください。" romaji="Tsugi no kado o migi ni magatte kudasai." english="Please turn right at the next corner." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="山道[やまみち]を登[のぼ]ります。" romaji="Yamamichi o noborimasu." english="I climb up the mountain trail." />

<Hint>None of these verbs, 行く, 曲がる, 登る, are being done "to" the road or the corner the way 書く is done to a kanji. を here marks the space the movement happens through, not a target being acted on. English keeps this distinction with different prepositions, "along," "at," "up", Japanese uses the same particle for all of it.</Hint>
