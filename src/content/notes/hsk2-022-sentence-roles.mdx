Someone asks the time, then asks how much the notebook costs. In Chinese, short facts like these can finish with a noun or number phrase—no 是 needed. It’s a useful pattern for days, times, ages, and prices.

<CSentenceBlock simplified="今天星期二。" traditional="今天星期二。" pinyin="jīntiān xīngqī'èr." jyutping="gam1 tin1 sing1 kei4 ji6." english="Today is Tuesday." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="现在三点半。" traditional="現在三點半。" pinyin="xiànzài sān diǎn bàn." jyutping="jin6 zoi6 saam1 dim2 bun3." english="It’s half past three." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="这本笔记本二十块钱。" traditional="這本筆記本二十塊錢。" pinyin="zhè běn bǐjìběn èrshí kuài qián." jyutping="ze5 bun2 bat1 gei3 bun2 ji6 sap6 faai3 cin2." english="This notebook costs twenty yuan." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我妹妹今年十八岁。" traditional="我妹妹今年十八歲。" pinyin="wǒ mèimei jīnnián shíbā suì." jyutping="ngo5 mui6 mui6 gam1 nin4 sap6 baat3 seoi3." english="My younger sister is eighteen this year." />

<Hint type="hint">For many simple facts, Chinese puts the information directly after the subject: 今天星期二, “Today [is] Tuesday.” Use 是 when you identify or classify someone or something: 她是老师 (“She’s a teacher”).</Hint>
