You cannot stop worrying about tomorrow's presentation, no matter how often you check your notes. てしょうがない describes a feeling that is hard to contain; ようがない says there is no way to do something.

These patterns express intensity and limits. てしょうがない, てたまらない, and てならない say a feeling is so strong it cannot be helped; ないではいられない and ずにはいられない say the speaker cannot stop themselves from acting. て当然だ says an outcome is only natural. ようがない means there is no way to do something, often because the method or information is missing. さすが marks something as “just what you'd expect”; だけのことはある and だけあって recognize that a result matches someone's effort or reputation. ものがある expresses a noticeable quality or strong impression. 何といっても emphasizes the most important point, を込めて means “putting a feeling into” an action or object, and 甲斐がある says effort was worthwhile. がち means prone to or often tends to; ぎみ means slightly or somewhat. っぽい describes a quality that seems characteristic, sometimes with a critical tone.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="結果[けっか]が気[き]になってしょうがありません。" romaji="Kekka ga ki ni natte shou ga arimasen." english="I can't help worrying about the result." />

てしょうがない (also てしかたがない) follows a feeling or state and expresses an overwhelming intensity. It is stronger than simply saying 気になる and often describes something the speaker cannot control.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="試験[しけん]の結果[けっか]が心配[しんぱい]でたまりません。" romaji="Shiken no kekka ga shinpai de tamarimasen." english="I'm unbearably worried about the exam results." />

てたまらない is very close to てしょうがない and often describes a strong physical or emotional feeling. てならない tends to sound more written or reflective: 不思議でならない, “I can't help finding it strange.”

<JSentenceBlock furigana="一生懸命[いっしょうけんめい]練習[れんしゅう]したのだから、上達[じょうたつ]して当然[とうぜん]です。" romaji="Isshoukenmei renshuu shita no dakara, joutatsu shite touzen desu." english="Since you practiced so hard, it's only natural that you improved." />

て当然だ presents a result as expected or justified by the circumstances. さすが can praise a result that matches a reputation: さすがプロだ, “that's what you'd expect of a pro.” だけのことはある similarly says a result reflects someone's effort or ability.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="毎日[まいにち]練習[れんしゅう]しただけあって、動[うご]きが自然[しぜん]です。" romaji="Mainichi renshuu shita dake atte, ugoki ga shizen desu." english="As you'd expect after practicing every day, the movements are natural." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="連絡先[れんらくさき]が分[わ]からないので、確[たし]かめようがありません。" romaji="Renrakusaki ga wakaranai node, tashikameyou ga arimasen." english="I don't know the contact details, so there's no way to check." />

Verb stem + ようがない means no method exists to do something. It differs from できない, which states inability without explaining that the method is unavailable.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="その話[はなし]を聞[き]いたら、笑[わら]わずにはいられませんでした。" romaji="Sono hanashi o kiitara, warawazu ni wa iraremasen deshita." english="After hearing that story, I couldn't help laughing." />

<Hint type="insight">A host might say さすがですね to praise a skill the listener is known for. だけのことはある more explicitly ties the result to the effort: 毎日練習しただけのことはある, “the daily practice paid off.”</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">Overwhelming feeling: てしょうがない. Natural outcome: て当然. No available way: Vようがない. Expected excellence: さすが. Result worthy of effort: だけのことはある.</Hint>
