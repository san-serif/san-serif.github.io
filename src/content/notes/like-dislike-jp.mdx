好き (like) and 嫌い (dislike) are な-adjectives, not verbs, even though English "like" and "dislike" feel like actions. Whatever's liked or disliked takes が, the same pattern ほしい and 分かる both run on.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="私[わたし]は映画[えいが]が好[す]きです。でも、テレビドラマは嫌[きら]いです。" romaji="Watashi wa eiga ga suki desu. Demo, terebi dorama wa kirai desu." english="I like movies. But I dislike TV dramas." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="私[わたし]は子供[こども]の時[とき]、ジュースが好[す]きでした。でも、牛乳[ぎゅうにゅう]は嫌[きら]いでした。" romaji="Watashi wa kodomo no toki, juusu ga suki deshita. Demo, gyuunyuu wa kirai deshita." english="When I was a kid, I liked juice. But I disliked milk." />

<Hint>好き conjugates exactly like any other な-adjective: 好きです, 好きでした, 好きじゃないです, 好きじゃなかったです. There's no separate verb form hiding underneath it, and が never becomes を no matter how action-like "like" feels in English.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="どんな音楽[おんがく]が好[す]きですか。" romaji="Donna ongaku ga suki desu ka." english="What kind of music do you like?" />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="田中[たなか]さんが嫌[きら]いな食[た]べ物[もの]は何[なん]ですか。" romaji="Tanaka-san ga kiraina tabemono wa nan desu ka." english="What food does Tanaka dislike?" />

<Hint>Modifying a noun directly needs 好きな or 嫌いな, the な that gives な-adjectives their name: 嫌いな食べ物 (disliked food), not 嫌い食べ物. 大好き and 大嫌い push the same words to an extreme, "love" and "really can't stand," just by adding 大 (big) on the front.</Hint>
