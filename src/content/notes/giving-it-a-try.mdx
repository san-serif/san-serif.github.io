<JapaneseLessonHead />

てみる attaches to a verb's て-form and means "try doing it," testing something out to see what happens. みる here comes from 見る, to look or see, but once attached to て it no longer means "look," it means "try and find out."

<JSentenceBlock furigana="大学[だいがく]の本屋[ほんや]に行[い]ってみました。" romaji="Daigaku no hon'ya ni ittemimashita." english="I tried going to the university bookstore." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="色々[いろいろ]なクラスに出[で]てみました。" romaji="Iroiro na kurasu ni detemimashita." english="I tried sitting in on various classes." />

<Hint>てみる conjugates exactly like いる at the end, てみます, てみました, てみたい, てみたことがある, layering neatly onto patterns you already know. 図書館で本を借りてみました combines it with a plain past action, "I tried borrowing a book from the library."</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="面白[おもしろ]い映画[えいが]を見[み]てみたらどうですか。" romaji="Omoshiroi eiga o mitemitara dou desu ka." english="Why don't you try watching an interesting movie?" />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="富士山[ふじさん]に登[のぼ]ってみたいです。" romaji="Fujisan ni nobottemitai desu." english="I'd like to try climbing Mt. Fuji." />

<Hint>てみたらどうですか, built on たら, softens a suggestion into "why don't you try," and てみたい, built on たい, expresses wanting to try something you haven't done yet, both leaning on grammar you've already met.</Hint>
