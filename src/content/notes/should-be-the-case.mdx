<JapaneseLessonHead />

はず attaches to the plain form and says something should logically be true, based on evidence or reasoning, not just a guess the way かもしれない or だろう would be.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="トムさんは、昨日[きのう]クラスで発表[はっぴょう]をしたはずです。" romaji="Tomu-san wa, kinou kurasu de happyou o shita hazu desu." english="Tom should have given his presentation in class yesterday." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="トムさんは、来週[らいしゅう]暇[ひま]なはずです。" romaji="Tomu-san wa, raishuu hima na hazu desu." english="Tom should be free next week." />

<Hint>だろう marks an open guess, かもしれない marks a bare possibility, but はず sits above both, a conclusion you're confident in because you have a specific reason to believe it, a schedule, a pattern, something you know.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="毎日[まいにち]開[あ]いているから、使[つか]えるはずです。" romaji="Mainichi aiteiru kara, tsukaeru hazu desu." english="It's open every day, so it should be usable." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="毎学期[まいがっき]先生[せんせい]が変[か]わるから、田中[たなか]先生[せんせい]じゃないはずです。" romaji="Maigakki sensei ga kawaru kara, Tanaka-sensei janai hazu desu." english="The teacher changes every semester, so it shouldn't be Tanaka-sensei." />

<Hint>な-adjectives and nouns insert な before はず, 暇なはず, just as they do before の or のに, while い-adjectives and verbs attach directly. The reasoning behind はず is often spelled out explicitly with から right before it, as both examples above show.</Hint>
