These N3 phrases all attach to a noun, but describe different relationships. について and に関して introduce a topic (“about”); に対して marks something directed toward a person or thing, or sets up a contrast; にとって gives the point of view that matters (“for / to”).

<JSentenceBlock furigana="この計画[けいかく]について、もう少[すこ]し相談[そうだん]したいです。" romaji="Kono keikaku ni tsuite, mou sukoshi soudan shitai desu." english="I'd like to discuss this plan a little more." />

について means “about” or “concerning” and is common in conversation and writing. に関して has the same basic meaning but sounds more formal and is common in reports, announcements, or careful professional speech. Both can modify a following noun with の: 環境についての本, 環境に関する本, “a book about the environment.”

Other particles also combine with の to modify a noun: 友達との約束, “a promise with a friend”; 日本への旅行, “a trip to Japan”; 会議での発言, “remarks at the meeting.” The particle keeps its relationship to the first noun, while の lets the whole phrase describe the next noun.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="社員[しゃいん]に対[たい]して、失礼[しつれい]なことを言[い]ってはいけません。" romaji="Shain ni taishite, shitsurei na koto o itte wa ikemasen." english="You shouldn't say rude things to employees." />

に対して can mark the target of an action, comment, or attitude: “toward / to.” To contrast two full clauses, use clause + のに対して: it sets the first situation against the second, often with the sense “while / whereas.”

<JSentenceBlock furigana="兄[あに]は毎日[まいにち]自炊[じすい]するのに対[たい]して、弟[おとうと]は外食[がいしょく]します。" romaji="Ani wa mainichi jisui suru no ni taishite, otouto wa gaishoku shimasu." english="While my older brother cooks for himself every day, my younger brother eats out." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="私[わたし]にとって、家族[かぞく]と過[す]ごす時間[じかん]が一番[いちばん]大切[たいせつ]です。" romaji="Watashi ni totte, kazoku to sugosu jikan ga ichiban taisetsu desu." english="For me, time spent with family is the most important." />

にとって frames a judgment from someone's point of view, “for / to [person].” It does not mark a recipient or target; it tells us whose values or experience the statement is based on.

When you want to focus on one particular aspect, use a clause or noun phrase + という点で, “in terms of / in the respect that.” The quoted clause names the point being considered; it does not identify whose viewpoint matters. にかかわる means “related to / concerning” and often modifies a noun: 命にかかわる問題, “a life-threatening issue.” It signals direct involvement, not simply a broad topic for discussion. には can mark what is needed in order to do something: この漢字を読むには辞書が必要です, “you need a dictionary to read this kanji.” It can also set a topic or criterion, as in この仕事には経験が必要, “experience is needed for this job.” The predicate clarifies whether it means “for/in order to” or “for this matter.”

<JSentenceBlock furigana="毎日[まいにち]練習[れんしゅう]しているという点[てん]で、彼[かれ]は評価[ひょうか]されています。" romaji="Mainichi renshuu shite iru to iu ten de, kare wa hyouka sarete imasu." english="He is valued in that he practices every day." />

For a particular standard or perspective, use [noun] + で言[い]うと, “speaking in terms of…” or “by the measure of…”. It often narrows what the speaker is discussing rather than changing the grammar of the rest of the sentence.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="価格[かかく]で言[い]うと、こちらの店[みせ]の方[ほう]が安[やす]いです。" romaji="Kakaku de iu to, kochira no mise no hou ga yasui desu." english="In terms of price, this shop is cheaper." />

For a particular skill or field, noun + にかけては means “when it comes to…” and often highlights an area where someone excels. To frame an assessment by a criterion, [noun/clause] + という点から考[かんが]えると means “considering it from the standpoint that…”

<JSentenceBlock furigana="料理[りょうり]にかけては、祖母[そぼ]にかなう人[ひと]はいません。" romaji="Ryouri ni kakete wa, sobo ni kanau hito wa imasen." english="When it comes to cooking, no one can match my grandmother." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="予算[よさん]という点[てん]から考[かんが]えると、A案[あん]が現実的[げんじつてき]です。" romaji="Yosan to iu ten kara kangaeru to, A-an ga genjitsuteki desu." english="Considering the budget, proposal A is realistic." />

<Hint type="insight">In real life, について and に関して are useful in work or school when introducing a topic: プロジェクトについてご相談します, “I'd like to consult with you about the project.” Use について for an ordinary topic and に関して when you want the more formal, report-like tone.</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">Keep the noun phrase straight: topic = について / に関して; target or contrast = に対して; viewpoint = にとって. English “to” or “about” can translate more than one of them, so choose by the role the noun plays in the sentence.</Hint>
