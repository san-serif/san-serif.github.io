The last train is leaving and you still have a report to submit. っこない says an outcome is impossible; ざるを得ない says there is no real choice but to act.

These forms express different degrees of force. っこない is a blunt, conversational “there's no way”; ざるを得ない is a formal “have no choice but to”; and なくはない carefully concedes that something is possible, while implying it may be difficult or limited. Verb stem + 得る (える) means something can happen, while 得ない (えない) means it is impossible or inconceivable. まい expresses a firm intention not to do something or a formal negative guess. ものか is a strong, often emotional “no way!” These are not interchangeable versions of “can/can't.”

<JSentenceBlock furigana="そんな短[みじか]い時間[じかん]では、全部[ぜんぶ]終[お]わりっこないよ。" romaji="Sonna mijikai jikan de wa, zenbu owari kko nai yo." english="There's no way we'll finish everything in such a short time." />

っこない attaches to a verb stem and makes a strong, informal prediction that something cannot happen. The more standard form is わけがない. Avoid っこない in formal writing or when its bluntness could sound dismissive.

Verb stem + 得る (える) is a formal possibility, often translated “can occur”; its negative 得ない (えない) means “cannot happen” or “is inconceivable.” This is about whether an event is possible, not a person's learned ability, which uses potential conjugation.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="こんな条件[じょうけん]では、引[ひ]き受[う]けるものか。" romaji="Konna jouken de wa, hikiukeru mono ka." english="With conditions like these, there's no way I'm taking it on." />

まい is a formal negative volitional: もう同じ間違いはするまい, “I won't make the same mistake again.” With する, both するまい and すまい occur. てはいられない says the speaker cannot keep doing or not doing something because action is urgent: もう待ってはいられない, “we can't keep waiting.”

<JSentenceBlock furigana="大切[たいせつ]な書類[しょるい]なので、内容[ないよう]を確認[かくにん]せざるを得[え]ません。" romaji="Taisetsu na shorui na node, naiyou o kakunin sezaru o emasen." english="Because these are important documents, we have no choice but to check the contents." />

ざるを得ない expresses a decision forced by circumstances. Attach it to the negative verb stem; する changes to せざる. It is formal and often carries a sense of reluctant necessity.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="少[すこ]し難[むずか]しいですが、できなくはありません。" romaji="Sukoshi muzukashii desu ga, dekinaku wa arimasen." english="It's a little difficult, but it isn't impossible." />

なくはない is a double negative that allows a limited “not impossible” reading. It often softens an answer rather than offering enthusiastic approval.

<Hint type="insight">In a casual conversation, そんなの食べられっこない, “there's no way I can eat that,” is emphatic. In a work update, 見直さざるを得ない sounds measured and formal. できなくはない leaves room for reservations.</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">っこない = strong and casual impossibility; ざるを得ない = reluctant necessity; なくはない = qualified possibility. Check the register before choosing.</Hint>
