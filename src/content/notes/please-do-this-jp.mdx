〜てください attaches to a verb's て-form to mean "please do this." Swap in the negative て-form, 〜ないでください, and it flips to "please don't."

<JSentenceBlock furigana="そこに立[た]ってください。" romaji="Soko ni tatte kudasai." english="Please stand there." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="靴[くつ]を脱[ぬ]いでください。" romaji="Kutsu o nuide kudasai." english="Please take off your shoes." />

<Hint>てください is built on the exact same て-form you already use for connecting actions and for 〜ている, it's not a separate conjugation to learn, just a new job for a shape you already know.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="美術館[びじゅつかん]では、大[おお]きい鞄[かばん]を持[も]って歩[ある]かないでください。" romaji="Bijutsukan dewa, ookii kaban o motte arukanaide kudasai." english="Please don't carry a large bag around in the art museum." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="絵[え]の前[まえ]では、写真[しゃしん]を撮[と]らないでください。" romaji="E no mae dewa, shashin o toranaide kudasai." english="Please don't take photos in front of the paintings." />

<Hint>ないでください is built the same way, just on the verb's plain negative stem instead of the plain て-form: 撮る becomes 撮らない (don't take), then 撮らないでください (please don't take). It's the polite version of the plain-form ないで from Without Doing, dressed up as a request instead of a connector.</Hint>
