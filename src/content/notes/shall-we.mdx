You’re standing outside the cinema and deciding together: 들어갈까요? Attach -(으)ㄹ까요? to suggest an action or ask what someone thinks. The stem ending determines whether to use -ㄹ까요 or -을까요.

### Shall We?

<SentenceBlock polite="오늘 같이 저녁을 먹을까요?" politeRom="oneul gachi jeonyeogeul meogeulkkayo?" casual="오늘 같이 저녁을 먹을까?" casualRom="oneul gachi jeonyeogeul meogeulkka?" english="Shall we eat dinner together tonight?" />

<SentenceBlock polite="주말에 같이 영화를 볼까요?" politeRom="jumare gachi yeonghwareul bolkkayo?" casual="주말에 같이 영화를 볼까?" casualRom="jumare gachi yeonghwareul bolkka?" english="Shall we watch a movie together this weekend?" />

<SentenceBlock polite="여기에서 사진을 찍을까요?" politeRom="yeogieseo sajineul jjigeulkkayo?" casual="여기에서 사진을 찍을까?" casualRom="yeogieseo sajineul jjigeulkka?" english="Shall we take a photo here?" />

<SentenceBlock polite="오늘 산책할까요?" politeRom="oneul sanchaekhalkkayo?" casual="오늘 산책할까?" casualRom="oneul sanchaekhalkka?" english="Shall we take a walk today?" />

<Hint type="hint">Dropping straight to 을까?/ㄹ까? with no 요 is exactly how Koreans text friends about plans: 먹을까? on its own is a complete, natural "wanna eat?" No subject, no object needed when it's obviously about the two of you.</Hint>

### Do You Think...?

The same ending doubles as wondering out loud or asking someone's opinion, not just proposing a plan together.

<SentenceBlock polite="이 식당이 좋을까요?" politeRom="i sikdangi joeulkkayo?" casual="이 식당이 좋을까?" casualRom="i sikdangi joeulkka?" english="Do you think that restaurant is good?" />

<SentenceBlock polite="내일 날씨가 추울까요?" politeRom="naeil nalssiga chuulkkayo?" casual="내일 날씨가 추울까?" casualRom="naeil nalssiga chuulkka?" english="Do you think it will be cold tomorrow?" />

<SentenceBlock polite="저는 게임을 좋아해요. 그럼 같이 게임할까요?" politeRom="jeoneun geimeul joahaeyo. geureom gachi geimhalkkayo?" casual="나는 게임을 좋아해. 그럼 같이 게임할까?" casualRom="naneun geimeul joahae. geureom gachi geimhalkka?" english="I like games. Then shall we play games together?" />

<Hint type="insight">Notice there's no formal register here. -(으)ㄹ까요 already carries a built-in softness (it's a question, a proposal, never a command), so Korean doesn't reach for the stiffer -습니다 style with it in everyday use. You'll see this ending almost exclusively in polite or casual speech.</Hint>

### Suggest It With the Ordinary Polite Form

A plain -아요/어요 sentence can also make a suggestion when the situation and intonation invite doing something together: 같이 가요, “Let’s go together.” -(으)ㄹ까요? explicitly asks for the listener’s opinion; 같이 가요 puts the proposed action forward directly.

<SentenceBlock formal="같이 갑시다." formalRom="gachi gapsida." polite="같이 가요." politeRom="gachi gayo." casual="같이 가자." casualRom="gachi gaja." english="Let’s go together." />

<SentenceBlock formal="여기에서 잠깐 쉽시다." formalRom="yeogieseo jamkkan swipsida." polite="여기에서 잠깐 쉬어요." politeRom="yeogieseo jamkkan swieoyo." casual="여기에서 잠깐 쉬자." casualRom="yeogieseo jamkkan swija." english="Let’s rest here for a moment." />
