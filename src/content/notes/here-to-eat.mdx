You already know 을/를 해요 for stating an activity plainly. Attach -(으)러 to a verb stem instead, and the sentence becomes "go (somewhere) in order to (verb)": 하러 always needs a travel verb riding along with it, usually 가요 or 와요. You can't just say 하러 and stop.

### How Often

자주 (often), 거의 안 (almost never), 별로 안 (not really, not much) all slot in right before the verb. 거의 안 denies something almost completely; 별로 안 is softer, closer to "not particularly."

<SentenceBlock formal="바다에 자주 갑니다." formalRom="badae jaju gamnida." polite="바다에 자주 가요." politeRom="badae jaju gayo." casual="바다에 자주 가." casualRom="badae jaju ga." english="I often go to the beach." />

<SentenceBlock formal="봉사활동은 별로 안 합니다." formalRom="bongsahwaldongeun byeollo an hamnida." polite="봉사활동은 별로 안 해요." politeRom="bongsahwaldongeun byeollo an haeyo." casual="봉사활동은 별로 안 해." casualRom="bongsahwaldongeun byeollo an hae." english="I don't volunteer much." />

<SentenceBlock formal="저는 일본 음식을 자주 먹습니다." formalRom="jeoneun ilbon eumsigeul jaju meokseumnida." polite="저는 일본 음식을 자주 먹어요." politeRom="jeoneun ilbon eumsigeul jaju meogeoyo." casual="나는 일본 음식을 자주 먹어." casualRom="naneun ilbon eumsigeul jaju meogeo." english="I often eat Japanese food." />

<Hint>거의 안 and 별로 안 aren't interchangeable. 거의 안 가요 ("I almost never go") describes something close to never happening at all. 별로 안 좋아해요 ("I don't really like it") is much gentler, closer to lukewarm than to outright dislike.</Hint>

<SentenceBlock formal="비빔밥을 먹으러 한국 식당에 갔습니다." formalRom="bibimbabeul meogeureo hanguk sikdange gatseumnida." polite="비빔밥을 먹으러 한국 식당에 갔어요." politeRom="bibimbabeul meogeureo hanguk sikdange gasseoyo." casual="비빔밥을 먹으러 한국 식당에 갔어." casualRom="bibimbabeul meogeureo hanguk sikdange gasseo." english="I went to a Korean restaurant to eat bibimbap." />

<Hint>하러 doesn't change shape for tense. The 갔어요 at the end carries the past tense from [-았/었어요](/notes/already-done); 하러 (or 먹으러 here) stays exactly the same whether the trip already happened, is happening, or hasn't happened yet.</Hint>
