You spot a new book on the desk and tell a friend whose it is: 这是我的新书. The description and owner come before 书, with 的 linking a modifier to the noun. Chinese also tends to place time and location before the action, so a sentence often sets the scene before saying what happens.

### Examples

<CSentenceBlock simplified="这是一本新书。" traditional="這是一本新書。" pinyin="Zhè shì yì běn xīn shū." jyutping="ze5 si6 jat1 bun2 san1 syu1." english="This is a new book." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我今天在家学习。" traditional="我今天在家學習。" pinyin="Wǒ jīntiān zài jiā xuéxí." jyutping="ngo5 gam1 tin1 zoi6 gaa1 hok6 zaap6." english="I’m studying at home today." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="他很认真地听。" traditional="他很認真地聽。" pinyin="Tā hěn rènzhēn de tīng." jyutping="taa1 han2 jing6 zan1 dei6 teng1." english="He listens attentively." />

<Hint type="hint">Do not place an English-style adjective after the noun. Chinese says 新书, literally “new book,” and 我的书, “my book.”</Hint>
