に marks a fixed point in space, where something exists or ends up placed. で marks the location an action happens in. Both translate as "at" or "in" in English, but they answer different questions: に asks "where does it sit," で asks "where does it happen."

<JSentenceBlock furigana="このノートに名前[なまえ]を書[か]いてください。" romaji="Kono nooto ni namae o kaite kudasai." english="Please write your name on this notebook." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="ここにプレゼントを置[お]いてください。" romaji="Koko ni purezento o oite kudasai." english="Please put the present here." />

<Hint>書く and 置く both end with something landing at a fixed spot, the name ends up on the notebook, the present ends up here, which is exactly what に marks, the destination point of the result, not the setting of an ongoing activity.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="学校[がっこう]で友達[ともだち]とテニスをします。" romaji="Gakkou de tomodachi to tenisu o shimasu." english="I play tennis with friends at school." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="図書館[としょかん]で宿題[しゅくだい]をします。" romaji="Toshokan de shukudai o shimasu." english="I do homework at the library." />

<Hint>する here describes an activity actively unfolding, playing tennis, doing homework, and で always marks the stage that activity plays out on. This is the same で you already know from by-at-with, extended to mark any location where an action, not just a static state, takes place.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="今[いま]、どこにいますか。" romaji="Ima, doko ni imasu ka." english="Where are you right now?" />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="喫茶店[きっさてん]で何[なに]をしていますか。" romaji="Kissaten de nani o shite imasu ka." english="What are you doing at the coffee shop?" />

<Hint>いる and ある, existence verbs, almost always take に, どこにいますか, never どこでいますか, since existing somewhere is a state, not an action. Any verb describing an actual activity, however, takes で, even inside the exact same coffee shop.</Hint>
