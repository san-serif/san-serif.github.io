State a personal evaluation carefully, qualify a claim, and express a judgment with an appropriate degree of force. These patterns let an author state an evaluation while signaling its source or strength. Separate the observable facts from the writer’s judgment, then notice whether a phrase narrows the claim or makes it more forceful.

### Patterns in this lesson

- **如く・如き・如し** — Like, Similar to, As if, The same as
- **きらいがある** — Have a tendency to, Tend to, Be slightly~
- **かたわら** — While, Besides, In addition to
- **をいいことに** — To take advantage of
- **をおいてほかに〜ない** — No...but, Nothing else but, None other than
- **あっての** — Exist only because of or due to, Owe everything to
- **ながらに** — While being, Born (with), Traditional (old-fashioned, as it was long ago), As always, While crying or in tears, (Burned) alive
- **たなり・なり** — While... ~ing, Remaining, Not or ever since, Keep or leave
- **だの** — Things like, And so on, Or something, And whatnot
- **あくまでも** — To the (bitter) end, Stubbornly, Persistently
- **ところを** — When, While, In spite of (the circumstances), Regardless of (the circumstances)
- **にして②** — To be ~ and ~, To be ~ as well as ~, Both ~ and ~
- **いかなる** — What kind of, No matter what kind of, No … (with a negative verb), Any... (with a negative verb)

<JSentenceBlock furigana="この見方[みかた]には、一定[いってい]の偏[かたよ]りがある。" romaji="Kono mikata ni wa, ittei no katayori ga aru." english="This viewpoint has a certain bias." />

きらいがある points to a tendency, often an undesirable one, without claiming it happens every time. The pattern softens a direct accusation.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="文章[ぶんしょう]の文脈[ぶんみゃく]から、筆者[ひっしゃ]の意図[いと]を読[よ]み取[と]りましょう。" romaji="Bunshou no bunmyaku kara, hissha no ito o yomitorimashou." english="Use the context to identify the writer’s intention." />

When summarizing an editorial, label each sentence as evidence, interpretation, or evaluation. These forms often signal the last two.

<Hint type="insight">In a newspaper column, きらいがある can signal a tendency without accusing every person in a group. That cautious wording matters when translating criticism.</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">Track who holds the viewpoint and whether the writer is stating a fact or assessing it. Note the softer tendency marker separately from the more direct judgments.</Hint>
