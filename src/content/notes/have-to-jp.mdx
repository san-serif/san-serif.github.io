なければならない attaches to a verb or adjective's negative stem and expresses obligation, literally "if not, it won't do." It's built from two pieces you already have, ば (if) and ならない (won't become okay), stacked together into a single fixed phrase.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="このクラスでは5時[ごじ]までに宿題[しゅくだい]を出[だ]さなければなりません。" romaji="Kono kurasu de wa goji made ni shukudai o dasanakereba narimasen." english="In this class, homework has to be turned in by 5:00." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="漢字[かんじ]を300覚[おぼ]えなければなりません。" romaji="Kanji o sanbyaku oboenakereba narimasen." english="We have to memorize 300 kanji." />

<Hint>The literal shape is "verb-ば-negative + ならない," if you don't do it, it doesn't work out. In practice it just reads as a plain obligation, "have to," with none of that literal if-then logic surfacing in translation.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="これが売[う]れるためには、環境[かんきょう]に優[やさ]しくなければなりません。" romaji="Kore ga ureru tame ni wa, kankyou ni yasashiku nakereba narimasen." english="For this to sell, it has to be environmentally friendly." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="このレポートは日本語[にほんご]で書[か]かなければなりませんか。" romaji="Kono repooto wa nihongo de kakanakereba narimasen ka." english="Does this report have to be written in Japanese?" />

<Hint>い-adjectives swap い for くなければならない (優しくなければならない), and な-adjectives and nouns add でなければならない (便利でなければならない), following the same negative-stem pattern that なくて also builds on.</Hint>
