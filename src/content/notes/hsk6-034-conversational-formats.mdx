These are fixed conversational frames. Learn each as a whole: the repeated words and the sentence ending carry the attitude.

### From Not Knowing to Finding Out

不X不……，一X…… sets up a contrast: without X, the result does not happen; once X happens, something follows.

<CSentenceBlock simplified="不问不知道，一问吓一跳。" traditional="不問不知道，一問嚇一跳。" pinyin="Bù wèn bù zhīdào, yì wèn xià yí tiào." jyutping="bat1 man6 bat1 zi1 dou6, jat1 man6 haa6 jat1 tiu3." english="If you don’t ask, you won’t know; ask once and you may be surprised." />


### An Emphatic Address

好你个X calls someone out with strong feeling. Depending on tone, it can sound admiring, teasing, or annoyed.

<CSentenceBlock simplified="好你个小王，真有你的！" traditional="好你個小王，真有你的！" pinyin="Hǎo nǐ ge Xiǎo Wáng, zhēn yǒu nǐ de!" jyutping="hou2 nei5 go3 siu2 wong4, zan1 jau5 nei5 dik1!" english="Well, Xiao Wang, you really did it!" />


### Whatever You Say Goes

动词+什么就是什么 accepts the other person’s wording or decision, sometimes sincerely and sometimes with a hint of resignation.

<CSentenceBlock simplified="你说什么就是什么。" traditional="你說什麼就是什麼。" pinyin="Nǐ shuō shénme jiù shì shénme." jyutping="nei5 syut3 sam6 mo1 zau6 si6 sam6 mo1." english="Whatever you say goes." />

<Hint type="hint">Read the frame as a whole before translating each word. The repeated words are part of the pattern, not accidental repetition.</Hint>

<Hint type="insight">These forms are conversational: speakers use them to show annoyance, resignation, or emphasis. Tone of voice helps you tell playful teasing from real criticism.</Hint>
