ように attaches after a noun with の, or after a verb in its plain form, and means "like," "as," or "as if," drawing a resemblance between two things.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="沙耶[さや]さんは、先生[せんせい]のように上手[じょうず]に教[おし]えます。" romaji="Saya-san wa, sensei no you ni jouzu ni oshiemasu." english="Saya teaches skillfully, just like a teacher." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="拓也[たくや]さんは、毎日[まいにち]のようにサーフィンに行[い]きます。" romaji="Takuya-san wa, mainichi no you ni saafin ni ikimasu." english="Takuya goes surfing like it's every day." />

<Hint>先生のように, 毎日のように, both use のように directly on a noun, "like a teacher," "like it's every day," pulling in that noun as the standard being compared to. It doesn't need to be literal, 毎日のように often just means "almost every day," an exaggeration for emphasis.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="姫路城[ひめじじょう]は白[しろ]い鳥[とり]が休[やす]んでいるように見[み]えます。" romaji="Himeji-jou wa shiroi tori ga yasunde iru you ni miemasu." english="Himeji Castle looks like a white bird resting." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="由紀[ゆき]さんは困[こま]っているように見[み]えます。" romaji="Yuki-san wa komatte iru you ni miemasu." english="Yuki looks like she's troubled." />

<Hint>ように見える, "looks as if," pairs ように with 見える (to appear visually) for a vivid, image-based comparison, often used to describe how something looks from the outside, closely related to the visual そうだ but built with a fuller descriptive clause instead of just an adjective stem.</Hint>
