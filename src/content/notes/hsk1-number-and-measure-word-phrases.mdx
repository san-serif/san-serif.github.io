You’re setting the table: two books go in a stack, three cups of water on the tray. Chinese slips a measure word between each number and noun—两本书, 三杯水. The measure word gives the count its shape, and “two” is usually 两 before it; 二 appears in counting and numbers like 二十.

### Examples

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我有两本书。" traditional="我有兩本書。" pinyin="Wǒ yǒu liǎng běn shū." jyutping="ngo5 jau5 loeng5 bun2 syu1." english="I have two books." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="桌上有三杯水。" traditional="桌上有三杯水。" pinyin="Zhuō shàng yǒu sān bēi shuǐ." jyutping="zoek3 soeng6 jau5 saam1 bui1 seoi2." english="There are three cups of water on the table." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="这个房间有四个人。" traditional="這個房間有四個人。" pinyin="Zhège fángjiān yǒu sì ge rén." jyutping="ze5 go3 fong4 gaan1 jau5 sei3 go3 jan4." english="There are four people in this room." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我们家有五口人，桌上还放着两块蛋糕。" traditional="我們家有五口人，桌上還放著兩塊蛋糕。" pinyin="Wǒmen jiā yǒu wǔ kǒu rén, zhuō shàng hái fàngzhe liǎng kuài dàngāo." jyutping="ngo5 mun4 gaa1 jau5 ng5 hau2 jan4, zoek3 soeng6 waan4 fong3 zoek6 loeng5 faai3 daan6 gou1." english="There are five people in our family, and two pieces of cake on the table." />

<Hint type="hint">Learn a noun with its usual measure word: 本 for books, 杯 for drinks, 间 for rooms, 口 for family members, 块 for pieces, and 页 for pages. 个 is common, especially for people, but it does not replace every measure word.</Hint>
