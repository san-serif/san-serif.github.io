Describing the sky uses ordinary descriptive verbs, the same -아요/-어요 shape you already know. Rain and snow get their own construction: 비가 와요, 눈이 와요, literally "rain comes," "snow comes," built on 오다, the same verb you'd use for a person arriving.

<SentenceBlock formal="덥습니다." formalRom="deopseumnida." polite="더워요." politeRom="deowoyo." casual="더워." casualRom="deowo." english="It's hot." />

<SentenceBlock formal="춥습니다." formalRom="chupseumnida." polite="추워요." politeRom="chuwoyo." casual="추워." casualRom="chuwo." english="It's cold." />

<Hint>덥다 and 춥다 are ㅂ-irregular, the same family as 어렵다 (difficult) from [을/를 해요](/notes/what-are-you-doing). Almost every adjective describing intensity in this shape follows the pattern: swap the ㅂ for 우 before a vowel ending. 쉽다 (easy) becomes 쉬워요, 맵다 (spicy) becomes 매워요.</Hint>

### When It Rains

<SentenceBlock formal="내일 비가 옵니다." formalRom="naeil biga omnida." polite="내일 비가 와요." politeRom="naeil biga wayo." casual="내일 비가 와." casualRom="naeil biga wa." english="It will rain tomorrow." />

<SentenceBlock formal="눈이 옵니다." formalRom="nuni omnida." polite="눈이 와요." politeRom="nuni wayo." casual="눈이 와." casualRom="nuni wa." english="It's snowing." />

<SentenceBlock formal="바람이 많이 붑니다." formalRom="barami mani bumnida." polite="바람이 많이 불어요." politeRom="barami mani bureoyo." casual="바람이 많이 불어." casualRom="barami mani bureo." english="The wind blows strongly." />

Two full sentences chain together with -고, attached directly to the stem with no change: verb/adjective + 고 just means "and," letting you set up a contrast like summer against winter in one breath.

<SentenceBlock formal="여름은 덥고, 겨울은 춥습니다." formalRom="yeoreumeun deopgo, gyeoureun chupseumnida." polite="여름은 덥고, 겨울은 추워요." politeRom="yeoreumeun deopgo, gyeoureun chuwoyo." casual="여름은 덥고, 겨울은 추워." casualRom="yeoreumeun deopgo, gyeoureun chuwo." english="Summer is hot, winter is cold." />

<Hint>날씨 (weather) small talk is just as universal in Korean as it is in English. Opening with 오늘 날씨가 어때요? or commenting on the heat with a stranger in an elevator is completely normal, low-stakes conversation, not a sign anyone's genuinely curious about meteorology.</Hint>
