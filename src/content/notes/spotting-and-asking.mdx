が marks something you're noticing or pointing out right now, new information entering the conversation. は sets up a topic and asks or states something about it. Both particles show up constantly side by side, and picking the right one changes what the sentence is actually doing.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="あ、バスが来[き]ましたよ。乗[の]りましょう。" romaji="A, basu ga kimashita yo. Norimashou." english="Oh, the bus is coming! Let's get on." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="あ、桜[さくら]が咲[さ]いていますよ。見[み]に行[い]きましょう。" romaji="A, sakura ga saiteimasu yo. Mi ni ikimashou." english="Oh, the cherry blossoms are blooming! Let's go see them." />

<Hint>が here marks something just noticed, a bus pulling up, flowers suddenly in view. This is the same logic behind Old News, New News in Korean: が announces something new, while は would sound strange here, as if the bus or the blossoms were already the agreed topic of conversation.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="食堂[しょくどう]はどこですか。" romaji="Shokudou wa doko desu ka." english="Where is the cafeteria?" />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="お風呂[ふろ]は何階[なんかい]ですか。" romaji="Ofuro wa nankai desu ka." english="What floor is the bathroom on?" />

<Hint>は sets the topic first, the cafeteria, the bathroom, then asks or says something about it. These questions aren't spotting something new, they're asking for a detail about a thing already under discussion, which is exactly the job は does.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="これは妹[いもうと]が作[つく]ったケーキです。" romaji="Kore wa imouto ga tsukutta keeki desu." english="This is the cake my sister made." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="兄[あに]が飼[か]っている犬[いぬ]の写真[しゃしん]です。" romaji="Ani ga katteiru inu no shashin desu." english="It's a photo of the dog my older brother keeps." />

<Hint>Inside a clause that modifies a noun, the subject of that inner clause always takes が, never は, even when the whole sentence's real topic is marked with は elsewhere. これは [妹が作った] ケーキです nests a full が-marked clause, "my sister made it," inside the bigger は-marked sentence, "this is a cake." This is the same が you'll meet again in Relative Clauses.</Hint>
