You’re describing a friend’s new coat, but the color is what you want to comment on. Chinese can put the person or thing first, then give a comment whose own subject names the feature you mean: “As for this coat, the color is lovely.”

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我弟弟个子很高。" traditional="我弟弟個子很高。" pinyin="wǒ dìdi gèzi hěn gāo." jyutping="ngo5 dai6 dai2 go3 zi2 han2 gou1." english="My younger brother is tall." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="这件衣服颜色很好看。" traditional="這件衣服顏色很好看。" pinyin="zhè jiàn yīfu yánsè hěn hǎokàn." jyutping="ze5 gin6 ji1 fuk6 ngaan4 sik1 han2 hou2 hon3." english="This coat has a lovely color." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我妹妹汉语说得很流利。" traditional="我妹妹漢語說得很流利。" pinyin="wǒ mèimei Hànyǔ shuō de hěn liúlì." jyutping="ngo5 mui6 mui6 hon3 jyu5 syut3 dak1 han2 lau4 lei6." english="My younger sister speaks Chinese fluently." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我家离学校很近。" traditional="我家離學校很近。" pinyin="wǒ jiā lí xuéxiào hěn jìn." jyutping="ngo5 gaa1 lei4 hok6 haau6 han2 gan6." english="My home is close to the school." />

<Hint type="hint">The opening phrase tells your listener what you’re talking about. The rest of the sentence comments on it, often with a second noun phrase: 我弟弟 / 个子很高 (“my brother / is tall”).</Hint>
