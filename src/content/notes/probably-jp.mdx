だろう and でしょう attach to the plain form and turn a flat statement into an educated guess, "probably." They're the same word at two politeness levels, だろう plain, でしょう polite, and both often pair with たぶん to make the guess explicit.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="たぶん月曜日[げつようび]でしょう。" romaji="Tabun getsuyoubi deshou." english="It's probably Monday." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="ええ、たぶん難[むずか]しいでしょう。" romaji="Ee, tabun muzukashii deshou." english="Yeah, it's probably difficult." />

<Hint>だろう and でしょう attach directly to the plain form with no extra connector, verbs and い-adjectives straight on, and な-adjectives or nouns straight on too,元気でしょう, 先生でしょう, no だ needed in between.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="今日[きょう]学校[がっこう]に来[こ]なかったでしょう？" romaji="Kyou gakkou ni konakatta deshou?" english="You didn't come to school today, did you?" />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="リンさんはどうしたんでしょうか。" romaji="Rin-san wa doushitan deshou ka." english="I wonder what happened to Lin." />

<Hint>Rising intonation turns でしょう into a soft confirmation check, closer to "right?" than to a genuine guess. でしょうか does something different again, wondering aloud rather than asking the listener directly, gentler than a plain question.</Hint>
