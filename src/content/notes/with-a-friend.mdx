하고 does two jobs. Stuck between two nouns, it means "and": 빵하고 과자 is "bread and snacks." Stuck after a person, it means "with": 친구하고 is "with a friend." Same particle, and context always makes clear which job it's doing.

<SentenceBlock formal="친구하고 놉니다." formalRom="chinguhago nomnida." polite="친구하고 놀아요." politeRom="chinguhago norayo." casual="친구하고 놀아." casualRom="chinguhago nora." english="I play with a friend." />

<Hint>놀다 covers adults hanging out just as much as kids playing. 친구하고 놀아요 is a completely normal thing for a grown adult to say about getting a drink with friends. English "play" sounds childish in that context; Korean 놀다 doesn't.</Hint>

These pair naturally with 에서, the location-of-action particle from Where Is It?, since where you do something and who you're with usually show up in the same sentence.

<SentenceBlock formal="집에서 드라마를 봅니다." formalRom="jibeseo deuramareul bomnida." polite="집에서 드라마를 봐요." politeRom="jibeseo deuramareul bwayo." casual="집에서 드라마를 봐." casualRom="jibeseo deuramareul bwa." english="I watch dramas at home." />

<SentenceBlock formal="공원에서 자전거를 탑니다." formalRom="gongwoneseo jajeongeoreul tamnida." polite="공원에서 자전거를 타요." politeRom="gongwoneseo jajeongeoreul tayo." casual="공원에서 자전거를 타." casualRom="gongwoneseo jajeongeoreul ta." english="I ride a bike at the park." />

<SentenceBlock formal="도서관에서 책을 읽습니다." formalRom="doseogwaneseo chaegeul ikseumnida." polite="도서관에서 책을 읽어요." politeRom="doseogwaneseo chaegeul ilgeoyo." casual="도서관에서 책을 읽어." casualRom="doseogwaneseo chaegeul ilgeo." english="I read a book at the library." />

<SentenceBlock formal="백화점에서 쇼핑합니다." formalRom="baekhwajeomeseo syopinghamnida." polite="백화점에서 쇼핑해요." politeRom="baekhwajeomeseo syopinghaeyo." casual="백화점에서 쇼핑해." casualRom="baekhwajeomeseo syopinghae." english="I shop at the department store." />

<SentenceBlock formal="빵하고 과자를 먹습니다." formalRom="ppanghago gwajareul meokseumnida." polite="빵하고 과자를 먹어요." politeRom="ppanghago gwajareul meogeoyo." casual="빵하고 과자를 먹어." casualRom="ppanghago gwajareul meogeo." english="I eat bread and snacks." />

<SentenceBlock formal="친구하고 카페에서 커피를 마십니다." formalRom="chinguhago kapeeseo keopireul masimnida." polite="친구하고 카페에서 커피를 마셔요." politeRom="chinguhago kapeeseo keopireul masyeoyo." casual="친구하고 카페에서 커피를 마셔." casualRom="chinguhago kapeeseo keopireul masyeo." english="I drink coffee at a cafe with a friend." />

<Hint>하고 is the spoken choice. Open a book, an essay, or anything formally written, and 와/과 shows up doing the same job instead. You'll understand it fine by context, but 하고 is what to reach for when you're actually talking.</Hint>
