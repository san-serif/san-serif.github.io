多い (many) and 少ない (few) are ordinary い-adjectives describing a count, always attaching to が, never directly modifying the noun in front like English "many books" does.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="新[あたら]しい会社[かいしゃ]は仕事[しごと]が多[おお]いです。" romaji="Atarashii kaisha wa shigoto ga ooi desu." english="The new company has a lot of work." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="新[あたら]しい会社[かいしゃ]は休[やす]みが少[すく]ないです。" romaji="Atarashii kaisha wa yasumi ga sukunai desu." english="The new company has few days off." />

<Hint>多い never sits directly in front of a noun the way English "many books" does, 多い本 is wrong. Say 本が多い (books are many) as its own clause, or use たくさんの本 (many books) instead if you need it inside a noun phrase.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="この町[まち]は美味[おい]しいレストランが多[おお]くていいですよ。" romaji="Kono machi wa oishii resutoran ga ookute ii desu yo." english="This town has a lot of good restaurants, which is nice." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="公園[こうえん]が多[おお]い町[まち]に住[す]みたいです。" romaji="Kouen ga ooi machi ni sumitai desu." english="I want to live in a town with a lot of parks." />

<Hint>多くて is just 多い's て-form, the same chaining trick every い-adjective uses to link two descriptions into one sentence: レストランが多くていい literally strings together "restaurants are many" and "good" into "has a lot of restaurants, which is nice." 多い does turn directly into 多い町 (a town with a lot of X) when it's modifying the noun as a full clause rather than standing alone, that's different from trying to jam it in front of the counted noun itself.</Hint>
