You are texting a close friend about how life has changed since moving. Casual endings bring the conversation closer; 에는 and 에서는 put a particular time or place in contrast with another.

### Drop 요 With Someone Close

The everyday casual style often uses the same -아/어 form as polite speech without 요: 먹어요 → 먹어, 바빠요 → 바빠. Past and future follow familiar shapes too: 먹었어, 갈 거야. Nouns need 이야 after a consonant and 야 after a vowel.

<SentenceBlock polite="요즘 한국 생활이 어때요?" politeRom="yojeum hanguk saenghwari eottaeyo?" casual="요즘 한국 생활이 어때?" casualRom="yojeum hanguk saenghwari eottae?" english="How is life in Korea these days?" />

<SentenceBlock polite="처음에는 힘들었어요." politeRom="cheoeumeneun himdeureosseoyo." casual="처음에는 힘들었어." casualRom="cheoeumeneun himdeureosseo." english="It was hard at first." />

### Contrast a Time or Place

Add 는 to 에 or 에서: 처음에는, 지금은, 학교에서는. 에는 often sets a time or location as the topic; 에서는 sets the place where an action happens as the topic. Keep the original particle’s job when adding 는.

<SentenceBlock polite="집에서는 한국어로 이야기해요." politeRom="jibeseoneun hangugeoro iyagihaeyo." casual="집에서는 한국어로 이야기해." casualRom="jibeseoneun hangugeoro iyagihae." english="At home, we speak Korean." />

<SentenceBlock polite="주말에는 시간이 좀 있어요." politeRom="jumareneun sigani jom isseoyo." casual="주말에는 시간이 좀 있어." casualRom="jumareneun sigani jom isseo." english="On weekends, I have a little time." />

<Hint type="hint">Casual speech depends on the relationship and agreement between speakers. Removing 요 from a sentence does not make it appropriate with a teacher or a person you have just met. This page keeps the polite version available for comparison.</Hint>
