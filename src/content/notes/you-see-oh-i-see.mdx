A friend wonders why you skipped lunch. You explain that you already ate. They respond with a small realisation. -거든요 supplies background they may be missing; -구나 marks something becoming clear.

### Give the Missing Explanation

Attach -거든요 to a verb or adjective stem, or -(이)거든요 to a noun. Past forms are possible: 먹었거든요. At the end of a turn it often explains a previous remark or answers an implied why-question.

<SentenceBlock polite="저는 조금 이따 먹을게요. 아까 간식을 먹었거든요." politeRom="jeoneun jogeum itta meogeulgeyo. akka gansigeul meogeotgeodeunyo." casual="나는 조금 이따 먹을게. 아까 간식을 먹었거든." casualRom="naneun jogeum itta meogeulge. akka gansigeul meogeotgeodeun." english="I’ll eat a little later. I had a snack earlier, you see." />

<SentenceBlock polite="내일은 못 가요. 시험이 있거든요." politeRom="naeireun mot gayo. siheomi itgeodeunyo." casual="내일은 못 가. 시험이 있거든." casualRom="naeireun mot ga. siheomi itgeodeun." english="I can’t go tomorrow. I have an exam, you see." />

### React With a Realisation

Present action verbs take -는구나: 잘하는구나, 먹는구나. Adjectives take -구나: 맵구나, 좋구나. Nouns take -(이)구나. Past forms use -았/었구나. The polite counterpart is -(는)군요, though it can sound more reflective or formal than -네요.

<SentenceBlock polite="요리를 정말 잘하는군요." politeRom="yorireul jeongmal jalhaneungunyo." casual="요리를 정말 잘하는구나." casualRom="yorireul jeongmal jalhaneunguna." english="Oh, you’re really good at cooking." />

<SentenceBlock polite="생각보다 맵군요." politeRom="saenggakboda maepgunyo." casual="생각보다 맵구나." casualRom="saenggakboda maepguna." english="Oh, it’s spicier than I expected." />

<Hint type="hint">Compare [-잖아요](/notes/you-know-i-was-going-to): it appeals to something the listener can recognise or be reminded of. -거든요 often fills in a reason they did not know. Repeated explanations with a sharp tone can sound defensive.</Hint>
