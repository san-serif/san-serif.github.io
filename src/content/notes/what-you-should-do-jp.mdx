Your friend is stuck: the form is confusing, the deadline is close, and they ask what to do. ばいい offers a workable next step; べき and ことだ can make the advice firmer.

The N5 lesson on ほうがいい gives a common, personal recommendation (“you'd better…”). These N3 forms let you choose a different angle: ばいい points to a workable solution, べき frames an action as an obligation or standard, and ことだ gives direct advice about what to do.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="分[わ]からなかったら、先生[せんせい]に聞[き]けばいいです。" romaji="Wakaranakattara, sensei ni kikeba ii desu." english="If you don't understand, you can ask the teacher." />

ばいい attaches to the conditional form and suggests what would solve the problem: “if you do X, that would be good.” It often feels lighter and more practical than べき, especially in a direct reply to someone asking what to do.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="大事[だいじ]な約束[やくそく]は守[まも]るべきです。" romaji="Daiji na yakusoku wa mamoru beki desu." english="You should keep important promises." />

べき follows a verb's dictionary form and presents an action as something one ought to do, based on a rule, responsibility, or strong principle. Its negative is べきではない: the action is something one ought not to do. べき can sound forceful when directed at another person, so it is often safer for general principles than casual advice.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="人[ひと]を外見[がいけん]だけで判断[はんだん]するべきではありません。" romaji="Hito o gaiken dake de handan suru beki de wa arimasen." english="You shouldn't judge people by appearance alone." />

ことだ follows a dictionary or negative verb and gives straightforward advice about the action to take: “the thing to do is…” It can sound authoritative, as if the speaker is laying out a course of action rather than offering a casual suggestion.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="疲[つか]れているなら、今日[きょう]は早[はや]く寝[ね]ることです。" romaji="Tsukarete iru nara, kyou wa hayaku neru koto desu." english="If you're tired, the thing to do is go to bed early tonight." />

<Hint type="insight">In real life, べき often appears in public statements, essays, and debates about responsibility (“what a company should do”). Between friends, it can sound like a reprimand. For a softer personal suggestion, ほうがいい or ばいい is usually more natural.</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">A quick scale: ばいい = a practical option; ほうがいい = a personal recommendation; ことだ = direct guidance; べき = a principle or obligation. べきではない is similarly strong, while ないほうがいい (from the N5 lesson) is more like advice not to do something.</Hint>

たらいい and といい are softer ways to offer advice or express a hope. たらいい often points to a practical next step: 受付で聞いたらいいですよ, “you could ask at reception.” といい is common for wishes about a future outcome: 早く元気になるといいですね, “I hope you feel better soon.” They sound less like a rule than べき.

に越したことはない says that something is best or safest, while recognizing that it may not be essential: 早めに予約するに越したことはありません, “it's best to book early.” It is less forceful than べき and often presents a prudent recommendation.
