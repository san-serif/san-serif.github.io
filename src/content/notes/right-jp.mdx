ね tacks onto the end of a sentence to invite agreement, close to English "isn't it?" or "right?" It works on nearly anything, statements, observations, even single adjectives standing alone.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="暑[あつ]いですね。" romaji="Atsui desu ne." english="It's hot, isn't it." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="あの犬[いぬ]は可愛[かわい]いですね。" romaji="Ano inu wa kawaii desu ne." english="That dog is cute, isn't it." />

<Hint>ね on a shared observation isn't really a question, it's small talk glue, commenting on something you both can already see and inviting a quick nod back. This is the exact same move Korean's -지요/죠 makes, keeping a conversation moving rather than genuinely asking for new information.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="10時[じゅうじ]からですね。分[わ]かりました。" romaji="Juuji kara desu ne. Wakarimashita." english="From 10, right? Got it." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="毎日[まいにち]残業[ざんぎょう]していますね。大丈夫[だいじょうぶ]ですか。" romaji="Mainichi zangyou shiteimasu ne. Daijoubu desu ka." english="You've been working overtime every day, huh. Are you okay?" />

<Hint>ね also checks a specific detail before moving on, 10時からですね confirms the time was heard correctly, closer to "that's 10, right?" than genuine small talk. The same particle covers both jobs, warm agreement and quick confirmation, context and tone are what tell them apart.</Hint>
