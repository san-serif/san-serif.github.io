おかげで, せいで, and ために all connect a cause to a result, but they reveal how the speaker sees that cause. おかげで credits something positive; せいで blames or points to an unwanted cause; ために is more neutral and often sounds formal or factual. The older から and ので lessons explain reasons, while this group adds the speaker's attitude toward the cause.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="友達[ともだち]が手伝[てつだ]ってくれたおかげで、引[ひ]っ越[こ]しが早[はや]く終[お]わりました。" romaji="Tomodachi ga tetsudatte kureta okage de, hikkoshi ga hayaku owarimashita." english="Thanks to my friend helping, the move finished quickly." />

おかげで frames the cause as something the speaker is grateful for. It can follow a plain clause, or a noun with の: 先生のおかげで, “thanks to the teacher.” It is common for thanking someone directly or acknowledging help that led to a good result.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="寝不足[ねぶそく]のせいで、会議中[かいぎちゅう]に眠[ねむ]くなりました。" romaji="Nebusoku no sei de, kaigichuu ni nemuku narimashita." english="Because I was short on sleep, I got sleepy during the meeting." />

せいで marks a cause the speaker sees as responsible for a bad or inconvenient outcome. With a noun, use のせいで; with a plain clause, attach せいで directly. It can sound accusatory when the cause is another person, so use it carefully when speaking to them.

<Hint type="insight">In real life, おかげで is a natural way to give credit in a thank-you: 皆さんのおかげで、無事に終わりました, “Thanks to all of you, it went smoothly.” It can also be used sarcastically for a bad result: 君のおかげで遅刻した, “Thanks to you, I was late.” せいで directly presents a cause as unwelcome and is more likely to sound like blame.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="大雪[おおゆき]のために、電車[でんしゃ]が遅[おく]れています。" romaji="Ooyuki no tame ni, densha ga okurete imasu." english="The trains are delayed because of heavy snow." />

ために presents a cause more neutrally, often in careful explanations, notices, or reports. With a noun, use のために; a plain clause can also come directly before ために. The same pattern can express purpose (“in order to”), as in the existing lesson on ために, so use the surrounding result to tell the two readings apart.

だけに means “precisely because” and often makes the result feel especially expected or all the more striking: 長年使ってきただけに、愛着があります, “because I've used it for years, I'm especially attached to it.” 結果 / の結果 states an outcome: 雨の結果、試合が中止になった, “as a result of the rain, the game was canceled.” の結果 is more formal and follows a noun or process; it does not itself say whether the cause was welcome or blameworthy.

からこそ emphasizes a reason as the decisive one: 努力したからこそ、目標を達成できた, “it was precisely because I worked hard that I reached my goal.” そのために can mean “for that reason” or “for that purpose,” depending on whether it refers back to a cause or forward to an aim. Casual だって can introduce a reason in conversation (“because…”), often sounding explanatory or defensive.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="一度[いちど]うそをついたばかりに、誰[だれ]も信[しん]じてくれなくなりました。" romaji="Ichido uso o tsuita bakari ni, dare mo shinjite kurenaku narimashita." english="Just because I lied once, nobody trusts me anymore." />

ばかりに emphasizes that one particular cause led to an especially regrettable result: “just because of X, this bad thing happened.” It often carries hindsight or frustration, rather than the more neutral explanation given by ために.

<Hint type="hint">A quick choice: おかげで normally gives credit (but can be sarcastic), せいで assigns an unwanted cause, ばかりに highlights a regrettable consequence of one cause, and ために gives a relatively neutral explanation. A good result after せいで is unusual and will sound ironic or deliberately contradictory.</Hint>

After a long or repeated process, あげく describes the eventual result, often one that is unwanted or disappointing. It follows a verb in the past form or a noun + の: 何度も話し合ったあげく, “after discussing it many times.”

<JSentenceBlock furigana="何度[なんど]も話[はな]し合[あ]ったあげく、計画[けいかく]を中止[ちゅうし]することになりました。" romaji="Nando mo hanashiatta ageku, keikaku o chuushi suru koto ni narimashita." english="After discussing it many times, we ended up canceling the plan." />

<Hint type="insight">あげく adds the sense of a drawn-out sequence leading to a final result. If you only want to state a straightforward cause, ために is more neutral and doesn't imply all the effort that came first.</Hint>
