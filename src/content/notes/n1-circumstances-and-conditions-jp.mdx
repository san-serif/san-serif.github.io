Describe the circumstances surrounding an event, then show how a condition or situation shapes what follows. The expressions here describe how circumstances shape an event: some state an essential premise, while others limit or frame what is possible. Identify the condition first, then read the main clause as its result.

### Patterns in this lesson

- **れる・られる + ままに** — Do as (one is told or ordered…), In accordance with, In compliance with, Just like
- **にまつわる** — Surrounding, Related or relating to, Associated with, Connected to
- **たる** — To be (in a position of), As a, For
- **をものともせず** — Making nothing of, Ignoring, In defiance of, Regardless of, Disregarding
- **ものと思う** — Think that, Believe that, Expect that, Have confidence that, Absolutely
- **ゆえに** — Because, The reason is, Due to, Therefore, So
- **という** — Every single, Each and every, Emphasis, Everything that could be called
- **にとどまらず** — Not limited to, Not only...but also, Not stopping at
- **か否か** — Whether or not
- **たら〜で** — Even if, If
- **べくして** — As expected, Meant to, Supposed to, Destined to, Sure to
- **まま(に)** — As (one likes, feels or pleases), Spur of the moment, Wherever one fancies, Spontaneously, On a whim
- **〜に〜ない** — Cannot even...if wants, Would like to...but cannot, Wants to...but cannot

<JSentenceBlock furigana="地域[ちいき]の協力[きょうりょく]あっての計画[けいかく]です。" romaji="Chiiki no kyouryoku atte no keikaku desu." english="This plan exists because of the community’s cooperation." />

あっての says the second thing depends on the first for its existence or success. It expresses a stronger connection than simply saying the first thing helped.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="文章[ぶんしょう]の文脈[ぶんみゃく]から、筆者[ひっしゃ]の意図[いと]を読[よ]み取[と]りましょう。" romaji="Bunshou no bunmyaku kara, hissha no ito o yomitorimashou." english="Use the context to identify the writer’s intention." />

When reading a policy statement, underline the premise and circle the action or result that depends on it.

<Hint type="insight">In project planning, あっての can credit the people or resources a success depends on. It is a natural way to acknowledge indispensable support in a formal thank-you.</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">Underline the prerequisite first. Ask whether the following clause could still happen without it; あっての usually says it could not.</Hint>
