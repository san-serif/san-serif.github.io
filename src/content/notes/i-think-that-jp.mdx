と思う attaches after a whole plain-form clause and means "I think that." と here works like a quoting particle, marking everything before it as the content of the thought, with 思う simply naming it as an opinion.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="いい大学[だいがく]だと思[おも]います。" romaji="Ii daigaku da to omoimasu." english="I think it's a good university." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="先生[せんせい]は厳[きび]しくないと思[おも]います。" romaji="Sensei wa kibishikunai to omoimasu." english="I don't think the teacher is strict." />

<Hint>The whole clause before と stays in its plain form, even in a polite sentence, いい大学だ, not いい大学です, since と思います is what carries the politeness at the very end. Negating the opinion itself, thinking the teacher isn't strict, reads more naturally in Japanese than negating 思う, saying "I don't think," which sounds unnatural here.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="町[まち]の人[ひと]は外国人[がいこくじん]に優[やさ]しいと思[おも]います。" romaji="Machi no hito wa gaikokujin ni yasashii to omoimasu." english="I think the people in this town are kind to foreigners." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="夏休[なつやす]みに寮[りょう]を出[で]なくてはいけないと思[おも]います。" romaji="Natsuyasumi ni ryou o denakute wa ikenai to omoimasu." english="I think we have to move out of the dorm for summer break." />

<Hint>と思います stacks cleanly on top of grammar you already know, なければならない, てはいけない, かもしれない, all sitting comfortably right before と思います to soften a claim into a personal opinion rather than a flat statement of fact.</Hint>
