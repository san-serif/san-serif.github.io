One rule may apply only on weekdays, while another applies as long as a condition holds. に限って narrows the scope; 限り marks the boundary of what remains true.

に限って singles out a particular time, person, or situation as an exception. It often carries frustration or surprise: “just this time...” / “of all people...” 限り has several related uses: “as long as” a condition holds, “as far as” the speaker knows, or “within the limits of” a stated range. せめて means “at least,” setting a minimum hope or request when more is not possible. The clause before 限り tells which kind of boundary is meant.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="彼[かれ]は普段[ふだん]遅[おく]れないのに、今日[きょう]に限[かぎ]って電車[でんしゃ]に乗[の]り遅[おく]れました。" romaji="Kare wa fudan okurenai noni, kyou ni kagitte densha ni noriokuremashita." english="He is usually never late, but of all days, he missed the train today." />

Time + に限って highlights an unusual exception. Person + に限って can express surprise that someone in particular would do something, often because it does not fit their usual character.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="必要[ひつよう]な限[かぎ]り、いつでも相談[そうだん]してください。" romaji="Hitsuyou na kagiri, itsu demo soudan shite kudasai." english="Please consult with me whenever necessary." />

Verb dictionary form + 限り means “as long as” the action or condition continues. Plain form + 限りでは means “as far as” the speaker knows or can tell: 私が知る限りでは, “as far as I know.”

次第で means “depending on,” making the outcome vary with a condition: 選び方次第で結果が変わる, “the result changes depending on how you choose.” 次第だ can also summarize what led to a result.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="予算[よさん]の限[かぎ]りで、できるだけの準備[じゅんび]をしました。" romaji="Yosan no kagiri de, dekiru dake no junbi o shimashita." english="Within the budget, we made as much preparation as possible." />

Noun + の限りで marks a limit or scope. It often appears in formal explanations about resources, authority, or the information available.

<Hint type="insight">今日に限って can sound like the speaker is exasperated because a rare problem happened at the worst time. 知る限りでは is a useful hedge in a report or conversation because it clearly limits the claim to available knowledge.</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">A surprising exception: Xに限って. “As long as” a condition holds: Vる限り. “As far as I know”: 知る限りでは. Within a stated boundary: Nの限りで.</Hint>
