Like こと, which you already know, の can also nominalize a whole clause, turning it into a noun. の is the natural choice with preference verbs (好き, 上手, 下手) and, especially, with verbs of direct perception, seeing or hearing something happen right in front of you.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="拓也[たくや]さんはスポーツを見[み]るのが大好[だいす]きです。" romaji="Takuya-san wa supootsu o miru no ga daisuki desu." english="Takuya loves watching sports." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="拓也[たくや]さんは寝[ね]るのが早[はや]いです。" romaji="Takuya-san wa neru no ga hayai desu." english="Takuya goes to bed early." />

<Hint>好き, 嫌い, 上手, 下手, 早い, 遅い, all naturally pair with の rather than こと, an idiomatic preference that's easiest to just memorize by pattern. こと would technically be understandable in most of these but sounds noticeably less natural.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="男[おとこ]の人[ひと]がいるのを見[み]ました。" romaji="Otoko no hito ga iru no o mimashita." english="I saw that there was a man there." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="ケータイで話[はな]しているのが聞[き]こえました。" romaji="Keetai de hanashite iru no ga kikoemashita." english="I could hear someone talking on their phone." />

<Hint>見る, 聞く, 聞こえる, 見える always take の, never こと, when the object is something you directly witnessed happening, a live event caught by your own eyes or ears, rather than an abstract fact you simply know about.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="今日[きょう]から新[あたら]しい生活[せいかつ]なのは、嬉[うれ]しいです。" romaji="Kyou kara atarashii seikatsu na no wa, ureshii desu." english="Starting a new life today makes me happy." />

<Hint>こと usually fits better when the nominalized clause is abstract, a fact, an idea, a decision, ことができる, ことになる, ことにする, all patterns you've already learned. の fits better when it's something concrete, immediate, or personally felt, closer to watching a scene unfold than stating an abstract truth.</Hint>
