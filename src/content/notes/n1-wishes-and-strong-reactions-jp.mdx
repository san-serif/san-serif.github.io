Express restrained wishes, strong feelings, and reactions that are difficult to put into ordinary words. This set includes literary ways to express wishes and strong reactions. A wish may be presented as continuing; another pattern may describe an emotional response that the speaker cannot suppress.

### Patterns in this lesson

- **い-Adj[く] + もなんともない** — Not... or anything, Not at all, Not, By no means, Definitely not, Nothing like that
- **に至っても** — Even when
- **Verb + だに** — Just, Even just, Even merely
- **ないでもない** — Kind of, Might, Slightly, It’s not (impossible) that, Somewhat, In some respects
- **もさることながら** — It is a fact that, But, Even more than, It goes without saying, Not only but also,
- **ものと思っていた** — Was under the impression that, (Always) the thought used to think
- **でなくてなんだろう** — If … is not … then what is?
- **はさておき・はさておいて** — (Leaving or putting) aside, Apart
- **折には** — When, Occasion, Opportunity, Time
- **を兼ねて** — And also, Also (partly) to, Also (partly) for (the purpose of), At the same time, To double as, When … also, While … as well, And as
- **Verb[ない]もの(だろう)か** — (Why) can’t, Isn’t there, Wish that, If only
- **Verb[て] + みせる** — I will definitely, I will certainly, Just watch, Whatever it takes, I swear, for sure
- **相まって** — Combined with, Coupled with, Together with

<JSentenceBlock furigana="地域[ちいき]の復興[ふっこう]を願[ねが]ってやみません。" romaji="Chiiki no fukkou o negatte yamimasen." english="I sincerely hope for the community’s recovery." />

てやまない emphasizes a continuing, heartfelt wish. It is common in speeches and formal messages, rather than quick everyday exchanges.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="文章[ぶんしょう]の文脈[ぶんみゃく]から、筆者[ひっしゃ]の意図[いと]を読[よ]み取[と]りましょう。" romaji="Bunshou no bunmyaku kara, hissha no ito o yomitorimashou." english="Use the context to identify the writer’s intention." />

In a real speech, this kind of expression signals emotional commitment. Translate the intensity, but avoid adding a stronger claim than the speaker made.

<Hint type="insight">A graduation speech or public message may use 願ってやまない to express a continuing heartfelt wish. This sounds more ceremonious than a casual 願っている.</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">For expressive forms, identify the emotion and its duration, then choose an English intensity that fits the surrounding speech.</Hint>
