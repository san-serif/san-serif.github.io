は～くらいです gives a modest estimate or approximate example: “as little as / only about…” The context determines whether the amount feels small, large, or sufficient.

さえ, こそ, くらい, ことか, and ことに add emphasis in different ways. さえ means “even,” often identifying the minimum needed for something to be true. こそ puts a spotlight on the marked word: “this is exactly the one.” くらい can describe an extreme degree (“so much that…”). ことか expresses a strong feeling, often in rhetorical exclamation. ことに highlights the speaker's evaluation of a situation, as in 驚いたことに, “surprisingly.”

<JSentenceBlock furigana="名前[なまえ]さえ分[わ]かれば、受付[うけつけ]で確認[かくにん]できます。" romaji="Namae sae wakareba, uketsuke de kakunin dekimasu." english="If you know even the name, you can check at reception." />

さえ often marks a minimum condition or an unexpected example. こそ marks the focus more strongly: 今こそ始めよう, “now is exactly the time to begin.” It does not always imply that other options are impossible; it emphasizes the chosen one.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="彼[かれ]が来[き]てくれて、どれほど心強[こころづよ]かったことか。" romaji="Kare ga kite kurete, dore hodo kokorozuyokatta koto ka." english="How reassuring it was that he came." />

くらい can mark the extent of a result: 立てないくらい疲れた, “I was so tired I couldn't stand.” ことか turns a feeling into an emphatic rhetorical exclamation. ことに commonly follows an adjective in its adverbial form to frame an evaluation: 不思議なことに, “strangely.”

<Hint type="insight">さえ can make a task seem easy by naming the smallest requirement: 電源を入れさえすれば使える, “you only need to turn on the power.” こそ instead gives strong focus to the reason or person that matters most: あなたこそ必要です, “you are exactly who we need.”</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">Unexpected “even” or minimum condition: さえ. Strong focus: こそ. To the extent that: くらい. “How very…”: ことか. Evaluation lead-in: ことに.</Hint>
