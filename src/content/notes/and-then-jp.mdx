それから means "and then," picking up right where the previous action or item left off. It pairs naturally with a て-form clause to sequence two actions, and works just as well starting a new sentence.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="メールをチェックして、それから仕事[しごと]をします。" romaji="Meeru o chekku shite, sorekara shigoto o shimasu." english="I'll check my email, and then do work." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="11時半[じゅういちじはん]に大阪[おおさか]に着[つ]いて、それから昼[ひる]ご飯[はん]を食[た]べました。" romaji="Juuichiji han ni Oosaka ni tsuite, sorekara hirugohan o tabemashita." english="I arrived in Osaka at 11:30, and then ate lunch." />

<Hint>それから almost always follows a て-form clause, the same connector from How's the Weather? and And Then, listing two actions with no built-in relationship beyond order. それから just makes that order explicit: this happened, then that happened next.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="コーヒーと紅茶[こうちゃ]、それからジュースがあります。" romaji="Koohii to koucha, sorekara juusu ga arimasu." english="There's coffee and tea, and also juice." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="ラーメンと寿司[すし]、それから焼[や]き鳥[とり]も食[た]べました。" romaji="Raamen to sushi, sorekara yakitori mo tabemashita." english="I ate ramen and sushi, and also yakitori." />

<Hint>それから works on plain noun lists too, not just actions, tacking one more item onto items already joined by と. It reads like an afterthought that isn't really an afterthought, the speaker planned to mention it, just saved it for last.</Hint>
