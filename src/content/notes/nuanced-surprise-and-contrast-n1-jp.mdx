These forms manage expectations and contrast. Sentence-final とは can express surprise or disbelief. と思いきや introduces an outcome contrary to what someone expected. にひきかえ draws a sharp contrast between two people or things. ものを often expresses regret or dissatisfaction about an unrealized alternative, while とは言うものの acknowledges a statement and then qualifies it. じゃあるまいし makes a pointed comparison: “you're not X, so...”

<JSentenceBlock furigana="彼[かれ]が優勝[ゆうしょう]するとは、誰[だれ]も予想[よそう]していませんでした。" romaji="Kare ga yuushou suru to wa, dare mo yosou shite imasen deshita." english="No one expected that he would win." />

Sentence-final とは marks a fact as surprising or hard to believe. The speaker's reaction is stronger than a neutral report with と聞いた or と知った.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="静[しず]かな人[ひと]だと思[おも]いきや、舞台[ぶたい]では堂々[どうどう]と話[はな]しました。" romaji="Shizuka na hito da to omoikiya, butai de wa doudou to hanashimashita." english="I thought they were quiet, but on stage they spoke confidently." />

と思いきや marks an unexpected reversal after an assumption. にひきかえ explicitly compares two different cases: 兄は慎重なのにひきかえ、弟は大胆だ, “unlike the older brother, the younger one is bold.”

<JSentenceBlock furigana="一言[ひとこと]相談[そうだん]してくれればよかったものを。" romaji="Hitokoto soudan shite kurereba yokatta mono o." english="You could have at least talked it over with me." />

ものを can imply frustration or regret that something did not happen. とは言うものの concedes what was said before adding a qualification. じゃあるまいし is blunt and colloquial, often reproaching someone for acting as if they were a child or another inappropriate comparison.

<Hint type="insight">A speaker may use とは言うものの to sound balanced: “I understand the concern; that said, we need more information.” ものを is more emotionally charged and can sound reproachful because the alternative seems obvious in hindsight.</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">Surprised that X: sentence-final とは. Expected X, but Y: と思いきや. Sharp comparison: にひきかえ. Regretful “you could have”: ものを. Acknowledge, then qualify: とは言うものの.</Hint>
