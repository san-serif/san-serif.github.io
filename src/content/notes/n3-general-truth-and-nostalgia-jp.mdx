ものだ has two useful functions here. Plain form + ものだ can express a general truth, expected behavior, or an observation about how life works. Past form + ものだ looks back nostalgically on something the speaker used to do often. The present or past form changes whether the comment is general or tied to repeated personal experience.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="人[ひと]は失敗[しっぱい]から学[まな]ぶものです。" romaji="Hito wa shippai kara manabu mono desu." english="People learn from mistakes." />

Plain form + ものだ presents a broad observation or something the speaker considers natural. Depending on context, it can also give gentle advice about what one should do.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="子[こ]どものころは、夏[なつ]になると毎日[まいにち]川[かわ]で泳[およ]いだものです。" romaji="Kodomo no koro wa, natsu ni naru to mainichi kawa de oyoida mono desu." english="When I was a child, I used to swim in the river every day in summer." />

Past form + ものだ recalls a repeated or familiar past habit, often with nostalgia. It does not usually describe a single completed event; use the plain past alone for that.

<Hint type="insight">A speaker may say 時間は大切にするものだ as a general reminder, “one should value time.” 思えば、よく一緒に遊んだものだ looks back fondly on a repeated past activity.</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">General truth or expected behavior: Vるものだ. Repeated past habit recalled with feeling: Vたものだ. The past marker is what shifts the sentence from general comment to looking back.</Hint>
