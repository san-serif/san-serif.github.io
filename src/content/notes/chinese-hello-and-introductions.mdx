You're meeting a new classmate. A few complete Mandarin phrases let you greet them, exchange names, and say goodbye. The following lessons unpack the sentence patterns behind these first words.

### Hello and goodbye

<CSentenceBlock simplified="你好！" traditional="你好！" pinyin="Nǐ hǎo!" english="Hello!" />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="老师好！" traditional="老師好！" pinyin="Lǎoshī hǎo!" english="Hello, teacher!" />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="再见！" traditional="再見！" pinyin="Zàijiàn!" english="Goodbye!" />

<Hint type="hint">您好 is a respectful greeting; 你好 is the everyday form. Both use 好, “good.” This lesson shows Mandarin Pinyin; a Cantonese reading is shown only on lessons whose Cantonese wording has been reviewed.</Hint>

### Exchange names

Use 我叫 plus your name. To ask the other person, replace your name with 什么名字. Mandarin normally leaves that question phrase where the answer would appear.

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我叫安娜。" traditional="我叫安娜。" pinyin="Wǒ jiào Ānnà." english="My name is Anna." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="你叫什么名字？" traditional="你叫什麼名字？" pinyin="Nǐ jiào shénme míngzi?" english="What's your name?" />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我叫李明。很高兴认识你。" traditional="我叫李明。很高興認識你。" pinyin="Wǒ jiào Lǐ Míng. Hěn gāoxìng rènshi nǐ." english="I'm Li Ming. Nice to meet you." />

<Hint type="hint">我 means “I”; 你 means “you.” 叫 introduces a name here. The next lessons explain 是 for identifying someone and 有 for having something or saying that something exists.</Hint>
