Alongside ことができる, which you already know, Japanese has a shorter potential form built directly into the verb. る-verbs add られる to the stem; う-verbs swap their final う-sound for an え-sound plus る.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="大輔[だいすけ]さんはピアノを弾[ひ]けます。" romaji="Daisuke-san wa piano o hikemasu." english="Daisuke can play the piano." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="直樹[なおき]さんは人[ひと]の名前[なまえ]がすぐに覚[おぼ]えられます。" romaji="Naoki-san wa hito no namae ga sugu ni oboeraremasu." english="Naoki can remember people's names quickly." />

<Hint>弾く becomes 弾ける (く→け), 覚える becomes 覚えられる (drop る, add られる). The object of the original verb often switches from を to が in the potential form, ピアノを弾く becomes ピアノが弾ける, though を still works too in casual speech.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="子供[こども]の頃[ころ]は海[うみ]で泳[およ]げませんでしたが、今[いま]は泳[およ]げます。" romaji="Kodomo no koro wa umi de oyogemasen deshita ga, ima wa oyogemasu." english="I couldn't swim in the ocean as a kid, but I can now." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="子供[こども]の頃[ころ]はどこでも寝[ね]られましたが、今[いま]は寝[ね]られません。" romaji="Kodomo no koro wa doko demo neraremashita ga, ima wa neraremasen." english="I could sleep anywhere as a kid, but I can't now." />

<Hint>The passive form and the potential form of a る-verb look completely identical, 見られる can mean either "is seen" or "can see," depending on context alone. う-verbs avoid this ambiguity entirely since their passive and potential forms are built differently, 飲まれる (passive) versus 飲める (potential).</Hint>
