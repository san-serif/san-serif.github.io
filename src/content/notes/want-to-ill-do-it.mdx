You are arranging a meal with friends. First ask what someone wants to do; then volunteer for a job yourself. -(으)ㄹ래요? invites a choice, while -(으)ㄹ게요 makes a commitment the listener can use.

### Ask for a Choice

Use -ㄹ래요 after a vowel or ㄹ and -을래요 after another consonant: 갈래요, 만들래요, 먹을래요. In a question it asks about the listener’s wishes; in a statement it expresses the speaker’s choice.

<SentenceBlock polite="금요일에 같이 저녁 먹을래요?" politeRom="geumyoire gachi jeonyeok meogeullaeyo?" casual="금요일에 같이 저녁 먹을래?" casualRom="geumyoire gachi jeonyeok meogeullae?" english="Would you like to have dinner together on Friday?" />

<SentenceBlock polite="저는 비빔밥을 먹을래요." politeRom="jeoneun bibimbabeul meogeullaeyo." casual="나는 비빔밥을 먹을래." casualRom="naneun bibimbabeul meogeullae." english="I’ll have bibimbap." />

### Volunteer in Response

Use the same stem split for -ㄹ게요/-을게요: 제가 예약할게요. This is a first-person promise, offer, or decision made with the listener in mind. [-(으)ㄹ 거예요](/notes/going-to) can simply state a plan; -ㄹ게요 makes it relevant to this conversation.

<SentenceBlock polite="제가 식당을 예약할게요." politeRom="jega sikdangeul yeyakhalgeyo." casual="내가 식당을 예약할게." casualRom="naega sikdangeul yeyakhalge." english="I’ll book the restaurant." />

<SentenceBlock polite="그럼 저는 음료수를 가져올게요." politeRom="geureom jeoneun eumnyosureul gajyeoolgeyo." casual="그럼 나는 음료수를 가져올게." casualRom="geureom naneun eumnyosureul gajyeoolge." english="Then I’ll bring the drinks." />

<Hint type="hint">갈게요 is spelled with ㄱ even though the ending sounds like 께요. These are conversational endings: the examples offer polite and casual versions because a formal ending would change the expression being taught.</Hint>
