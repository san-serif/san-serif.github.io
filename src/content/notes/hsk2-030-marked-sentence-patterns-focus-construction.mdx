Your friend knows you went to Shanghai, but asks how you got there. The trip is already understood; 是…的 helps you spotlight the detail they’re asking about. Put that detail between 是 and 的.

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我是坐火车去上海的。" traditional="我是坐火車去上海的。" pinyin="wǒ shì zuò huǒchē qù Shànghǎi de." jyutping="ngo5 si6 co5 fo2 ce1 heoi3 Soeng6 hoi2 dik1." english="I went to Shanghai by train." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我们是昨天到的。" traditional="我們是昨天到的。" pinyin="wǒmen shì zuótiān dào de." jyutping="ngo5 mun4 si6 zok3 tin1 dou3 dik1." english="We arrived yesterday." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="她是在北京学的中文。" traditional="她是在北京學的中文。" pinyin="tā shì zài Běijīng xué de Zhōngwén." jyutping="taa1 si6 zoi6 Bak1 ging1 hok6 dik1 zung1 man4." english="She learned Chinese in Beijing." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="这张照片是我弟弟拍的。" traditional="這張照片是我弟弟拍的。" pinyin="zhè zhāng zhàopiàn shì wǒ dìdi pāi de." jyutping="ze5 zoeng1 ziu3 pin3 si6 ngo5 dai6 dai2 paak3 dik1." english="My younger brother took this photo." />

<Hint type="hint">This pattern usually talks about a completed event. It highlights one piece of background information: when, where, how, or who. You can often leave out 是 in casual speech, but keep 的.</Hint>
