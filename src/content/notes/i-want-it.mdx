ほしい is an い-adjective meaning "wanted," used for wanting a thing rather than wanting to do something, that job belongs to たい. The thing wanted takes が, not を.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="私[わたし]は今[いま]、ピアノがほしいです。" romaji="Watashi wa ima, piano ga hoshii desu." english="I want a piano right now." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="新[あたら]しい部屋[へや]に何[なに]が欲[ほ]しいですか。" romaji="Atarashii heya ni nani ga hoshii desu ka." english="What do you want for your new room?" />

<Hint>ほしい takes が for the thing wanted, the same pattern たい runs on. Neither borrows を, even though English "want" would normally take a direct object. Both wants describe a state you're in, not an action you're doing to the object.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="田中[たなか]さんは最近[さいきん]、車[くるま]を欲[ほ]しがっています。" romaji="Tanaka-san wa saikin, kuruma o hoshigatteimasu." english="Tanaka has been wanting a car recently." />

<Hint>Talking about someone else's want swaps ほしい for 欲しがっている, the exact same shift たい makes into たがっている. Notice the particle changes too, 欲しがる takes を like a normal verb, while plain ほしい takes が like an adjective. The whole word class changes, not just the ending.</Hint>
