무슨 and 어떤 both slot in front of a noun and both translate loosely as "what kind of," but they're asking for different things. 무슨 wants a category or a name: Korean food, action movies. 어떤 wants a quality or a description: spicy food, a movie that makes you cry.

<SentenceBlock formal="무슨 계절을 좋아합니까?" formalRom="museun gyejeoreul joahamnikka?" polite="무슨 계절을 좋아해요?" politeRom="museun gyejeoreul joahaeyo?" casual="무슨 계절을 좋아해?" casualRom="museun gyejeoreul joahae?" english="What season do you like?" />

<SentenceBlock formal="어떤 영화를 좋아합니까?" formalRom="eotteon yeonghwareul joahamnikka?" polite="어떤 영화를 좋아해요?" politeRom="eotteon yeonghwareul joahaeyo?" casual="어떤 영화를 좋아해?" casualRom="eotteon yeonghwareul joahae?" english="What kind of movie do you like?" />

<Hint>Test it by imagining the answer. 무슨 음식을 좋아해요? expects "Korean food" or "Italian food," a category with a name. 어떤 음식을 좋아해요? expects "spicy food" or "something not too sweet," a description. If the natural reply names a type, reach for 무슨. If it names a trait, reach for 어떤.</Hint>

<SentenceBlock formal="어떤 선물이 좋았습니까?" formalRom="eotteon seonmuri joatseumnikka?" polite="어떤 선물이 좋았어요?" politeRom="eotteon seonmuri joasseoyo?" casual="어떤 선물이 좋았어?" casualRom="eotteon seonmuri joasseo?" english="What kind of gift was good?" />

<SentenceBlock formal="어떤 사람을 좋아합니까?" formalRom="eotteon sarameul joahamnikka?" polite="어떤 사람을 좋아해요?" politeRom="eotteon sarameul joahaeyo?" casual="어떤 사람을 좋아해?" casualRom="eotteon sarameul joahae?" english="What kind of person is your type?" />

<Hint>In fast, casual conversation, native speakers sometimes blur this line, using 무슨 where 어떤 would be textbook-correct. The distinction is still worth learning properly: it's the difference between sounding natural and sounding fluent.</Hint>
