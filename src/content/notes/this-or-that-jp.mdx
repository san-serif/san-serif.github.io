か between two nouns means "or," offering a choice the same way と means "and." It attaches directly to both nouns with nothing else needed.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="電車[でんしゃ]かバスで行[い]きます。" romaji="Densha ka basu de ikimasu." english="I'll go by train or bus." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="コーヒーか野菜[やさい]ジュースを飲[の]みます。" romaji="Koohii ka yasai juusu o nomimasu." english="I'll drink coffee or vegetable juice." />

<Hint>か only offers a choice between things, not a choice between actions. Choosing between two verbs needs 〜たり〜たりする or a different construction entirely, this か is strictly for nouns standing in for each other.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="メールか電話[でんわ]かどちらかをします。" romaji="Meeru ka denwa ka dochira ka o shimasu." english="I'll either email or call, one or the other." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="私[わたし]の家[いえ]かけんさんのアパートかどちらかでしましょう。" romaji="Watashi no ie ka Ken-san no apaato ka dochira ka de shimashou." english="Let's do it at either my place or Ken's apartment." />

<Hint>どちらか tacked on after the か pair spells out explicitly that a choice still needs to be made between them, "one of the two." It's not required, メールか電話をします already works fine, but どちらか makes the sentence read less like a vague list and more like a genuine either-or decision.</Hint>
