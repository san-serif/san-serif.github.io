You missed a call because you were preparing for a meeting. Later, someone compliments the presentation. -느라고 explains what occupied you; -기는요 gently pushes back on what someone has just said.

### One Activity Takes Up the Time

Verb stem + -느라고 gives an activity that occupied you and led to a difficulty, missed action, or unwelcome result. Drop ㄹ before ㄴ: 만들다 → 만드느라고. In the basic pattern, both clauses have the same actor. Put tense on the final verb, rather than before -느라고.

<SentenceBlock polite="자료를 만드느라고 잠을 못 잤어요." politeRom="jaryoreul mandeuneurago jameul mot jasseoyo." casual="자료를 만드느라고 잠을 못 잤어." casualRom="jaryoreul mandeuneurago jameul mot jasseo." english="I couldn’t sleep because I was making the materials." />

### Gently Push Back

Attach -기는요 to the word you are responding to: 잘하기는요, 고맙기는요. The casual version is -기는. It can soften a rejection of praise or dismiss the need for thanks; a short explanation usually makes the response easier to understand.

<SentenceBlock polite="잘하기는요. 아직 많이 배워야 해요." politeRom="jalhagineunyo. ajik mani baewoya haeyo." casual="잘하기는. 아직 많이 배워야 해." casualRom="jalhagineun. ajik mani baewoya hae." english="Oh, I’m hardly that good. I still have a lot to learn." />

<SentenceBlock polite="고맙기는요. 어려운 일도 아니었어요." politeRom="gomapgineunyo. eoryeoun ildo anieosseoyo." casual="고맙기는. 어려운 일도 아니었어." casualRom="gomapgineun. eoryeoun ildo anieosseo." english="No need to thank me. It wasn’t difficult." />

<Hint type="hint">Use -느라고 with an activity that occupied the same person: 공부하느라고 못 갔어요. “Because the weather was cold” needs another reason connector, such as 추워서. With praise, -기는요 is one possible response; 감사합니다 is also a complete, natural choice.</Hint>
