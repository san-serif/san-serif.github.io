These patterns express limits from different angles. しかない says there is only one option left. がたい means something is difficult to do, often because it is emotionally or intellectually hard to accept. わけにはいかない says circumstances, responsibility, or social expectations make an action impossible to take. 決して〜ない and めったに〜ない strengthen negative statements, meaning “never” and “rarely.” 切る and 抜く can both mean carrying an action through completely; 切れない marks inability to finish it all. ないことはない is a cautious double negative: something is possible, though perhaps not ideal. Volitional + とする describes trying or starting to do something; ようとしない says someone refuses or does not try. Volitional + としたが describes an attempt that did not lead to the intended result.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="今[いま]は待[ま]つしかありません。" romaji="Ima wa matsu shika arimasen." english="For now, all we can do is wait." />

Verb dictionary form + しかない presents the sole remaining option. It can sound resigned, but may also be a straightforward assessment of the situation.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="大切[たいせつ]な約束[やくそく]なので、今[いま]さら断[ことわ]るわけにはいきません。" romaji="Taisetsu na yakusoku na node, imasara kotowaru wake ni wa ikimasen." english="It's an important promise, so I can't back out now." />

わけにはいかない means the speaker cannot do something because it would violate a responsibility or expectation. It is different from physical inability, which uses できない. がたい often appears with verbs such as 信じる or 理解する: 信じがたい, “hard to believe.”

<JSentenceBlock furigana="彼[かれ]が約束[やくそく]を忘[わす]れることは決[けっ]してありません。" romaji="Kare ga yakusoku o wasureru koto wa kesshite arimasen." english="He never forgets a promise." />

決して is used with a negative to make a strong “never/not at all.” めったに also pairs with a negative but means “rarely,” so it is less absolute.

一晩で全部読み切った means “I finished reading it all in one night”; 読み切れなかった means “I couldn't finish it all.” ないことはない softens a positive claim: 行けないことはない, “it's not that I can't go,” often leaving some reservation unstated.

<Hint type="insight">Use わけにはいかない when you could physically do something but feel you must not: 約束したから、途中でやめるわけにはいかない, “I promised, so I can't quit halfway.” Use しかない when the alternatives are simply gone.</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">Only option: Vるしかない. Hard to accept or do: Vがたい. Cannot do because of duty or circumstances: Vるわけにはいかない. Never: 決して～ない. Rarely: めったに～ない.</Hint>
