You walk into a room and tell your friend what’s there. Start with the place, then say what exists or what you can see. 有 introduces something present; 着 describes an arrangement that’s already in place.

<CSentenceBlock simplified="桌子上有一本书。" traditional="桌子上有一本書。" pinyin="zhuōzi shàng yǒu yì běn shū." jyutping="zoek3 zi2 soeng6 jau5 jat1 bun2 syu1." english="There’s a book on the table." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="门口站着两个人。" traditional="門口站著兩個人。" pinyin="ménkǒu zhàn zhe liǎng ge rén." jyutping="mun4 hau2 zaam6 zoek6 loeng5 go3 jan4." english="Two people are standing at the doorway." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="墙上挂着一张照片。" traditional="牆上掛著一張照片。" pinyin="qiáng shàng guà zhe yì zhāng zhàopiàn." jyutping="coeng4 soeng6 gwaa3 zoek6 jat1 zoeng1 ziu3 pin3." english="There’s a photo hanging on the wall." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="楼下有一家咖啡店。" traditional="樓下有一家咖啡店。" pinyin="lóuxià yǒu yì jiā kāfēidiàn." jyutping="lau4 haa6 jau5 jat1 gaa1 gaa3 fe1 dim3." english="There’s a café downstairs." />

<Hint type="hint">Use 有 to introduce what exists in a place. With 着, the verb often shows the visible position or state—站着, 挂着, 开着—rather than a new event happening right now.</Hint>
