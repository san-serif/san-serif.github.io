At the market, you can point to a new notebook, say you’re buying fruit, or tell a friend you came with your sister. Each phrase works as one useful bundle: the words hold together to name a thing, an action, or the people involved. You can follow the sentence without stopping to label every part.

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我和老师一起去买水果。" traditional="我和老師一起去買水果。" pinyin="wǒ hé lǎoshī yìqǐ qù mǎi shuǐguǒ." jyutping="ngo5 wo4 lou5 si1 jat1 hei2 heoi3 maai5 seoi2 gwo2." english="The teacher and I are going to buy fruit together." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="这个新笔记本是姐姐的。" traditional="這個新筆記本是姐姐的。" pinyin="zhè ge xīn bǐjìběn shì jiějie de." jyutping="ze5 go3 san1 bat1 gei3 bun2 si6 ze2 ze2 dik1." english="This new notebook is my older sister’s." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我把老师说的话记下来了。" traditional="我把老師說的話記下來了。" pinyin="wǒ bǎ lǎoshī shuō de huà jì xiàlai le." jyutping="ngo5 baa2 lou5 si1 syut3 dik1 waa6 gei3 haa6 loi4 liu5." english="I wrote down what the teacher said." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="买水果的地方就在前面。" traditional="買水果的地方就在前面。" pinyin="mǎi shuǐguǒ de dìfang jiù zài qiánmiàn." jyutping="maai5 seoi2 gwo2 dik1 dei6 fong1 zau6 zoi6 cin4 min6." english="The place to buy fruit is just ahead." />

<Hint type="hint">A phrase can have a few words but still fill one slot in the sentence. 的 often turns a description into a noun phrase; a verb plus its object—like 买水果—can act as a unit too.</Hint>
