たら attaches after a verb, adjective, or noun's plain past た-form and means "if" or "when," setting up a condition for whatever follows. It's the most flexible of Japanese's conditionals, comfortable with one-time events, hypotheticals, and polite suggestions alike.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="明日[あした]、時間[じかん]があったら、出[で]かけませんか。" romaji="Ashita, jikan ga attara, dekakemasen ka." english="If you have time tomorrow, want to go out?" />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="日曜日[にちようび]、天気[てんき]が良[よ]かったら、海[うみ]に行[い]きませんか。" romaji="Nichiyoubi, tenki ga yokattara, umi ni ikimasen ka." english="If the weather's good on Sunday, want to go to the beach?" />

<Hint>Built literally on the past tense, 起きたら, 買わなかったら, 高かったら, but the meaning is entirely about the future or a hypothetical, not the past. Combined with ませんか at the end, たら becomes a natural way to float a plan that depends on something else working out first.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="課長[かちょう]の都合[つごう]が悪[わる]かったらどうしてますか。" romaji="Kachou no tsugou ga warukattara dou shitemasu ka." english="What do you do if it turns out to be inconvenient for the manager?" />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="明日[あした]、宿題[しゅくだい]が終[お]わったら、お茶[ちゃ]を飲[の]みませんか。" romaji="Ashita, shukudai ga owattara, ocha o nomimasen ka." english="If you finish your homework tomorrow, want to grab tea?" />

<Hint>どうしますか after たら asks what someone's plan is for a given scenario, "what do you do if X happens," useful for talking through contingencies rather than committing to a single outcome.</Hint>
