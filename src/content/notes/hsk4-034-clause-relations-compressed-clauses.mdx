You ask your friend whether they’re joining the hike. They say, “You’re not going, and I’m not going either.” The clauses sit side by side; the listener understands their relationship without a separate word such as 因为 or 所以.

<CSentenceBlock simplified="你不想去，我也不想去。" traditional="你不想去，我也不想去。" pinyin="nǐ bù xiǎng qù, wǒ yě bù xiǎng qù." jyutping="nei5 bat1 soeng2 heoi3, ngo5 jaa5 bat1 soeng2 heoi3." english="You don’t want to go, and I don’t either." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="天气预报说会下雨，我们就不爬山了。" traditional="天氣預報說會下雨，我們就不爬山了。" pinyin="tiānqì yùbào shuō huì xiàyǔ, wǒmen jiù bù páshān le." jyutping="tin1 hei3 jyu6 bou3 syut3 wui5 haa6 jyu5, ngo5 mun4 zau6 bat1 paa4 saan1 liu5." english="The forecast says it’ll rain, so we’re not going hiking." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="他负责买票，我负责订酒店，谁也不用操心。" traditional="他負責買票，我負責訂酒店，誰也不用操心。" pinyin="tā fùzé mǎi piào, wǒ fùzé dìng jiǔdiàn, shéi yě búyòng cāoxīn." jyutping="taa1 fu6 zaak3 maai5 piu3, ngo5 fu6 zaak3 ding6 zau2 dim3, seoi4 jaa5 bat1 jung6 cou1 sam1." english="He’ll buy the tickets, I’ll book the hotel, and neither of us needs to worry." />

<Hint type="hint">The clauses are short because the relationship is easy to infer from what comes before and after. 不……也…… makes the shared negative meaning explicit, but the sentence still needs no separate linking conjunction.</Hint>
