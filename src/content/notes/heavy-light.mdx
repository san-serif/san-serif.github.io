Any adjective stem ending in ㅂ drops it and replaces it with 우 before an -아요/-어요 ending, then contracts with 어요 into 워요. 무겁다 (heavy) becomes 무거워요, never 무겁어요. This is the full version of the pattern that's been sitting in hints since [날씨가 어때요?](/notes/hows-the-weather).

<SentenceBlock formal="숙제가 어렵습니다." formalRom="sukjega eoryeopseumnida." polite="숙제가 어려워요." politeRom="sukjega eoryeowoyo." casual="숙제가 어려워." casualRom="sukjega eoryeowo." english="The homework is difficult." />

<SentenceBlock formal="제주도가 아름답습니다." formalRom="jejudoga areumdapseumnida." polite="제주도가 아름다워요." politeRom="jejudoga areumdawoyo." casual="제주도가 아름다워." casualRom="jejudoga areumdawo." english="Jeju Island is beautiful." />

<Hint>Any two-syllable adjective ending in 겁다, 럽다, 롭다, or 웁다 is very likely ㅂ-irregular. It's one of the most common irregular shapes in the language, common enough that guessing 워요 the first time you meet a new ㅂ-ending adjective is usually the right bet.</Hint>

Not every adjective plays this game. 하다-adjectives, 따뜻하다 (warm), 시원하다 (cool) from [날씨가 어때요?](/notes/hows-the-weather), conjugate completely predictably, always contracting to 해요 no matter what.

<SentenceBlock formal="날씨가 따뜻합니다." formalRom="nalssiga ttatteuthamnida." polite="날씨가 따뜻해요." politeRom="nalssiga ttatteuthaeyo." casual="날씨가 따뜻해." casualRom="nalssiga ttatteuthae." english="The weather is warm." />

<SentenceBlock formal="어제 날씨가 더웠습니다." formalRom="eoje nalssiga deowotseumnida." polite="어제 날씨가 더웠어요." politeRom="eoje nalssiga deowosseoyo." casual="어제 날씨가 더웠어." casualRom="eoje nalssiga deowosseo." english="Yesterday the weather was hot." />

<Hint>더웠어요 stacks two things you now know: the ㅂ-irregular shift (덥 becomes 더우) and the past tense from [-았/었어요](/notes/already-done) (그 + 었어요). Irregular verbs don't get a pass on tense, they just carry the irregularity into whatever ending comes next.</Hint>
