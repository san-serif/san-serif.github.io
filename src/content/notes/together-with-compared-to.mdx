と marks a partner in an action that inherently involves two people, like meeting, dating, or marrying, and marks the other side of a comparison, like being the same as or different from something.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="大輔[だいすけ]さんは沙耶[さや]さんと来月[らいげつ]結婚[けっこん]します。" romaji="Daisuke-san wa Saya-san to raigetsu kekkon shimasu." english="Daisuke is marrying Saya next month." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="直樹[なおき]さんは由紀[ゆき]さんとよく話[はな]し合[あ]った。" romaji="Naoki-san wa Yuki-san to yoku hanashiatta." english="Naoki and Yuki talked things through a lot." />

<Hint>Verbs like 結婚する, 話し合う, and 別れる describe something that structurally needs two people, and と, not を, marks the other party involved, since neither person is simply the object of the other's action, they're both doing it together.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="私[わたし]の考[かんが]え方[かた]は部長[ぶちょう]の考[かんが]え方[かた]と大体[だいたい]同[おな]じです。" romaji="Watashi no kangaekata wa buchou no kangaekata to daitai onaji desu." english="My way of thinking is pretty much the same as the department head's." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="今[いま]の電話[でんわ]は昔[むかし]の電話[でんわ]と違[ちが]います。" romaji="Ima no denwa wa mukashi no denwa to chigaimasu." english="Today's phones are different from phones in the past." />

<Hint>同じ (the same) and 違う (different) both need と to mark what they're being compared against, X はYと同じです, "X is the same as Y," X はYと違います, "X is different from Y," a fixed pattern worth memorizing as a set.</Hint>
