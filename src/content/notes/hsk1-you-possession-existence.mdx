有 means “have” when the subject possesses something, and it introduces existence when the place comes first: 桌子上有一本书. In an existence sentence, the new or indefinite thing typically follows 有.

### Examples

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我有一个弟弟。" traditional="我有一個弟弟。" pinyin="Wǒ yǒu yí ge dìdi." jyutping="ngo5 jau5 jat1 go3 dai6 dai2." english="I have a younger brother." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="教室里有很多学生。" traditional="教室裡有很多學生。" pinyin="Jiàoshì lǐ yǒu hěn duō xuésheng." jyutping="gaau3 sat1 leoi5 jau5 han2 do1 hok6 saang1." english="There are many students in the classroom." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="桌子上有一杯水。" traditional="桌子上有一杯水。" pinyin="Zhuōzi shàng yǒu yì bēi shuǐ." jyutping="zoek3 zi2 soeng6 jau5 jat1 bui1 seoi2." english="There is a cup of water on the table." />

<Hint type="hint">For “there is/are,” put the place first and the thing after 有. This differs from English there-sentences.</Hint>
