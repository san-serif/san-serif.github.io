まだ means "still," もう means "already." Both describe where something stands relative to a change, and both shift meaning once a negative verb shows up next to them.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="まだ駅[えき]で電車[でんしゃ]を待[ま]っています。" romaji="Mada eki de densha o matteimasu." english="I'm still waiting for the train at the station." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="いいえ、まだ食[た]べていません。" romaji="Iie, mada tabeteimasen." english="No, I haven't eaten yet." />

<Hint type="hint">まだ paired with a negative doesn't mean "still not," it means "not yet." まだ食べていません is the standard way to answer "have you eaten?" with a no, the event just hasn't happened up to this point, it isn't a permanent refusal.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="もう買[か]いました。" romaji="Mou kaimashita." english="I already bought it." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="もうチョコレートを食[た]べません。" romaji="Mou chokoreeto o tabemasen." english="I don't eat chocolate anymore." />

<Hint type="hint">もう paired with a negative flips to "not anymore," describing something that used to be true and now isn't, the opposite direction from まだ's "not yet." Both words describe the same idea, a state relative to change, まだ looks at what hasn't happened up to now, もう looks at what has, or has stopped.</Hint>

とっくに means “long ago / well before now” and emphasizes that an event happened much earlier than the speaker expected. 未[いま]だに means “still / even now,” often stressing that a situation continues longer than expected.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="締[し]め切[き]りはとっくに過[す]ぎましたが、原因[げんいん]は未[いま]だに分[わ]かっていません。" romaji="Shimekiri wa tokku ni sugimashita ga, gen'in wa imada ni wakatte imasen." english="The deadline passed long ago, but the cause is still unknown." />

<Hint type="insight">とっくに is useful when something should already be finished; 未だに highlights that an expected change has not happened, often with surprise or frustration. Both add stronger timing expectations than everyday もう and まだ.</Hint>
