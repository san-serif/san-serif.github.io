必ずしも～ない means “not necessarily.” It denies that a claim is always or automatically true, leaving room for exceptions.

わけ frames how the speaker interprets a conclusion. わけだ says the conclusion follows from what we know (“no wonder”); わけではない corrects an assumption (“it doesn't mean that”); わけがない rejects a conclusion as impossible (“there's no way”). The phrases look similar, but the particles だ・ではない・がない change the logic.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="毎日[まいにち]練習[れんしゅう]しているんですか。だから上手[じょうず]なわけですね。" romaji="Mainichi renshuu shite iru n desu ka. Dakara jouzu na wake desu ne." english="You practice every day? No wonder you're so good." />

わけだ presents a result as understandable once the reason is known. It often follows だから or a newly learned fact: “given that, it makes sense that…” With a な-adjective or noun, use なわけだ.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="日本[にほん]に長[なが]く住[す]んでいても、漢字[かんじ]が全部[ぜんぶ]読[よ]めるわけではありません。" romaji="Nihon ni nagaku sunde ite mo, kanji ga zenbu yomeru wake de wa arimasen." english="Even if you live in Japan for a long time, it doesn't mean you can read every kanji." />

わけではない denies the interpretation or generalization, not necessarily the underlying fact. The example doesn't claim that nobody who lives in Japan can read kanji; it pushes back against the assumption that length of residence guarantees it.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="あの人[ひと]が犯人[はんにん]なわけがありません。" romaji="Ano hito ga hannin na wake ga arimasen." english="There's no way that person is the culprit." />

わけがない is a strong judgment that something cannot be true or cannot happen. Nouns and な-adjectives use なわけがない; verbs and い-adjectives attach in their plain form.

考[かんが]えられない means “unthinkable” or “hard to imagine.” It describes the speaker's reaction to an idea as unreasonable or unbelievable; わけがない makes the stronger logical claim that something cannot be the case.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="そんな大切[たいせつ]な約束[やくそく]を忘[わす]れるなんて、考[かんが]えられません。" romaji="Sonna taisetsu na yakusoku o wasureru nante, kangaeraremasen." english="I can't imagine forgetting such an important promise." />

とても〜ない expresses practical impossibility when something is so extreme that it cannot be done: 一日ではとても終わらない, “there's no way I can finish it in one day.” Unlike わけがない, it focuses on the difficulty or degree, not the speaker's logical certainty that a claim is false.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="この量[りょう]は多[おお]すぎて、今日中[きょうじゅう]にはとても終[お]わりません。" romaji="Kono ryou wa oosugite, kyoujuu ni wa totemo owarimasen." english="This is too much; there's no way I'll finish it today." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="有名[ゆうめい]な店[みせ]なら、必[かなら]ずおいしいとは限[かぎ]りません。" romaji="Yuumei na mise nara, kanarazu oishii to wa kagirimasen." english="A famous restaurant isn't necessarily delicious." />

とは限らない is a related way to reject an overgeneralization: “not necessarily” or “not always.” Unlike わけがない, it does not say something is impossible; it says there is no guarantee that it is true in every case.

からといって warns that one fact alone does not justify a conclusion: 安いからといって、質が悪いとは限らない, “just because it's cheap doesn't mean the quality is bad.” というものではない and というわけではない also deny a simplistic generalization, often with a slightly more explanatory tone: 努力すれば必ず成功するというものではない, “it's not that you will always succeed just by making an effort.” ことにはならない likewise says a fact does not automatically mean that the stated result follows.

のも当然[とうぜん]だ and のももっともだ say that a reaction or outcome is understandable or only natural. わけだ draws a conclusion from newly understood facts; these phrases evaluate whether a response makes sense.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="急[きゅう]に予定[よてい]を変[か]えられたら、彼[かれ]が困[こま]るのも当然[とうぜん]です。" romaji="Kyuu ni yotei o kaeraretara, kare ga komaru no mo touzen desu." english="If the plan is changed suddenly, it's only natural that he'd be troubled." />

<Hint type="insight">In real life, わけではない is useful for softening a correction: 日本の食べ物が嫌いなわけではないんです, “It's not that I dislike Japanese food.” The speaker narrows or corrects what the listener inferred, without bluntly saying the listener is wrong.</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">Keep the force in order: わけだ = “that explains it”; わけではない = “that doesn't mean…”; とは限らない = “not always / not necessarily”; わけがない = “there's no way.” はず and に違いない, from the earlier lessons, express expectation or confidence; this わけ group explains the speaker's reasoning about a conclusion.</Hint>
