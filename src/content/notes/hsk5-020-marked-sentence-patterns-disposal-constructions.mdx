The suitcase is open on the bed, and you’re packing for a trip. 把 lets you bring a known object forward and say what you do to it: pack it, move it, use it, or take care of it. The result of the action is the point of the sentence.

<CSentenceBlock simplified="先把钥匙放在桌上，回家就不用找了。" traditional="先把鑰匙放在桌上，回家就不用找了。" pinyin="Xiān bǎ yàoshi fàng zài zhuō shàng, huí jiā jiù búyòng zhǎo le." jyutping="sin1 baa2 joek6 si4 fong3 zoi6 zoek3 soeng6, wui4 gaa1 zau6 bat1 jung6 zaau2 liu5." english="Put the keys on the table first, so you won’t have to look for them when you get home." />

Here the keys are already known, so they come after 把. The phrase 放在桌上 tells us where they end up.

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我把照片发给同事了，你也看看吧。" traditional="我把照片發給同事了，你也看看吧。" pinyin="Wǒ bǎ zhàopiàn fā gěi tóngshì le, nǐ yě kànkan ba." jyutping="ngo5 baa2 ziu3 pin3 faat3 kap1 tung4 si6 liu5, nei5 jaa5 hon3 hon3 baa6." english="I sent the photos to my colleague; have a look too." />

The second half is an ordinary suggestion. In the first half, 把 puts the photos before the completed action of sending them.

<CSentenceBlock simplified="周末我把孩子的衣服洗干净了。" traditional="週末我把孩子的衣服洗乾淨了。" pinyin="Zhōumò wǒ bǎ háizi de yīfu xǐ gānjìng le." jyutping="zau1 mut6 ngo5 baa2 haai4 zi2 dik1 ji1 fuk6 sai2 gon1 zeng6 liu5." english="I got the children’s clothes clean over the weekend." />

The result matters here: the clothes are clean after the washing. 把 is a natural fit because the action affects them.

<Hint type="hint">A 把 sentence needs an object both speakers can identify, and the verb phrase needs to say what was done with it. “我把钥匙放在桌上” works; just “我把钥匙” leaves the action unfinished.</Hint>

<Hint type="insight">Use a plain object sentence when you are simply introducing something: 我买了钥匙 (I bought some keys). Use 把 when the next part tells what you did with those keys: 我把钥匙放在桌上.</Hint>
