<JapaneseLessonHead />

つもり attaches after a verb's plain dictionary form or negative form and states a firm intention, "I plan to," or "I don't plan to." It's most natural talking about yourself, since it claims to know your own settled decision.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="毎朝[まいあさ]、早[はや]く起[お]きるつもりです。" romaji="Maiasa, hayaku okiru tsumori desu." english="I plan to wake up early every morning." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="ケーキを食[た]べないつもりです。" romaji="Keeki o tabenai tsumori desu." english="I don't plan to eat cake." />

<Hint>つもり sounds more settled and deliberate than たい, which just names a want. "I want to wake up early" doesn't guarantee anything, "I plan to wake up early" frames it as a decision you've already made.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="友達[ともだち]と旅行[りょこう]をするつもりです。" romaji="Tomodachi to ryokou o suru tsumori desu." english="I plan to travel with a friend." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="一週間[いっしゅうかん]くらい日本[にほん]にいるつもりです。" romaji="Isshuukan kurai nihon ni iru tsumori desu." english="I plan to be in Japan for about a week." />

<Hint>つもりでした, the past tense, describes a plan you had, often one that fell through, "I was planning to," implying things didn't go that way after all, distinct from つもりです which is still an active plan.</Hint>
