A verb describing an ongoing or habitual action takes -는 to modify a noun, no vowel-or-consonant split at all, always just -는: 좋아하는 노래, "the song I like." [큰 가방](/notes/a-big-bag)'s adjectives split into -ㄴ/은; a verb's present action doesn't split, because a fixed trait and an ongoing action aren't the same kind of description.

<SentenceBlock formal="제가 좋아하는 노래입니다." formalRom="jega joahaneun noraeimnida." polite="제가 좋아하는 노래예요." politeRom="jega joahaneun noraeyeyo." casual="내가 좋아하는 노래야." casualRom="naega joahaneun noraeya." english="This is the song I like." />

<SentenceBlock formal="제가 공부하는 세종학당입니다." formalRom="jega gongbuhaneun sejonghakdangimnida." polite="제가 공부하는 세종학당이에요." politeRom="jega gongbuhaneun sejonghakdangieyo." casual="내가 공부하는 세종학당이야." casualRom="naega gongbuhaneun sejonghakdangiya." english="This is the Sejong Institute I study at." />

<SentenceBlock formal="지금 에릭 씨가 먹는 빵이 무엇입니까?" formalRom="jigeum erik ssiga meongneun ppangi mueosimnikka?" polite="지금 에릭 씨가 먹는 빵이 뭐예요?" politeRom="jigeum erik ssiga meongneun ppangi mwoyeyo?" casual="지금 에릭 씨가 먹는 빵이 뭐야?" casualRom="jigeum erik ssiga meongneun ppangi mwoya?" english="What bread is Eric eating now?" />

<Hint>[-(으)ㄴ 후에](/notes/then-what) already showed you a verb taking -ㄴ/은, but only ever glued in front of 후에. On its own, that same ending modifies a noun with a completed, past action instead of an ongoing one: 만난 사람 is "the person I met," not "the person I'm meeting." Swap -는 for -(으)ㄴ and the whole clause moves into the past.</Hint>

<SentenceBlock formal="제가 자주 듣는 한국 음악입니다." formalRom="jega jaju deutneun hanguk eumagimnida." polite="제가 자주 듣는 한국 음악이에요." politeRom="jega jaju deutneun hanguk eumagieyo." casual="내가 자주 듣는 한국 음악이야." casualRom="naega jaju deutneun hanguk eumagiya." english="This is the Korean music I often listen to." />

<SentenceBlock formal="지금 만나는 사람 있습니까?" formalRom="jigeum mannaneun saram isseumnikka?" polite="지금 만나는 사람 있어요?" politeRom="jigeum mannaneun saram isseoyo?" casual="지금 만나는 사람 있어?" casualRom="jigeum mannaneun saram isseo?" english="Are you seeing anyone right now?" />

<Hint>지금 만나는 사람 있어요? is the standard, indirect way to ask if someone's dating anyone. It leans on [만나다](/notes/what-are-you-doing) doing double duty, the same verb used for a plain social "meeting," now carrying a romantic implication purely from context.</Hint>

<SentenceBlock formal="웃는 사람을 좋아합니다." formalRom="unneun sarameul joahamnida." polite="웃는 사람을 좋아해요." politeRom="unneun sarameul joahaeyo." casual="웃는 사람을 좋아해." casualRom="unneun sarameul joahae." english="I like people who are smiling." />

<SentenceBlock formal="제가 자주 가는 곳은 쇼핑몰입니다." formalRom="jega jaju ganeun goseun syopingmorimnida." polite="제가 자주 가는 곳은 쇼핑몰이에요." politeRom="jega jaju ganeun goseun syopingmorieyo." casual="내가 자주 가는 곳은 쇼핑몰이야." casualRom="naega jaju ganeun goseun syopingmoriya." english="The place I go often is the shopping mall." />
