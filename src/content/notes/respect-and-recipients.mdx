Your grandmother made a gift, and you are telling a friend about it. Respect for the person doing the action belongs inside the verb; “only to me” and “to me too” belong on the recipient.

### Respect the Person Doing It

Insert -(으)시- before the ending to honour the subject: 가시다, 읽으시다. In polite present speech this often becomes -세요; in the past, -(으)셨어요. With ㄹ stems, drop ㄹ: 만들다 → 만드시다. Adjectives also take it, as in 바쁘세요, and nouns use -(이)세요, as in 선생님이세요. Some everyday verbs have special respectful forms, such as 계시다 for 있다 and 드시다 for 먹다.

### Only to Someone, to Someone Too

Add 만 or 도 after 에게: 저에게만, 친구에게도. The spoken equivalents are 한테만 and 한테도. The respectful recipient particle is 께, as in 선생님께도. These particles describe who receives something, rather than who deserves -(으)시-.

<SentenceBlock formal="언니가 저에게만 선물을 줬습니다." formalRom="eonniga jeoegeman seonmureul jwotseumnida." polite="언니가 저에게만 선물을 줬어요." politeRom="eonniga jeoegeman seonmureul jwosseoyo." casual="언니가 나에게만 선물을 줬어." casualRom="eonniga naegeman seonmureul jwosseo." english="My older sister gave a present only to me." />

<SentenceBlock formal="친구에게도 사진을 보냈습니다." formalRom="chinguegedo sajineul bonaetseumnida." polite="친구에게도 사진을 보냈어요." politeRom="chinguegedo sajineul bonaesseoyo." casual="친구에게도 사진을 보냈어." casualRom="chinguegedo sajineul bonaesseo." english="I sent the photo to my friend too." />

<Hint type="hint">Politeness to the listener and respect for the subject are separate. 할머니가 오셨어 is casual speech to a close friend while still honouring your grandmother.</Hint>
