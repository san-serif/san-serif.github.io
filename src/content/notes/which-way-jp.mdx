へ and に both mark a destination and are interchangeable with plain movement verbs like 行く and 来る. に does extra work へ can't: marking exactly where an action lands, not just the general direction of travel.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="昨日[きのう]上野公園[うえのこうえん]へ行[い]きました。" romaji="Kinou Ueno kouen e ikimashita." english="I went to Ueno Park yesterday." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="来週[らいしゅう]アメリカのうちへ帰[かえ]ります。" romaji="Raishuu amerika no uchi e kaerimasu." english="I'm going back to my house in America next week." />

<Hint>With 行く and 帰る, へ and に are genuinely swappable, either reads naturally and means the same thing. へ leans slightly more formal or written; に is what you'll hear more in speech.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="水曜日[すいようび]に成田空港[なりたくうこう]に着[つ]きました。" romaji="Suiyoubi ni Narita kuukou ni tsukimashita." english="I arrived at Narita Airport on Wednesday." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="土曜日[どようび]に新幹線[しんかんせん]に乗[の]りました。" romaji="Doyoubi ni shinkansen ni norimashita." english="I got on the shinkansen on Saturday." />

<Hint>着く (arrive) and 乗る (board) only take に, never へ. Both verbs describe landing precisely on a specific point, not traveling in a general direction, and that's exactly the job へ can't do. The safer default once you're past 行く/来る/帰る is に.</Hint>
