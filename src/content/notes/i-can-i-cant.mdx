-(으)ㄹ 수 있다/없다 attaches to a verb stem the same way -(으)ㄹ 거예요 does: vowel or ㄹ-ending stems take -ㄹ 수, consonant-ending stems take -을 수. 있다/없다 on the end states whether that "way" exists.

<SentenceBlock formal="한국 요리를 만들 수 있습니까?" formalRom="hanguk yorireul mandeul su isseumnikka?" polite="한국 요리를 만들 수 있어요?" politeRom="hanguk yorireul mandeul su isseoyo?" casual="한국 요리를 만들 수 있어?" casualRom="hanguk yorireul mandeul su isseo?" english="Can you make Korean food?" />

<Hint>수 literally means "a way" or "a method." -(으)ㄹ 수 있다/없다 reads as "there is/isn't a way to do this," a broader statement about capability. 못 (I Can't, Not Won't) is punchier and tied to one specific moment: 수영을 못 해요 fits an immediate obstacle, while 수영을 할 수 없어요 can describe a standing inability, like never having learned at all.</Hint>

<Hint>~ㄹ 수 있어요? is the standard way to ask about someone's skills, the Korean equivalent of "can you speak English?" or "can you drive?" It's asking about general ability, not permission or a one-off circumstance.</Hint>
