ことがある attaches after a verb, and what it means depends entirely on the tense in front of it. Dictionary form + ことがある describes something that happens occasionally. Past た-form + ことがある describes something you've experienced at least once.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="時々[ときどき]、夜遅[よるおそ]く、ケーキを食[た]べることがあります。" romaji="Tokidoki, yoru osoku, keeki o taberu koto ga arimasu." english="Sometimes I eat cake late at night." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="週末[しゅうまつ]は、大抵[たいてい]レストランに来[き]ますが、時々[ときどき]うちで料理[りょうり]することもあります。" romaji="Shuumatsu wa, taitei resutoran ni kimasu ga, tokidoki uchi de ryouri suru koto mo arimasu." english="On weekends I usually go to restaurants, but sometimes I cook at home too." />

<Hint>This dictionary-form version pairs naturally with 時々 (sometimes) or 大抵〜が、時々〜 (usually X, but sometimes Y), describing a pattern with occasional exceptions, not a one-off event.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="富士山[ふじさん]に登[のぼ]ったことがありません。" romaji="Fujisan ni nobotta koto ga arimasen." english="I've never climbed Mt. Fuji." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="日本[にほん]の旅館[りょかん]に泊[と]まったことがあります。" romaji="Nihon no ryokan ni tomatta koto ga arimasu." english="I've stayed at a Japanese inn before." />

<Hint>The past-tense version names a life experience, ever having done something, regardless of when or how often. あります confirms you've done it at least once; ありません says you never have, both completely unrelated to routine or frequency.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="昔[むかし]、祖母[そぼ]は東京[とうきょう]に住[す]んでいたことがあります。" romaji="Mukashi, sobo wa Toukyou ni sunde ita koto ga arimasu." english="My grandmother used to live in Tokyo at one point." />

<Hint>い-adjectives and な-adjectives also take ことがある in the past form to describe something that was once true but no longer is, 髪が短かったことがあります, "her hair used to be short," a useful way to talk about a past state that's since changed.</Hint>
