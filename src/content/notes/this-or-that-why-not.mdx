Your friend keeps weighing two choices: take a course or practise alone? You can describe that indecision, then offer a suggestion with -지 그래요. The suggestion’s tone matters as much as its ending.

### Weigh Two Possibilities

Repeat -(으)ㄹ까 for the alternatives: 갈까 말까, 살까 빌릴까. It often leads into 고민하다 or 망설이다 to say the speaker is considering the options. Use -ㄹ까 after a vowel or ㄹ and -을까 after another consonant.

<SentenceBlock formal="이 책을 살까 빌릴까 망설였습니다." formalRom="i chaegeul salkka billilkka mangseoryeotseumnida." polite="이 책을 살까 빌릴까 망설였어요." politeRom="i chaegeul salkka billilkka mangseoryeosseoyo." casual="이 책을 살까 빌릴까 망설였어." casualRom="i chaegeul salkka billilkka mangseoryeosseo." english="I hesitated over buying this book or borrowing it." />

### Offer a Possible Next Step

Verb stem + -지 그래요? means something like “Why don’t you…?” or “How about…?” In a friendly setting it can offer advice. With a past form, -지 그랬어요? often means “Why didn’t you…?” and can sound reproachful.

<SentenceBlock polite="먼저 짧은 수업을 들어 보지 그래요?" politeRom="meonjeo jjalbeun sueobeul deureo boji geuraeyo?" casual="먼저 짧은 수업을 들어 보지 그래?" casualRom="meonjeo jjalbeun sueobeul deureo boji geurae?" english="Why don’t you try a short class first?" />

<SentenceBlock polite="친구한테 한번 물어보지 그래요?" politeRom="chinguhante hanbeon mureoboji geuraeyo?" casual="친구한테 한번 물어보지 그래?" casualRom="chinguhante hanbeon mureoboji geurae?" english="Why don’t you ask a friend?" />

<Hint type="hint">Use -지 그래요? with care when the listener has already struggled with the problem. [해 보는 게 어때요?](/notes/how-about-this) can offer the same practical step with a different conversational feel.</Hint>
