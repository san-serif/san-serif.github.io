A verb's stem, the part right before ます, followed by に and then 行く, 来る, or 帰る, names the purpose behind the trip, "go/come/return home in order to X."

<JSentenceBlock furigana="喫茶店[きっさてん]へコーヒーを飲[の]みに行[い]きます。" romaji="Kissaten e koohii o nomi ni ikimasu." english="I'm going to the coffee shop to drink coffee." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="家[いえ]へ忘[わす]れ物[もの]を取[と]りに帰[かえ]ります。" romaji="Ie e wasuremono o tori ni kaerimasu." english="I'm going home to get something I forgot." />

<Hint>飲む becomes 飲みに, 取る becomes 取りに, attaching to the same verb stem you've used for たい, たがる, and すぎる. The destination takes へ or に as usual; the purpose sits right before 行く/来る/帰る as its own separate piece.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="デパートへケーキを買[か]いに行[い]く。" romaji="Depaato e keeki o kai ni iku." english="I'm going to the department store to buy cake." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="浅草[あさくさ]へお寺[てら]を見[み]に行[い]く。" romaji="Asakusa e otera o mi ni iku." english="I'm going to Asakusa to see the temple." />

<Hint>する-verbs drop する entirely before に, leaving just the noun, カラオケをしに行きます can be shortened to カラオケしに行きます in casual speech, though both are used. This pattern is extremely common for describing errands and outings, one of the most natural ways to explain why you're headed somewhere.</Hint>
