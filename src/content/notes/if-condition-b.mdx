ば attaches to a verb or adjective by swapping the final う-sound for an え-sound and adding ば, or to a な-adjective or noun with なら. It states a general, logical condition, "if X, then Y," the most neutral of Japanese's several conditionals.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="たくさん水[みず]を飲[の]めば、早[はや]くよくなりますよ。" romaji="Takusan mizu o nomeba, hayaku yoku narimasu yo." english="If you drink lots of water, you'll get better quickly." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="よく寝[ね]れば、早[はや]くよくなりますよ。" romaji="Yoku nereba, hayaku yoku narimasu yo." english="If you sleep well, you'll get better quickly." />

<Hint>飲む becomes 飲めば, 寝る becomes 寝れば, swapping the final syllable for its え-row equivalent before adding ば. Unlike と, which describes an automatic mechanism, and たら, which leans toward one-time or hypothetical events, ば sounds the most like a general rule or piece of advice, closer to textbook logic than to a specific situation.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="天気[てんき]がよければ、行[い]きたいです。" romaji="Tenki ga yokereba, ikitai desu." english="If the weather's good, I want to go." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="家賃[やちん]が安[やす]ければ借[か]ります。" romaji="Yachin ga yasukereba karimasu." english="If the rent is cheap, I'll rent it." />

<Hint>い-adjectives drop the final い and add ければ, 安い becomes 安ければ. な-adjectives and nouns use なら instead of ば, 便利なら, which you already met as its own pattern, if that's the case.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="フラッシュカードを作[つく]れば、漢字[かんじ]が覚[おぼ]えられると思[おも]いますよ。" romaji="Furasshukaado o tsukureba, kanji ga oboerareru to omoimasu yo." english="If you make flashcards, I think you'll be able to remember the kanji." />

<Hint>ば pairs naturally with advice or a recommendation, especially followed by と思います, spelling out a strategy and its expected payoff, a common structure when someone asks how to accomplish something.</Hint>
