You’re inviting a friend to lunch: 시간이 있으면 같이 점심 먹어요. Attach -(으)면 to set up an “if” condition; with a regular or expected event, it can also mean “when.”

<SentenceBlock formal="시간이 있으면 같이 점심을 먹습니다." formalRom="sigani isseumyeon gachi jeomsimeul meokseumnida." polite="시간이 있으면 같이 점심을 먹어요." politeRom="sigani isseumyeon gachi jeomsimeul meogeoyo." casual="시간이 있으면 같이 점심을 먹어." casualRom="sigani isseumyeon gachi jeomsimeul meogeo." english="If you have time, let's have lunch together." />

<SentenceBlock formal="날씨가 좋으면 산에 갑니다." formalRom="nalssiga joeumyeon sane gamnida." polite="날씨가 좋으면 산에 가요." politeRom="nalssiga joeumyeon sane gayo." casual="날씨가 좋으면 산에 가." casualRom="nalssiga joeumyeon sane ga." english="If the weather is good, I go to the mountain." />

<Hint type="hint">When the stem ends in ㄹ, attach -면 directly: 만들다 becomes 만들면, not 만들으면. Some irregular stems also change before the ending: 춥다 becomes 추우면, following the ㅂ-irregular pattern.</Hint>

<SentenceBlock formal="수업이 끝나면 친구하고 밥을 먹을 겁니다." formalRom="sueobi kkeunnamyeon chinguhago babeul meogeul geomnida." polite="수업이 끝나면 친구하고 밥을 먹을 거예요." politeRom="sueobi kkeunnamyeon chinguhago babeul meogeul geoyeyo." casual="수업이 끝나면 친구하고 밥을 먹을 거야." casualRom="sueobi kkeunnamyeon chinguhago babeul meogeul geoya." english="When class ends, I'll eat with a friend." />

<Hint type="insight">In real life, -(으)면 often introduces a routine as well as a one-time possibility. 단 음식을 먹으면 기분이 좋아요 can mean “When I eat sweet food, I feel good,” describing a usual result rather than a plan for one particular time.</Hint>
