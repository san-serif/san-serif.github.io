A verb's stem, the part right before ます, attaches straight to 行く or 来る to mean "go to [verb]" or "come to [verb]." The purpose of the trip travels with the sentence in one breath, no separate clause needed.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="水曜日[すいようび]の朝[あさ]、空港[くうこう]に迎[むか]えに行[い]きます。" romaji="Suiyoubi no asa, kuukou ni mukae ni ikimasu." english="I'll go to the airport to meet them on Wednesday morning." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="土曜日[どようび]に一緒[いっしょ]に寿司[すし]を食[た]べに行[い]きます。" romaji="Doyoubi ni issho ni sushi o tabe ni ikimasu." english="I'll go eat sushi together on Saturday." />

<Hint>迎える (to go meet, pick up) becomes 迎えに, 食べる becomes 食べに, the pattern is always [stem] + に + [行く/来る]. This is the same shape as Here to Eat's -으러 가요 in Korean, a verb stem fused directly onto a travel verb to state the destination and the reason together.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="水曜日[すいようび]に両親[りょうしん]が日本[にほん]に来[き]ます。" romaji="Suiyoubi ni ryoushin ga nihon ni kimasu." english="My parents are coming to Japan on Wednesday." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="金曜日[きんようび]に私[わたし]のアパートへ晩[ばん]ご飯[はん]を食[た]べに来[き]ませんか。" romaji="Kinyoubi ni watashi no apaato e bangohan o tabe ni kimasenka." english="Won't you come eat dinner at my apartment on Friday?" />

<Hint>行く and 来る aren't interchangeable, they're chosen by whether the destination is away from the speaker or toward them, the exact same logic that splits あげる from くれる in Give and Take. Someone traveling to where you already are always takes 来る, never 行く, regardless of how far they're coming from.</Hint>
