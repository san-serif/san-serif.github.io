You want to tell your friend who bought the fruit: 我买水果. Chinese usually puts the person first, the action next, and the thing acted on after the verb. That subject–verb–object order also gives you a useful starting point for longer sentences; a description can fill the predicate slot too.

### Examples

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我买水果。" traditional="我買水果。" pinyin="Wǒ mǎi shuǐguǒ." jyutping="ngo5 maai5 seoi2 gwo2." english="I buy fruit." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="妹妹喜欢音乐。" traditional="妹妹喜歡音樂。" pinyin="Mèimei xǐhuan yīnyuè." jyutping="mui6 mui6 hei2 fun1 jam1 ngok6." english="My younger sister likes music." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="天气很好。" traditional="天氣很好。" pinyin="Tiānqì hěn hǎo." jyutping="tin1 hei3 han2 hou2." english="The weather is very nice." />

<Hint type="hint">The object normally follows the verb: 我看书. Keep the basic subject–predicate order even when you add time or place information.</Hint>
