けれども attaches to the plain form and connects two clauses in contrast, "but." It works exactly like the softening が you've already met, just a notch more formal, and often gets shortened to けど in casual speech.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="コーヒーを飲[の]んだけれども、まだ眠[ねむ]いです。" romaji="Koohii o nonda keredomo, mada nemui desu." english="I drank coffee, but I'm still sleepy." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="ゴルフは上手[じょうず]じゃないけれども、よくします。" romaji="Gorufu wa jouzu janai keredomo, yoku shimasu." english="I'm not good at golf, but I play often." />

<Hint>が, けれども, and けど all do the same job at different registers, けれども reads as the most formal, suited to writing or careful speech, while けど is what you'll hear constantly in casual conversation.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="先生[せんせい]、今日[きょう]の宿題[しゅくだい]のことなんですけど、今[いま]、よろしいでしょうか。" romaji="Sensei, kyou no shukudai no koto nan desu kedo, ima, yoroshii deshou ka." english="Sensei, it's about today's homework, would now be alright?" />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="あのう、金沢[かなざわ]はいい所[ところ]だと聞[き]いたんですけど、本当[ほんとう]ですか。" romaji="Anou, Kanazawa wa ii tokoro da to kiitan desu kedo, hontou desu ka." english="Um, I heard Kanazawa is a nice place, is that true?" />

<Hint>んですけど, built on the explanatory ん you already know, doesn't always contrast anything, it just softens the opening of a request or a question, easing into the topic rather than announcing it bluntly.</Hint>
