のに attaches to the plain form and connects two clauses that don't line up the way you'd expect, "even though X, Y," usually carrying a note of surprise, disappointment, or complaint.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="日本[にほん]に住[す]んでいるのに、日本語[にほんご]がなかなか上手[じょうず]になりません。" romaji="Nihon ni sunde iru noni, nihongo ga nakanaka jouzu ni narimasen." english="Even though I live in Japan, my Japanese isn't getting any better." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="日本人[にほんじん]の友達[ともだち]が多[おお]いのに、日本語[にほんご]がなかなか上手[じょうず]になりません。" romaji="Nihonjin no tomodachi ga ooi noni, nihongo ga nakanaka jouzu ni narimasen." english="Even though I have a lot of Japanese friends, my Japanese isn't getting any better." />

<Hint>から and ので both give a reason that leads naturally to the result. のに does the opposite, it announces that the result contradicts what the first clause would lead you to expect, which is why it so often reads as mild frustration.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="母[はは]は暇[ひま]なのに、いつも「忙[いそが]しい」と言[い]っています。" romaji="Haha wa hima na noni, itsumo 'isogashii' to itte imasu." english="Even though my mother is free, she's always saying 'I'm busy.'" />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="予約[よやく]しておいたのに、すぐに座[すわ]れませんでした。" romaji="Yoyaku shite oita noni, sugu ni suwaremasen deshita." english="Even though I'd made a reservation, we couldn't sit down right away." />

<Hint>な-adjectives and nouns insert な before のに, just like before ので, 暇なのに, 先生なのに, while い-adjectives and verbs attach directly with no change. Pay attention to which one a sentence needs, mixing up ので and のに swaps a reason for a contradiction and can flip the meaning entirely.</Hint>
