Some steps must happen before the next one can begin; others interrupt a journey or mark the result of a long discussion. Japanese uses different forms to place each event along that timeline.

すると can mean “then” or “when that happened,” introducing the natural next event. いきなり means suddenly, with little warning. て初めて marks something that became clear or possible only after an experience; 次第に means gradually, while 次第です gives a formal explanation of how a situation came about. 途中で marks the middle of a process, and ところへ presents an event arriving at just that moment. いよいよ signals that an anticipated moment has finally arrived; 末に describes the result reached after a long process. につけ means “whenever” or “each time,” often linking repeated occasions with a reaction. にわたって gives the span or duration over which something continues.

These patterns locate an action within a process. てからでないと means “not until X” and makes X a prerequisite. 途中で / 途中に refer to the middle of a journey, activity, or process. ところに / ところへ and かと思うと describe another event arriving just when something is happening. いよいよ marks an anticipated moment finally arriving or a situation reaching a decisive point. た末 / の末 presents a result reached after a long process or much thought. につけ means “whenever,” while にわたって marks a span across time, area, or range.

つつ can mean “while doing,” usually in concise formal writing: 働きつつ学ぶ, “study while working.”

て初めて means that only after an experience or event does the following result become possible or understood. 次第 can mean “as soon as” in formal instructions: 到着次第ご連絡ください, “please contact us as soon as you arrive.” 直ちに is a more general “immediately,” often used in official directions.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="結果[けっか]を確認[かくにん]してからでないと、次[つぎ]の手続[てつづ]きに進[すす]めません。" romaji="Kekka o kakunin shite kara de nai to, tsugi no tetsuzuki ni susumemasen." english="We can't move to the next procedure until we've checked the result." />

てからでないと is followed by a negative possibility or restriction. It says the first action must be completed before the next one can happen.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="旅行[りょこう]の途中[とちゅう]で、古[ふる]い友達[ともだち]に会[あ]いました。" romaji="Ryokou no tochuu de, furui tomodachi ni aimashita." english="I met an old friend in the middle of my trip." />

途中で focuses on an event during a process; 途中に can mark a point in the middle, often with a more specific location or moment. ところに / ところへ means “just as / right when,” often when something or someone arrives unexpectedly.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="長[なが]い話[はな]し合[あ]いの末[すえ]、計画[けいかく]を変更[へんこう]することにしました。" romaji="Nagai hanashiai no sue, keikaku o henkou suru koto ni shimashita." english="After a long discussion, we decided to change the plan." />

末 describes a result that came after a process or effort. Verb past form + 末 and noun + の末 often imply that time or deliberation led to a final outcome.

<Hint type="insight">A guide might say 予約してからでないと入れません, “you can't enter until you have a reservation.” In a story, ところへ can add a sudden interruption, while 末 summarizes the outcome after the whole process.</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">Prerequisite: Vてからでないと. During a process: 途中で / 途中に. Just as an event occurs: ところに / ところへ. Long-awaited turning point: いよいよ. Final result after a process: Vた末 / Nの末.</Hint>
