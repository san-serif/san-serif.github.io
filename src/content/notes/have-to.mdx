Conjugate the verb into its -아요/-어요 form the way you already know, then swap 요 for 야 돼요. 가다 → 가요 → 가야 돼요.

<SentenceBlock formal="병원에 가야 됩니다." formalRom="byeongwone gaya doemnida." polite="병원에 가야 돼요." politeRom="byeongwone gaya dwaeyo." casual="병원에 가야 돼." casualRom="byeongwone gaya dwae." english="I have to go to the hospital." />

<SentenceBlock formal="공부해야 됩니다." formalRom="gongbuhaeya doemnida." polite="공부해야 돼요." politeRom="gongbuhaeya dwaeyo." casual="공부해야 돼." casualRom="gongbuhaeya dwae." english="I must study." />

<Hint>돼요 comes from 되다, "to become" or "to be okay." -아/어야 돼요 literally reads as "it only becomes okay if...", which is exactly why the construction implies obligation. You'll also hear -아/어야 해요, built on 하다 instead, meaning almost exactly the same thing.</Hint>

<SentenceBlock formal="선물을 사야 됩니다." formalRom="seonmureul saya doemnida." polite="선물을 사야 돼요." politeRom="seonmureul saya dwaeyo." casual="선물을 사야 돼." casualRom="seonmureul saya dwae." english="I have to buy a gift." />

<SentenceBlock formal="물을 많이 마셔야 됩니다." formalRom="mureul mani masyeoya doemnida." polite="물을 많이 마셔야 돼요." politeRom="mureul mani masyeoya dwaeyo." casual="물을 많이 마셔야 돼." casualRom="mureul mani masyeoya dwae." english="You need to drink a lot of water." />

<SentenceBlock formal="비자가 있어야 됩니다." formalRom="bijaga isseoya doemnida." polite="비자가 있어야 돼요." politeRom="bijaga isseoya dwaeyo." casual="비자가 있어야 돼." casualRom="bijaga isseoya dwae." english="You must have a visa." />

<Hint>감기에 걸리다 ("to catch a cold," literally "to get caught by a cold") is worth learning as one fixed chunk rather than building it word by word. A surprising number of illness expressions in Korean work this way, describing symptoms as things that happen to you rather than states you're simply in.</Hint>
