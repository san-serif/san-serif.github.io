You both watch a programme, but your friend seems less enthusiastic. A negative question invites agreement. If you want to insist how good something is, 얼마나 with an emphatic -다고요 adds force to your evaluation.

### Invite the Listener’s Agreement

Verb or adjective stem + -지 않아요? can ask whether the listener shares a view: 재미있지 않아요? With a noun use -(이)지 않아요? Context and intonation decide whether it seeks agreement or is a literal negative question.

<SentenceBlock polite="이 프로그램은 정말 재미있지 않아요?" politeRom="i peurogeuraemeun jeongmal jaemiitji anayo?" casual="이 프로그램은 정말 재미있지 않아?" casualRom="i peurogeuraemeun jeongmal jaemiitji ana?" english="Isn’t this programme really interesting?" />

<SentenceBlock polite="두 사람의 목소리가 비슷하지 않아요?" politeRom="du saramui moksoriga biseuthaji anayo?" casual="두 사람의 목소리가 비슷하지 않아?" casualRom="du saramui moksoriga biseuthaji ana?" english="Don’t their voices sound similar?" />

### Insist on the Degree

얼마나 plus a statement-shaped -다고요 can emphasise how much or how strongly something is true. Present action verbs use -ㄴ다고요/-는다고요; adjectives use -다고요. The speaker is stressing their own claim here, rather than necessarily reporting someone else’s words.

<SentenceBlock polite="이 가수가 노래를 얼마나 잘한다고요!" politeRom="i gasuga noraereul eolmana jalhandagoyo!" casual="이 가수가 노래를 얼마나 잘한다고!" casualRom="i gasuga noraereul eolmana jalhandago!" english="You have no idea how well this singer sings!" />

<SentenceBlock polite="마지막 장면이 얼마나 감동적이었다고요!" politeRom="majimak jangmyeoni eolmana gamdongjeogieotdagoyo!" casual="마지막 장면이 얼마나 감동적이었다고!" casualRom="majimak jangmyeoni eolmana gamdongjeogieotdago!" english="The final scene was so moving!" />

<Hint type="hint">A plain 얼마나 재미있어요? can ask how interesting it is. The emphatic ending and intonation turn 얼마나 재미있다고요! into an insistence on the degree. Use this expressive style where enthusiasm or correction fits the conversation.</Hint>
