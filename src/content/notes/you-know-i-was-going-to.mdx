Your laptop has broken again. A friend reminds you about the repair shop nearby, but you were about to buy a new one. -잖아요 appeals to information the listener can recognise; -(으)려다가 introduces a plan that changed.

### Remind or Correct

Attach -잖아요 to the stem: 알잖아요, 좋잖아요. Nouns take -(이)잖아요. Past forms work too: 말했잖아요. It treats the information as something shared, recoverable, or worth reminding the listener of.

<SentenceBlock polite="역 앞에 수리점이 있잖아요." politeRom="yeok ape surijeomi itjanayo." casual="역 앞에 수리점이 있잖아." casualRom="yeok ape surijeomi itjana." english="There’s a repair shop in front of the station, remember?" />

<SentenceBlock polite="오늘은 일요일이잖아요." politeRom="oneureun iryoirijanayo." casual="오늘은 일요일이잖아." casualRom="oneureun iryoirijana." english="It’s Sunday today, you know." />

### An Intention Gets Redirected

Use -려다가 after a vowel or ㄹ and -으려다가 after another consonant: 사려다가, 만들려다가, 먹으려다가. You intended or were about to do the first action, then abandoned or changed it.

<SentenceBlock polite="새 노트북을 사려다가 수리했어요." politeRom="sae noteubugeul saryeodaga surihaesseoyo." casual="새 노트북을 사려다가 수리했어." casualRom="sae noteubugeul saryeodaga surihaesseo." english="I was going to buy a new laptop, but had it repaired instead." />

<SentenceBlock polite="친구에게 전화하려다가 메시지를 보냈어요." politeRom="chinguege jeonhwaharyeodaga mesijireul bonaesseoyo." casual="친구에게 전화하려다가 메시지를 보냈어." casualRom="chinguege jeonhwaharyeodaga mesijireul bonaesseo." english="I was going to call my friend, but sent a message instead." />

<Hint type="hint">[-다가](/notes/on-the-way-helping-out) interrupts an action already under way. -려다가 can abandon an intention before the action happens. Also watch your tone with -잖아요: a reminder can sound impatient when delivered sharply.</Hint>
