You want to say you can read kanji: 漢字を読むことができます. Put ことができる after a verb’s dictionary form to express ability. The verb stays in that form; ことができる carries the “can” meaning.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="妹[いもうと]は服[ふく]を作[つく]ることができます。" romaji="Imouto wa fuku o tsukuru koto ga dekimasu." english="My younger sister can make clothes." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="妹[いもうと]は辛[から]いものを食[た]べることができません。" romaji="Imouto wa karai mono o taberu koto ga dekimasen." english="My younger sister can't eat spicy food." />

<Hint type="insight">Many verbs also have a shorter potential form, 食べられる instead of 食べることができる, and both mean the same thing. ことができる sounds a little more formal and explicit, useful in writing or careful speech, while the potential form is what you'll hear more in casual conversation.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="ホテルで寿司[すし]を食[た]べることができますか。" romaji="Hoteru de sushi o taberu koto ga dekimasu ka." english="Is it possible to eat sushi at the hotel?" />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="夜[よる]もプールを使[つか]うことができますか。" romaji="Yoru mo puuru o tsukau koto ga dekimasu ka." english="Is it possible to use the pool at night too?" />

<Hint type="insight">Asked as a question like this, ことができますか often works less like "are you personally able to" and more like "is it possible to," checking whether a place or situation allows something, common when asking about hotel or facility rules.</Hint>
