まま attaches after a verb's past た-form, an adjective, or a noun with の, and describes a state left unchanged, "as is," "while still," often implying it stayed that way longer than expected.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="窓[まど]を開[あ]けたまま、寝[ね]てしまいました。" romaji="Mado o aketa mama, nete shimaimashita." english="I fell asleep with the window left open." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="スーツを着[き]たまま、寝[ね]てしまいました。" romaji="Suutsu o kita mama, nete shimaimashita." english="I fell asleep still wearing my suit." />

<Hint>Xたまま describes doing one thing (falling asleep) while a first state (the window open, the suit worn) stays exactly as it was, unresolved in the background. This overlaps with てある in describing a leftover state, but まま carries no sense of deliberate purpose, only that something didn't get changed back.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="いいえ、汚[きたな]いままです。" romaji="Iie, kitanai mama desu." english="No, it's still dirty, unchanged." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="いいえ、壊[こわ]れたままです。" romaji="Iie, kowareta mama desu." english="No, it's still broken." />

<Hint>まま answering a question like this reports that nothing has changed since some earlier point, still dirty, still broken, a state that was expected to improve but hasn't, similar in feel to まだ but framed around an unbroken continuation rather than a simple "still."</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="このまま出[だ]しておいてください。" romaji="Kono mama dashite oite kudasai." english="Please just leave it out as is." />

<Hint>このまま and そのまま are extremely common set phrases, "as it is," "just like that," used constantly in instructions when you want something left untouched rather than adjusted or moved.</Hint>
