言[い]うまでもない means “it goes without saying” or “needless to say.” There are two common shapes: a complete idea + のは言うまでもない states that the idea is obvious; a noun + は言うまでもなく adds that obvious example before mentioning another one.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="彼[かれ]が優勝[ゆうしょう]するのは言[い]うまでもありません。" romaji="Kare ga yuushou suru no wa iu made mo arimasen." english="It goes without saying that he'll win." />

With a verb or adjective clause, の nominalizes the idea before は言うまでもない. In conversation, 言うまでもないですが or 言うまでもありませんが can introduce a point the listener is expected to accept.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="健康[けんこう]は言[い]うまでもなく、休息[きゅうそく]も大切[たいせつ]です。" romaji="Kenkou wa iu made mo naku, kyuusoku mo taisetsu desu." english="Health goes without saying; rest is important too." />

は言うまでもなく often works like “not to mention” or “of course …, and … too.” The first noun is treated as an obvious case, then the sentence adds something else that also matters. Use this carefully in conversation: saying something is “obvious” can sound dismissive if the listener may not agree.

<Hint type="insight">In a presentation or report, this phrase can move quickly past shared background: 安全は言うまでもなく、使いやすさも重視しました, “Safety goes without saying; we also prioritized ease of use.” For a softer, less emphatic addition, use もちろん or だけでなく.</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">Remember the two shapes: [whole idea] + のは言うまでもない = “it goes without saying that…”; [noun] + は言うまでもなく + [another point] = “not to mention [noun], [the next thing] too.”</Hint>
