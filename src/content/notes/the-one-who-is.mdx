の, the nominalizer you already know, wraps a whole clause into "the one who," and paired with は〜だ at the end, it singles out exactly who or what the description points to, "the one who does X is Y."

<JSentenceBlock furigana="お酒[さけ]を飲[の]むのは大輔[だいすけ]さんです。" romaji="Osake o nomu no wa Daisuke-san desu." english="The one who drinks is Daisuke." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="ドイツ語[ご]を勉強[べんきょう]しているのは沙耶[さや]さんです。" romaji="Doitsugo o benkyou shite iru no wa Saya-san desu." english="The one studying German is Saya." />

<Hint>お酒を飲むのは wraps "the one who drinks alcohol" into a single noun phrase acting as the topic, with だ/です at the end naming exactly who fits it. This flips the normal order, putting the description first and the identity last, which throws extra emphasis onto whoever or whatever answers it.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="今[いま]住[す]んでいるのは東京[とうきょう]です。" romaji="Ima sunde iru no wa Toukyou desu." english="Where I'm living now is Tokyo." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="仕事[しごと]に必要[ひつよう]なのはコミュニケーションです。" romaji="Shigoto ni hitsuyou na no wa komyunikeeshon desu." english="What's necessary for the job is communication." />

<Hint>This pattern works just as well with a place, a time, or an abstract need as the answer, not only a person, 住んでいるのは東京, "the place I live is Tokyo," 必要なのはコミュニケーション, "what's needed is communication," always structured as [description] のは [answer] です.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="一番[いちばん]大変[たいへん]だったのはどんなことですか。" romaji="Ichiban taihen datta no wa donna koto desu ka." english="What was the hardest part?" />

<Hint>Turned into a question by swapping the answer for a question word, のは〜ですか asks the listener to fill in exactly who or what fits the description, a natural way to ask "which one was it" once the description itself is already clear.</Hint>
