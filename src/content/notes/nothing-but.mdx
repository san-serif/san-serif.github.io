しか and ばかり both narrow things down like だけ, which you already know, but each adds its own flavor. しか always pairs with a negative verb to state the same limit from the other direction. ばかり adds judgment, usually complaining that something happens too much.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="テレビしかありません。" romaji="Terebi shika arimasen." english="There's nothing but a TV." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="兄[あに]としか話[はな]しません。" romaji="Ani to shika hanashimasen." english="I only talk to my older brother (and no one else)." />

<Hint>しか always demands a negative verb right after it, テレビしかあります is simply wrong, and the negative is what carries the "nothing but" meaning, not しか by itself. It tends to land with more emphasis than だけ, closer to "nothing else at all."</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="弟[おとうと]は、毎日[まいにち]お菓子[かし]ばかり食[た]べています。" romaji="Otouto wa, mainichi okashi bakari tabete imasu." english="My younger brother eats nothing but sweets every day." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="実[じつ]は、先週[せんしゅう]始[はじ]めたばかりなんです。" romaji="Jitsu wa, senshuu hajimeta bakari nan desu." english="Actually, I only just started last week." />

<Hint>ばかり attached to a noun, お菓子ばかり, means "nothing but sweets," usually with a hint of disapproval. Attached to a verb's past-tense た-form instead, たばかり means something completely different, "just did it a moment ago," so watch which form comes right before ばかり.</Hint>
