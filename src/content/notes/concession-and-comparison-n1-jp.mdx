These advanced connectors concede a point while keeping the main claim in place. といえども means “even though one is,” often against an expected standard; とはいえ acknowledges a qualification before returning to the main point; ならまだしも says one case might be acceptable, but another is not. たところで says that even if an action happens, the result will not change. Each pattern narrows what the concession allows.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="専門家[せんもんか]といえども、間違[まちが]えることはあります。" romaji="Senmonka to iedomo, machigaeru koto wa arimasu." english="Even experts make mistakes." />

といえども follows a noun or plain form and emphasizes that the statement remains true despite the status or circumstances just mentioned. It is formal and often appears in writing or speeches.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="準備[じゅんび]はできました。とはいえ、出発[しゅっぱつ]は明日[あした]にしましょう。" romaji="Junbi wa dekimashita. To wa ie, shuppatsu wa ashita ni shimashou." english="We're ready. That said, let's leave tomorrow." />

とはいえ concedes the preceding fact, then introduces a qualification. ならまだしも compares cases: “A would be one thing, but B is different.” たところで often appears with a negative or dismissive result: 今さら急いだところで間に合わない, “Even if we hurry now, we won't make it.”

<Hint type="insight">A speaker can acknowledge that a plan is possible with できるとはいえ, then explain the limits. ならまだしも makes the contrast sharper: a short delay might be acceptable, but a full cancellation is a different matter.</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">といえども concedes despite a category or status; とはいえ qualifies a previous statement; ならまだしも says one case is tolerable but another is not; たところで says the hypothetical action would not help.</Hint>
