These expressions both deal with reasons, but do different jobs. Put a question word + かというと before an explanation to answer an implied “why?” Use a statement + という理由で to identify the reason being given for a decision or result.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="なぜ人気[にんき]かというと、地元[じもと]の野菜[やさい]を使[つか]っているからです。" romaji="Naze ninki ka to iu to, jimoto no yasai o tsukatte iru kara desu." english="The reason it's popular is that it uses local vegetables." />

なぜかというと and どうしてかというと commonly lead into an explanation, often ending in からです or ためです. The phrase signals that the speaker is about to make the reason explicit.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="問い合わせ[といあわせ]が多[おお]かったという理由[りゆう]で、説明会[せつめいかい]が開[ひら]かれました。" romaji="Toiawase ga ookatta to iu riyuu de, setsumeikai ga hirakaremashita." english="An information session was held because there had been many inquiries." />

という理由で packages a preceding fact as the reason for an outcome. It is useful when explaining or reporting the grounds for a decision; it can sound as though the reason is being cited rather than independently endorsed. For a plain causal explanation without that framing, use から or ので, covered in the earlier lessons.

どちらかというと uses the same “if you ask me to choose” shape to give a soft preference: “if anything, I’d say…” It usually introduces a tendency rather than a strong either-or decision.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="辛[から]い料理[りょうり]と甘[あま]い料理[りょうり]なら、どちらかというと辛[から]い料理[りょうり]が好[す]きです。" romaji="Karai ryouri to amai ryouri nara, dochira ka to iu to karai ryouri ga suki desu." english="Between spicy and sweet food, if anything, I prefer spicy food." />

<Hint type="insight">In a meeting, なぜこの方法にしたかというと… gives you a clear lead-in before explaining your choice. In a notice or report, 予算不足という理由で延期します states the reason directly and formally.</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">なぜかというと opens an explanation: “why? because…” A clause + という理由で labels a stated reason: “for the reason that…” どちらかというと is a separate conversational use that softens a preference to “if anything, I’d say…”</Hint>
