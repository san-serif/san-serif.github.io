You take the shortcut expecting to arrive early, then roadworks make the trip longer. それなのに flags the mismatch; 逆に turns the direction around, while それにしても adds your reaction.

These connectors relate a new statement to what came before. 逆に means “conversely” or “on the contrary,” and can also describe an unexpected reversal. それなのに says “despite that,” emphasizing that the result conflicts with the prior fact. に反して marks a result contrary to an expectation or rule. 却って means “instead / on the contrary,” often describing an unexpected reversal. むしろ means “rather,” correcting which of two descriptions is more accurate. ものの, 反面, つつも, and にもかかわらず all concede one fact before giving a contrast; にもかかわらず is the most formal and emphatic. なお can mean “still / even so,” maintaining a point despite a prior statement. それにしても expresses the speaker's reaction (“even so,” “that said”), often with surprise at the degree of something.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="近道[ちかみち]のはずでした。それなのに、工事[こうじ]でいつもより時間[じかん]がかかりました。" romaji="Chikamichi no hazu deshita. Sore na noni, kouji de itsumo yori jikan ga kakarimashita." english="It was supposed to be a shortcut. Even so, construction made it take longer than usual." />

それなのに points out a result that goes against the expectation created by the previous sentence. It often carries disappointment or frustration.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="雨[あめ]が強[つよ]くなったので、逆[ぎゃく]に人[ひと]が少[すく]なくなりました。" romaji="Ame ga tsuyoku natta node, gyaku ni hito ga sukunaku narimashita." english="As the rain got heavier, the number of people actually decreased." />

逆に marks an opposite direction or an outcome that is contrary to what one might expect. It can also correct a prior perspective: 逆に考える, “think about it the other way around.”

<JSentenceBlock furigana="それにしても、今日[きょう]はずいぶん暑[あつ]いですね。" romaji="Sore ni shite mo, kyou wa zuibun atsui desu ne." english="Even so, it sure is hot today." />

それにしても often introduces the speaker's reaction after acknowledging context. Unlike それなのに, it does not necessarily describe a contradiction; it can simply signal that something is notable despite what came before.

ことは～が acknowledges one fact before setting up a contrasting limitation: 便利なことは便利だが、値段が高い, “it is convenient, but it is expensive.” This gives a balanced concession without the strong surprise of それなのに.

<Hint type="insight">Use それなのに when a result conflicts with an expectation. Use 逆に to state an opposite direction or unexpected effect. Use それにしても to comment on something that still stands out, even after considering the circumstances.</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">Opposite direction or contrary effect: 逆に. Despite the prior fact: それなのに. “Even so / that sure is…” reaction: それにしても.</Hint>
