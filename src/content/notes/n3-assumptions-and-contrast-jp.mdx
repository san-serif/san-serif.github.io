These patterns invite the listener to consider a premise or sharpen a contrast. としたら / とすれば / とすると mean “if we assume that,” and set up a result or judgment. 万が一 introduces a very unlikely contingency (“if by some chance…”). ものなら means “if one could,” often implying the action is difficult or unreal. ようでは / ようじゃ describe a condition that would lead to an undesirable consequence if it continues. ては can set up a negative result if a condition is repeated or allowed to continue. にしても means “even if” or “even considering,” often with a limit or surprise. ながらも concedes one fact while introducing a contrast. くせに criticizes someone or something for acting contrary to an expectation. どころか means “far from” or “let alone,” correcting an earlier impression with a stronger reality.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="その話[はなし]が本当[ほんとう]だとしたら、驚[おどろ]きです。" romaji="Sono hanashi ga hontou da to shitara, odoroki desu." english="If that story is true, it's surprising." />

としたら, とすれば, and とすると all introduce a hypothetical premise. としたら often sounds conversational, while とすれば can sound more analytical; in many contexts they overlap.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="忙[いそが]しいにしても、連絡[れんらく]ぐらいはできるはずです。" romaji="Isogashii ni shite mo, renraku gurai wa dekiru hazu desu." english="Even if you're busy, you should at least be able to get in touch." />

にしても acknowledges a condition but says it does not fully excuse or change the conclusion. くせに often adds criticism, whereas ながらも is a more neutral or formal concession: 不便ながらも使いやすい, “although inconvenient, it's easy to use.”

<JSentenceBlock furigana="高[たか]いどころか、今[いま]は割引[わりびき]されています。" romaji="Takai dokoro ka, ima wa waribiki sarete imasu." english="Far from being expensive, it's discounted right now." />

どころか rejects the first assumption and presents a stronger or opposite reality. It can also mean “let alone”: 名前どころか顔も知らない, “I don't even know their face, let alone their name.”

<Hint type="insight">にしても often signals a standard the speaker still expects to be met. くせに can sound insulting, so avoid it when you want neutral contrast. どころか corrects an assumption more strongly than というより.</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">Assume X: としたら / とすれば / とすると. Even considering X: にしても. Despite X: ながらも. Critical “even though”: くせに. Far from X / let alone X: どころか.</Hint>
