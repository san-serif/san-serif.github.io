You’re meeting a classmate at school. You wave them over: 这儿, “over here.” They point to a bench across the courtyard: 那儿, “over there.” The same near/far choice works for things and groups, too.

### Choosing Between Two Things

<CSentenceBlock simplified="这个是我的，那个是你的。" traditional="這個是我的，那個是你的。" pinyin="Zhège shì wǒ de, nàge shì nǐ de." jyutping="ze5 go3 si6 ngo5 dik1, naa5 go3 si6 nei5 dik1." english="This one is mine; that one is yours." />

When you point to an item without naming it, use 这个 for “this one” and 那个 for “that one.” Put a measure word between 这/那 and a noun: 这本书, 那杯茶. [Two Cups, Three Books](/notes/hsk1-number-and-measure-word-phrases) explains how to choose that measure word.

### Pointing Out a Place

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我们在这儿等，别的同学在那儿。" traditional="我們在這兒等，別的同學在那兒。" pinyin="Wǒmen zài zhèr děng, biéde tóngxué zài nàr." jyutping="ngo5 mun4 zoi6 ze5 ji4 dang2, bit6 dik1 tung4 hok6 zoi6 naa5 ji4." english="We’ll wait here; the other classmates are over there." />

这儿/这里 point to where you are; 那儿/那里 point somewhere else. The 儿 forms are especially common in northern speech. You’ll also hear 这里 and 那里 widely, especially in careful or written language.

### Pointing to a Group

<CSentenceBlock simplified="这些是新书，那些是旧书。" traditional="這些是新書，那些是舊書。" pinyin="Zhèxiē shì xīn shū, nàxiē shì jiù shū." jyutping="ze5 se1 si6 san1 syu1, naa5 se1 si6 gau6 syu1." english="These are new books; those are old books." />

Add 些 to 这 or 那 when you mean “these” or “those.” It does not tell you exactly how many; it just gathers the things you’re pointing at into a group.

<Hint type="hint">When 这 or 那 comes before a counted noun, keep the measure word: 这本书, 那个人. When the noun is understood, 这个 and 那个 can stand alone.</Hint>

<Hint type="insight">Pointing works the same way in English: “this one” is close at hand, while “that one” is farther away. Chinese uses the same contrast for places, then adds 些 to point to a group rather than one item.</Hint>
