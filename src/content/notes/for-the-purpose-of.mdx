<JapaneseLessonHead />

ために attaches after a verb's dictionary form or after a noun with の, and names the purpose or goal behind an action, "in order to," "for the sake of."

<JSentenceBlock furigana="拓也[たくや]さんは、来年[らいねん]、日本[にほん]に留学[りゅうがく]するために日本語[にほんご]を勉強[べんきょう]しています。" romaji="Takuya-san wa, rainen, nihon ni ryuugaku suru tame ni nihongo o benkyou shite imasu." english="Takuya is studying Japanese in order to study abroad in Japan next year." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="直樹[なおき]さんは、日本語[にほんご]の先生[せんせい]になるために日本語[にほんご]を勉強[べんきょう]しています。" romaji="Naoki-san wa, nihongo no sensei ni naru tame ni nihongo o benkyou shite imasu." english="Naoki is studying Japanese in order to become a Japanese teacher." />

<Hint>ために always answers "what for," making the reasoning explicit in a way plain から doesn't. It works with a verb's dictionary form directly, or, when the goal is a thing rather than an action, with a noun plus の, 体のために, "for the sake of your health," 友達のために, "for a friend's sake."</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="これは、頭[あたま]が痛[いた]くなった時[とき]のための薬[くすり]です。" romaji="Kore wa, atama ga itaku natta toki no tame no kusuri desu." english="This is medicine for when your head starts to hurt." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="これは、日本[にほん]の諺[ことわざ]の意味[いみ]を調[しら]べるための本[ほん]です。" romaji="Kore wa, nihon no kotowaza no imi o shiraberu tame no hon desu." english="This is a book for looking up the meaning of Japanese proverbs." />

<Hint>Xのための[noun] describes an item's purpose, "a book for looking things up," "medicine for headaches," stacking ため directly in front of the noun it modifies, a pattern that shows up constantly on labels and product descriptions.</Hint>
