ながら attaches to a verb's stem, the part right before ます, and links two actions happening at the same time, done by the same person. The verb right before ながら is the background action; the verb at the end of the sentence is the main point.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="拓也[たくや]さんは、着替[きが]えながらテレビを見[み]ます。" romaji="Takuya-san wa, kigaenagara terebi o mimasu." english="Takuya watches TV while getting dressed." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="拓也[たくや]さんは、ケータイで話[はな]しながら会社[かいしゃ]に行[い]きます。" romaji="Takuya-san wa, keitai de hanashinagara kaisha ni ikimasu." english="Takuya goes to the office while talking on his phone." />

<Hint>着替える becomes 着替えながら, 話す becomes 話しながら, attaching to the same stem you've used for たい, はじめる, and すぎる. Unlike 間 (which needs ている first), ながら attaches straight to the plain stem with nothing extra in between.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="大抵[たいてい]メールを読[よ]みながらご飯[はん]を食[た]べます。" romaji="Taitei meeru o yominagara gohan o tabemasu." english="I usually eat while reading email." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="大抵[たいてい]音楽[おんがく]を聞[き]きながらお風呂[ふろ]に入[はい]ります。" romaji="Taitei ongaku o kikinagara ofuro ni hairimasu." english="I usually listen to music while taking a bath." />

<Hint>間 marks one action happening inside a longer span, often with different actors, while ながら always means the same person doing both things literally at once, closer together in time than 間 usually implies.</Hint>
