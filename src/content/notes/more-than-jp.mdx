より attaches after a noun and marks the thing being compared against, "more than X." Paired with ほうが on the item that wins the comparison, X のほうがYより [adjective] makes the comparison explicit on both sides.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="飛行機[ひこうき]は電車[でんしゃ]より速[はや]いです。" romaji="Hikouki wa densha yori hayai desu." english="Planes are faster than trains." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="漢字[かんじ]はひらがなより難[むずか]しいです。" romaji="Kanji wa hiragana yori muzukashii desu." english="Kanji are harder than hiragana." />

<Hint type="hint">より alone is enough to make a comparison, X はYより[adjective], with X automatically understood as the winning side. Adding のほうが in front makes the same comparison but places extra emphasis on X, useful when contrasting two options someone's actively choosing between.</Hint>

The pattern “[description] のは X の方だ” also uses の方 to identify which person or thing fits the description, “it's X who/that…” It is especially useful when correcting a choice between two possibilities.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="先[さき]に日本語[にほんご]を勉強[べんきょう]し始[はじ]めたのは、姉[あね]の方[ほう]です。" romaji="Saki ni nihongo o benkyou shi-hajimeta no wa, ane no hou desu." english="My older sister is the one who started studying Japanese first." />

<Hint type="hint">The comparison puts one side ahead; のはXの方だ selects the side that matches the description. In casual speech, the final です may be dropped, but keep the のは phrase attached to the whole description.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="東京[とうきょう]のほうが京都[きょうと]より人口[じんこう]が多[おお]いです。" romaji="Toukyou no hou ga Kyouto yori jinkou ga ooi desu." english="Tokyo has a bigger population than Kyoto." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="日本語[にほんご]を話[はな]すほうが、日本語[にほんご]を書[か]くより楽[たの]しいと思[おも]います。" romaji="Nihongo o hanasu hou ga, nihongo o kaku yori tanoshii to omoimasu." english="I think speaking Japanese is more fun than writing it." />

<Hint type="hint">Both sides of the comparison can be whole verb phrases, not just nouns, 漢字を覚えるほうが、単語を覚えるより大変, "memorizing kanji is harder than memorizing vocabulary," comparing two entire activities rather than two simple things.</Hint>

For a more neutral "compared with," に比[くら]べて puts the comparison itself in focus. It is common in explanations, reviews, and observations where neither side needs to be framed as the winner.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="去年[きょねん]に比[くら]べて、今年[ことし]は雨[あめ]が少[すく]ないです。" romaji="Kyonen ni kurabete, kotoshi wa ame ga sukunai desu." english="Compared with last year, there has been less rain this year." />

<Hint type="hint">より often gives a direct ranking, "A is faster than B." に比べて sets up a point of reference, "compared with B, A..." In casual conversation they can overlap, but に比べて sounds especially natural when describing a change or difference rather than choosing a winner.</Hint>

かなり means “quite / considerably” and gives a strong degree without naming a comparison. ずっと strengthens a comparison to mean “much / far”: X のほうがYよりずっと [adjective].

ほど～ない expresses “not as … as”: AはBほど高くない, “A isn't as expensive as B.” It sets B as the degree or benchmark and says A does not reach that level.

以上に means “more than / beyond” a stated point of comparison: 予想以上に難しかった, “it was more difficult than expected.” With a noun, it can also mean “more than X” in amount or degree.

だけましだ offers consolation by saying a situation is at least better than a worse alternative: 遅れたけれど、連絡があっただけましだ, “we were delayed, but at least they let us know.”

<JSentenceBlock furigana="今年[ことし]は去年[きょねん]よりずっと忙[いそが]しいです。" romaji="Kotoshi wa kyonen yori zutto isogashii desu." english="This year is much busier than last year." />

<Hint type="insight">かなり is useful for a stand-alone assessment, かなり難しいです, “it's quite difficult.” Use ずっと when you have a comparison point, こちらの方がずっと便利です, “this one is much more convenient.”</Hint>
