Frame assumptions explicitly and describe a change, transition, or circumstance with precision. The patterns in this unit frame assumptions and transitions. An explicit premise tells the reader what a decision rests on; temporal and change expressions then show how events develop from that starting point.

### Patterns in this lesson

- **ともあろう** — Of all people, Such,
- **こそすれ〜ない** — And certainly not, But certainly not, But, Although, Even though
- **並み** — As, On par with, Worthy of
- **と来たら** — For …(is the best), When it comes to, Concerning
- **ものとして** — Assuming, Supposing, (Interpret) as, On the assumption, Regarding, Considering
- **を前提に** — On the premise of
- **に限ったことではない** — Not limited to, Not restricted to, Not only
- **とは比べものにならない** — Nothing compared to, Cannot be compared to, Much more, Way better, Above comparison (beyond comparison), Far greater than
- **がん～** — Totally, Seriously, Completely, Furiously, Majorly
- **どうにか** — Somehow (or other)
- **訳あり(訳あって)** — ～ with reasons, Due to reasons, Defective, Problematic, Unique
- **～たはいいが** — It's all well and good but...
- **を顧みず** — Without regard for, Ignoring, Without concern for

<JSentenceBlock furigana="制度[せいど]の変更[へんこう]を前提[ぜんてい]に、準備[じゅんび]を進[すす]めています。" romaji="Seido no henkou o zentei ni, junbi o susumete imasu." english="We are moving preparations forward on the assumption that the system will change." />

を前提に makes an assumption explicit. The writer is using the change as a starting point, without necessarily arguing that it is certain.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="文章[ぶんしょう]の文脈[ぶんみゃく]から、筆者[ひっしゃ]の意図[いと]を読[よ]み取[と]りましょう。" romaji="Bunshou no bunmyaku kara, hissha no ito o yomitorimashou." english="Use the context to identify the writer’s intention." />

When following a long explanation, write down the assumption before tracing the consequences it supports.

<Hint type="insight">A budget discussion may use を前提に to state an assumption before presenting its figures. Treat that premise as the starting point of the calculation, not automatically as a proven fact.</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">Write the premise in a margin note, then trace the decisions that depend on it. This helps untangle long policy and planning sentences.</Hint>
