という sits between a name or a whole clause and the noun it belongs to, "called X" or "the news that Y." It's built from と, the quoting particle you met in と思う, plus 言う, to say, literally "a thing that's called."

<JSentenceBlock furigana="山田[やまだ]京子[きょうこ]という作家[さっか]です。" romaji="Yamada Kyouko to iu sakka desu." english="She's an author named Yamada Kyouko." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="ミッドタウンというデパート" romaji="Middotaun to iu depaato" english="a department store called Midtown" />

<Hint>という is most useful when introducing a name the listener probably hasn't heard, softening the introduction with "the one called," rather than stating the name as though it's already familiar to them.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="田中[たなか]さんが引[ひ]っ越[こ]すという知[し]らせ" romaji="Tanaka-san ga hikkosu to iu shirase" english="the news that Tanaka is moving" />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="雨[あめ]が降[ふ]るというニュース" romaji="Ame ga furu to iu nyuusu" english="the news that it's going to rain" />

<Hint>Here という does a different but related job, wrapping an entire clause into a noun phrase, "the news that X," "the rumor that Y." The clause before という stays in its plain form, exactly like the clause before と思う.</Hint>
