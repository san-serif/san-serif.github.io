Wrapping a verb's stem in お〜になる shows respect for someone else's action, raising them up. Wrapping your own action in お〜する does the opposite, lowering yourself to show humility toward the listener. Both are common in business and formal settings.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="ヤン社長[しゃちょう]は、午前[ごぜん]8時[はちじ]にオフィスにお着[つ]きになります。" romaji="Yan shachou wa, gozen hachiji ni ofisu ni otsuki ni narimasu." english="President Yan will arrive at the office at 8:00am." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="どんな音楽[おんがく]をよくお聞[き]きになりますか。" romaji="Donna ongaku o yoku okiki ni narimasu ka." english="What kind of music do you often listen to?" />

<Hint>着く becomes お着きになる, 聞く becomes お聞きになる, wrapping the verb's stem between お and になる. Some verbs skip this pattern entirely for a completely irregular respectful form instead: 行く/来る/いる become いらっしゃる, 言う becomes おっしゃる, する becomes なさる, 見る becomes ご覧になる, 食べる becomes 召し上がる.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="プロジェクトについてご相談[そうだん]します。" romaji="Purojekuto ni tsuite gosoudan shimasu." english="I'll consult with you about the project." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="会議室[かいぎしつ]にお案内[あんない]します。" romaji="Kaigishitsu ni oannai shimasu." english="Let me show you to the meeting room." />

<Hint>相談する becomes ご相談する, 案内する becomes お案内する, the same お/ご plus verb-stem pattern, but attached to する instead of になる to describe your own action humbly. Verbs of Chinese origin (漢語) usually take ご, native Japanese verbs (和語) usually take お, though there are exceptions worth just memorizing case by case.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="写真[しゃしん]をお撮[と]りしましょうか。" romaji="Shashin o otori shimashou ka." english="Shall I take a photo for you?" />

<Hint>お〜しましょうか politely offers to do something for someone, "shall I," a common combination in service settings. Irregular humble forms exist here too: 行く/来る become まいる, する becomes いたす, 見る becomes 拝見する, もらう becomes いただく.</Hint>
