の can stand in for a noun that's already clear from context, "the one that," letting you describe it with an adjective or clause without repeating the noun itself.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="黒[くろ]いのがいいです。" romaji="Kuroi no ga ii desu." english="I'll take the black one." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="いつも食[た]べているのがいいです。" romaji="Itsumo tabete iru no ga ii desu." english="The one I always eat is good." />

<Hint>黒いの stands in for something like 黒いかばん, "the black bag," with の replacing かばん once it's already clear from context what's being discussed, avoiding a clunky repeat of the same noun.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="ピザは、大[おお]きいのと小[ちい]さいのと、どっちがいいですか。" romaji="Piza wa, ookii no to chiisai no to, dotchi ga ii desu ka." english="For pizza, which is better, the big one or the small one?" />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="皿[さら]は、紙[かみ]のとプラスチックのと、どっちがいいですか。" romaji="Sara wa, kami no to purasuchikku no to, dotchi ga ii desu ka." english="For plates, which is better, the paper ones or the plastic ones?" />

<Hint>紙の, プラスチックの here attach の directly to a noun instead of an adjective, meaning "the paper one," "the plastic one," letting you compare two variants of the same item without saying its name twice.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="この箱[はこ]に入[はい]っているのですよ。" romaji="Kono hako ni haitte iru no desu yo." english="It's the one inside this box." />

<Hint>のです at the end of a sentence like this answers "which one" directly, naming the referent through a description rather than its name, a natural, efficient way to point something out in conversation.</Hint>
