たい attaches to a verb's stem (the part right before ます) and turns it into "want to." Once attached, たい behaves exactly like an い-adjective, not like the verb it came from.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="週末[しゅうまつ]、寿司[すし]を食[た]べたいです。" romaji="Shuumatsu, sushi o tabetai desu." english="I want to eat sushi this weekend." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="週末[しゅうまつ]、友達[ともだち]に会[あ]いたいです。" romaji="Shuumatsu, tomodachi ni aitai desu." english="I want to see a friend this weekend." />

<Hint>たい conjugates like an い-adjective from here on: 食べたい becomes 食べたくない (don't want to), 食べたかった (wanted to), 食べたくなかった (didn't want to). None of that borrows from 食べる's own conjugation pattern, たい runs on its own rules once it's attached.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="週末[しゅうまつ]、料理[りょうり]をしたいですが、掃除[そうじ]をしたくないです。" romaji="Shuumatsu, ryouri o shitai desu ga, souji o shitakunai desu." english="I want to cook this weekend, but I don't want to clean." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="昨日[きのう]は仕事[しごと]に行[い]きたくなかったです。" romaji="Kinou wa shigoto ni ikitakunakatta desu." english="I didn't want to go to work yesterday." />

<Hint>たい is only for your own wants, stated directly, the same restriction Korean's 고 싶다 has. Talking about someone else's want needs a different shape entirely, たがっている, not just swapping the subject into a たい sentence.</Hint>
