You see a friend heading to the beach and wish you could go too: 海に行きたいです. Add たい to a verb stem to say you want to do it.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="週末[しゅうまつ]、寿司[すし]を食[た]べたいです。" romaji="Shuumatsu, sushi o tabetai desu." english="I want to eat sushi this weekend." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="週末[しゅうまつ]、友達[ともだち]に会[あ]いたいです。" romaji="Shuumatsu, tomodachi ni aitai desu." english="I want to see a friend this weekend." />

<Hint type="hint">たい conjugates like an い-adjective from here on: 食べたい becomes 食べたくない (don't want to), 食べたかった (wanted to), 食べたくなかった (didn't want to). None of that borrows from 食べる's own conjugation pattern, たい runs on its own rules once it's attached.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="週末[しゅうまつ]、料理[りょうり]をしたいですが、掃除[そうじ]をしたくないです。" romaji="Shuumatsu, ryouri o shitai desu ga, souji o shitakunai desu." english="I want to cook this weekend, but I don't want to clean." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="昨日[きのう]は仕事[しごと]に行[い]きたくなかったです。" romaji="Kinou wa shigoto ni ikitakunakatta desu." english="I didn't want to go to work yesterday." />

<Hint type="hint">たい is only for your own wants, stated directly, the same restriction Korean's 고 싶다 has. Talking about someone else's want needs a different shape entirely, たがっている, not just swapping the subject into a たい sentence.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="田中[たなか]さんは週末[しゅうまつ]、映画[えいが]を見[み]たがっています。" romaji="Tanaka-san wa shuumatsu, eiga o mitagatteimasu." english="Tanaka wants to watch a movie this weekend." />
