This spoken pattern dismisses the importance of X. The speaker repeats X with 不 to push back against a concern, often reassuring a friend or correcting someone who is being overly formal.

### Wave Away a Concern

什么钱不钱的, 朋友之间别这么客气 rejects the idea that money needs to be discussed between friends. The repeated pair can be a noun or verb; the speaker’s tone makes the dismissal clear.

<CSentenceBlock simplified="什么钱不钱的，朋友之间别这么客气。" traditional="什麼錢不錢的，朋友之間別這麼客氣。" pinyin="shénme qián bù qián de, péngyou zhījiān bié zhème kèqi." jyutping="sam6 mo1 cin4 bat1 cin4 dik1, pang4 jau5 zi1 gaan1 bit6 ze2 mo1 hak3 hei3" english="Money, what money? Friends don’t need to be so formal." />

<Hint type="hint">This is not a neutral question asking for a yes-or-no answer. It dismisses the distinction as unimportant.</Hint>

### Brush Off Winning or Losing

Use the same shape when you want to say that a contrast does not matter and point to what matters instead.

<CSentenceBlock simplified="比赛输不输的没关系，认真参加就好。" traditional="比賽輸不輸的沒關係，認真參加就好。" pinyin="bǐsài shū bù shū de méi guānxi, rènzhēn cānjiā jiù hǎo." jyutping="bei2 coi3 syu1 bat1 syu1 dik1 mut6 gwaan1 hai6, jing6 zan1 caam1 gaa1 zau6 hou2" english="Winning or losing doesn’t matter; take part and do your best." />

<Hint type="insight">Friends may use this pattern to wave away money, thanks, or a small mistake. With a sharp tone, it can sound as if you are dismissing the other person’s concern.</Hint>
