“连老师也忘了带钥匙”—even the teacher forgot their keys. 连 points to the detail that sits furthest along the scale of expectation; 也 or 都 completes the thought. The surprising item comes right after 连.

<CSentenceBlock simplified="这个问题连老师都觉得难。" traditional="這個問題連老師都覺得難。" pinyin="Zhège wèntí lián lǎoshī dōu juéde nán." jyutping="ze5 go3 man6 tai4 lin4 lou5 si1 dou1 gok3 dak1 naan4." english="Even the teacher finds this question difficult." />

The teacher is the surprising example: if even the teacher finds it difficult, it must be a tough question.

<CSentenceBlock simplified="昨天太忙了，我连午饭都没吃。" traditional="昨天太忙了，我連午飯都沒吃。" pinyin="Zuótiān tài máng le, wǒ lián wǔfàn dōu méi chī." jyutping="zok6 tin1 taai3 mong4 liu5, ngo5 lin4 ng5 faan6 dou1 mut6 hek3." english="I was so busy yesterday that I didn’t even eat lunch." />

Here the missed lunch is the extreme detail. 连…都 makes the listener notice how busy the speaker was.

<CSentenceBlock simplified="他说得太快，连我也听不懂。" traditional="他說得太快，連我也聽不懂。" pinyin="Tā shuō de tài kuài, lián wǒ yě tīngbudǒng." jyutping="taa1 syut3 dak1 taai3 faai3, lin4 ngo5 jaa5 teng1 bat1 dung2." english="He speaks so fast that even I can’t understand him." />

也 can replace 都 in the second part. Both complete the pattern; choose the one that sounds natural in your sentence.

<Hint type="hint">Keep the focus after 连: 连老师都…, 连午饭也…. The word after 连 is the detail you want the listener to find surprising.</Hint>

<Hint type="insight">连…也/都 is common in conversation when you want to underline an exception or an extreme case: even this person, even that small thing, or even at that time.</Hint>
