それでは opens a closing line or a pivot toward a decision, "well then." じゃ (or じゃあ) is its everyday, casual counterpart, doing exactly the same job at a lower register.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="それでは、また来週[らいしゅう]。" romaji="Sore de wa, mata raishuu." english="Well then, see you next week." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="じゃ、また明日[あした]。" romaji="Ja, mata ashita." english="Well, see you tomorrow." />

<Hint>それでは and じゃ both wrap up a conversation and lead into a farewell, それでは fitting a formal or business setting, じゃ fitting friends and casual company. Neither actually changes the meaning, just the register the whole exchange is pitched at.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="それでは、今晩[こんばん]持[も]っていきます。" romaji="Sore de wa, konban motte ikimasu." english="Well then, I'll bring it tonight." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="そうですか。じゃあ、水曜日[すいようび]はどうですか。" romaji="Sou desu ka. Jaa, suiyoubi wa dou desu ka." english="I see. Well then, how about Wednesday?" />

<Hint>Beyond closing a conversation, それでは/じゃあ also pivots a discussion toward the next step, "given that, here's what we'll do," often followed by a suggestion or a decision, as in じゃあ、水曜日はどうですか, floating a new option after the first one didn't work.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="それじゃあ、プレゼントは、目覚[めざ]まし時計[どけい]にしましょう。" romaji="Sore jaa, purezento wa, mezamashi tokei ni shimashou." english="Well then, let's make the present an alarm clock." />

<Hint>それじゃあ, blending それでは's formality with じゃあ's shape, often introduces a final decision after weighing options, the same にしましょう pattern you already know from する・なる, "let's make it X."</Hint>
