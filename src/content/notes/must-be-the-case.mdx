に違いない attaches to the plain form and means "there's no mistaking it, it must be true." It sits at the confident end of the certainty scale, built from 違う (to differ) plus ない, literally "there's no way it's different."

<JSentenceBlock furigana="山本[やまもと]さんは、外国人[がいこくじん]の友達[ともだち]が多[おお]いに違[ちが]いありません。" romaji="Yamamoto-san wa, gaikokujin no tomodachi ga ooi ni chigai arimasen." english="Yamamoto must have a lot of foreign friends." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="山本[やまもと]さんは、引[ひ]っ越[こ]しに慣[な]れているに違[ちが]いありません。" romaji="Yamamoto-san wa, hikkoshi ni narete iru ni chigai arimasen." english="Yamamoto must be used to moving houses." />

<Hint>Ranked by confidence, かもしれない is the weakest, "might be," だろう sits in the middle, "probably," はず claims a reason-backed expectation, and に違いない tops them all, a near-certain conclusion drawn from strong evidence.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="駅[えき]のそばにあるなら、遅[おそ]くまで開[あ]いているに違[ちが]いありませんね。" romaji="Eki no soba ni aru nara, osoku made aite iru ni chigai arimasen ne." english="If it's right by the station, it must stay open late." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="最近[さいきん]、賞[しょう]をもらったなら、よく雑誌[ざっし]やテレビで紹介[しょうかい]されているに違[ちが]いありませんね。" romaji="Saikin, shou o moratta nara, yoku zasshi ya terebi de shoukai sarete iru ni chigai arimasen ne." english="If they won an award recently, they must be getting featured in magazines and on TV a lot." />

<Hint>な-adjectives and nouns attach directly with no だ, 元気に違いない, 田中さんに違いない, the same pattern as はず and のに. ありません politely softens ない at the end, in casual speech に違いない alone works fine.</Hint>
