Attach 込[こ]む to a verb stem to add depth or inward movement. With motion verbs, it often means “go into / put into.” With other verbs, it can mean doing something thoroughly, intensely, or for a long time.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="子供[こども]たちは、海[うみ]に飛[と]び込[こ]みました。" romaji="Kodomotachi wa, umi ni tobikomimashita." english="The children jumped into the sea." />

飛び込む combines 飛ぶ, “fly / jump,” with 込む to show entering a space. Other motion compounds include 入り込む, “go deep inside,” and 押し込む, “push into.”

<JSentenceBlock furigana="彼[かれ]は返事[へんじ]をせず、長[なが]い間[あいだ]考[かんが]え込[こ]んでいました。" romaji="Kare wa henji o sezu, nagai aida kangaekonde imashita." english="He didn't answer and spent a long time thinking deeply." />

考え込む means to become absorbed in thought. Similar compounds describe an action becoming intense or lasting: 黙り込む, “fall silent”; 読み込む, “read closely / load in.” The base verb suggests which action is happening; 込む adds the sense of depth or immersion.

<Hint type="insight">In a conversation, 考え込んでいるみたい, “it looks like they're deep in thought,” is a natural observation when someone pauses before answering. For physical movement, 飛び込む can describe jumping into water or rushing into a situation.</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">Ask whether the base verb involves movement. If so, 込む often means entering inward; otherwise, it often intensifies or deepens the action. Learn common compounds as words, since the exact nuance depends on the base verb.</Hint>
