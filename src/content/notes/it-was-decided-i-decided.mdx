ことになる attaches after a verb's plain form and reports that something has been decided, usually by circumstances or by someone else, not by your own personal will. ことにする reports the opposite: a decision you made yourself, on purpose.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="リーさんは、アフリカにボランティアをしに行[い]くことになりました。" romaji="Rii-san wa, Afurika ni borantia o shi ni iku koto ni narimashita." english="It's been decided that Lee will go volunteer in Africa." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="健太[けんた]さんは、大学院[だいがくいん]で勉強[べんきょう]することになりました。" romaji="Kenta-san wa, daigakuin de benkyou suru koto ni narimashita." english="It's been arranged that Kenta will study at graduate school." />

<Hint>ことになりました almost always implies the decision came from outside the speaker, a company transfer, a school assignment, a circumstance that worked out this way, rather than a personal choice freely made.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="締[し]め切[き]りが近[ちか]かったから、レポートを書[か]くことにしました。" romaji="Shimekiri ga chikakatta kara, repooto o kaku koto ni shimashita." english="Since the deadline was close, I decided to write the report." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="毎日[まいにち]8時間[はちじかん]寝[ね]ることにしています。" romaji="Mainichi hachijikan neru koto ni shite imasu." english="I make it a rule to sleep eight hours every day." />

<Hint>ことにしました reports a one-time decision, "I decided to." ことにしています, built with ている, reports an ongoing personal rule or habit you've committed to, "I make it a policy to," a subtle but useful shift in nuance.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="10時[じゅうじ]にお客様[きゃくさま]に電話[でんわ]することになっています。" romaji="Juuji ni okyakusama ni denwa suru koto ni natte imasu." english="It's scheduled that I call the customer at 10:00." />

<Hint>ことになっている, with ている on ことになる, describes a standing rule or schedule that's already in place, often a workplace policy or an arrangement everyone's expected to follow, rather than a single decision made once.</Hint>
