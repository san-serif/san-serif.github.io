<JapaneseLessonHead />

かもしれない attaches to the plain form of a verb, adjective, or noun and marks a statement as merely possible, somewhere well below だろう in how confident it sounds.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="明日[あした]は雨[あめ]が降[ふ]るかもしれません。" romaji="Ashita wa ame ga furu kamoshiremasen." english="It might rain tomorrow." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="大輔[だいすけ]は今日[きょう]、来[こ]ないかもしれません。" romaji="Daisuke wa kyou, konai kamoshiremasen." english="Daisuke might not come today." />

<Hint>かもしれない sits at the low end of a certainty scale that also includes だろう and でしょう, which you already know. だろう leans toward "probably," かもしれない just says "possible, no more than that," a genuinely open guess rather than an educated one.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="このカレーは辛[から]いかもしれません。" romaji="Kono karee wa karai kamoshiremasen." english="This curry might be spicy." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="直樹[なおき]は忙[いそが]しいかもしれません。" romaji="Naoki wa isogashii kamoshiremasen." english="Naoki might be busy." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="沙耶[さや]は今[いま]、家[いえ]じゃないかもしれません。" romaji="Saya wa ima, ie janai kamoshiremasen." english="Saya might not be home right now." />

<Hint>Nouns and な-adjectives drop だ entirely before かもしれない: 家だ becomes 家かもしれません, never 家だかもしれません. Verbs and い-adjectives attach in their plain form with no change, and かもしれない itself never conjugates to match politeness, only しれません softens it for polite speech.</Hint>
