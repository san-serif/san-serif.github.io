At the café, your friend orders another cup: 你还要一杯吗？ If you want the same thing, 也 lets you join in: 我也要一杯. 也 adds someone or something to the picture; 还 can keep an action going or add one more. The sentence tells you which meaning fits.

### Examples

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我也喜欢茶。" traditional="我也喜歡茶。" pinyin="Wǒ yě xǐhuan chá." jyutping="ngo5 jaa5 hei2 fun1 caa4." english="I like tea too." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="他还在学校。" traditional="他還在學校。" pinyin="Tā hái zài xuéxiào." jyutping="taa1 waan4 zoi6 hok6 haau6." english="He is still at school." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="你还要一杯吗？" traditional="你還要一杯嗎？" pinyin="Nǐ hái yào yì bēi ma?" jyutping="nei5 waan4 jiu3 jat1 bui1 maa3?" english="Would you like one more cup?" />

<Hint type="hint">也 normally comes before the verb or adjective: 我也去. English “too” may appear at the end, but Chinese 也 does not.</Hint>
