You’re talking about getting better after a cold: 감기가 나았어요. Some verb stems ending in ㅅ drop ㅅ before a vowel-starting ending: 낫다 becomes 나아요, and the change carries into the past as 나았어요.

<SentenceBlock formal="이 색이 더 낫습니다." formalRom="i saegi deo naseumnida." polite="이 색이 더 나아요." politeRom="i saegi deo naayo." casual="이 색이 더 나아." casualRom="i saegi deo naa." english="This color is better." />

<SentenceBlock formal="생강차를 마시면 감기가 빨리 낫습니다." formalRom="saenggangchareul masimyeon gamgiga ppalli naseumnida." polite="생강차를 마시면 감기가 빨리 나아요." politeRom="saenggangchareul masimyeon gamgiga ppalli naayo." casual="생강차를 마시면 감기가 빨리 나아." casualRom="saenggangchareul masimyeon gamgiga ppalli naa." english="If you drink ginger tea, your cold will get better quickly." />

<Hint type="hint">Not every ㅅ-stem is irregular. 씻다 keeps its ㅅ before a vowel: 씻어요, 씻었어요. Learn the common irregular verbs individually; the spelling alone doesn't tell you which group a verb belongs to.</Hint>

<SentenceBlock formal="강아지 이름을 뭐라고 지을 겁니까?" formalRom="gangaji ireumeul mworago jieul geomnikka?" polite="강아지 이름을 뭐라고 지을 거예요?" politeRom="gangaji ireumeul mworago jieul geoyeyo?" casual="강아지 이름을 뭐라고 지을 거야?" casualRom="gangaji ireumeul mworago jieul geoya?" english="What are you going to name the puppy?" />

<Hint type="insight">In real life, 낫다 often comes up in everyday check-ins about health: 감기가 나았어요? means “Has your cold gotten better?” Keep its meaning in context, since 낫다 can also mean “to be better” when comparing two choices.</Hint>
