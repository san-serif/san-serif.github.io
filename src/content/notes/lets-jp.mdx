ましょう attaches to a verb's stem to mean "let's." Add か and the same shape becomes a suggestion instead of a proposal, "shall we," softer and more tentative.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="ハワイでは海[うみ]で泳[およ]ぎましょう。" romaji="Hawai dewa umi de oyogimashou." english="Let's swim in the ocean in Hawaii." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="部屋[へや]を掃除[そうじ]しましょうか。" romaji="Heya o souji shimashou ka." english="Shall I clean the room?" />

<Hint>ましょうか often isn't proposing something for both of you, it's one person offering to do something alone and checking if that's wanted, closer to "should I..." than "let's." Context, especially who's actually doing the verb, tells the two uses apart.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="ハワイでは山[やま]に登[のぼ]ろう。" romaji="Hawai dewa yama ni noborou." english="Let's climb a mountain in Hawaii." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="ケーキを焼[や]こうか。" romaji="Keeki o yakou ka." english="Should we bake a cake?" />

<Hint>ろう/おう/よう is the casual version of ましょう, built by shifting the verb's dictionary-form ending the same way the negative and past tenses shift, not by attaching ましょう and then stripping the polite part off. 話す becomes 話そう, 食べる becomes 食べよう, 来る becomes 来よう, each verb group has its own casual volitional shape to learn.</Hint>
