ちゃんと and きちんと mean “properly,” “carefully,” or “as expected.” They describe doing something in the way it should be done, or making sure that an expected thing actually happens.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="提出[ていしゅつ]する前[まえ]に、書類[しょるい]をきちんと確認[かくにん]してください。" romaji="Teishutsu suru mae ni, shorui o kichinto kakunin shite kudasai." english="Please check the documents carefully before submitting them." />

きちんと often sounds orderly and careful, so it fits instructions and professional situations. ちゃんと is more conversational and can carry the same meaning: ちゃんと準備した, “I prepared properly.”

<JSentenceBlock furigana="約束[やくそく]した時間[じかん]に、ちゃんと来[き]てくださいね。" romaji="Yakusoku shita jikan ni, chanto kite kudasai ne." english="Please make sure you come at the time we agreed on." />

ちゃんと can gently remind someone to follow through. Depending on voice and context, it can also sound like a scolding: ちゃんと聞いて, “Listen properly.”

<Hint type="insight">Use きちんと for careful work such as checking paperwork or organizing records. Use ちゃんと in everyday reminders about showing up, eating, replying, or finishing a task. Both can be positive; a sharp tone makes either one sound critical.</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">Choose きちんと when you want a neat, careful tone. Choose ちゃんと for a more conversational reminder. The meaning overlaps, but the social tone can differ.</Hint>
