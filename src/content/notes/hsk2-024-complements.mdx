You’re asking a friend to repeat a story, then comparing how tall you are. Chinese uses a short measure after the verb to count an action, and after an adjective to say how large the difference is.

For a repeat, put the number and 遍 after the verb: 看了两遍, “watched it twice.” 遍 counts a complete pass through something, such as reading a page or watching a film. 次 also means “time” or “occasion,” but 遍 highlights going through the whole thing.

<CSentenceBlock simplified="这部电影我看了两遍。" traditional="這部電影我看了兩遍。" pinyin="zhè bù diànyǐng wǒ kàn le liǎng biàn." jyutping="ze5 bou6 din6 jing2 ngo5 hon3 liu5 loeng5 bin3." english="I’ve watched this film twice." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="请再说一遍，我没听清楚。" traditional="請再說一遍，我沒聽清楚。" pinyin="qǐng zài shuō yí biàn, wǒ méi tīng qīngchu." jyutping="cing2 zoi3 syut3 jat1 bin3, ngo5 mut6 teng1 cing1 co2." english="Could you say that once more? I didn’t hear clearly." />

To compare a measurable difference, put the amount after the adjective: 高十厘米, “ten centimetres taller”; 贵二十块, “twenty yuan more expensive.” The comparison word 比 often appears earlier in the sentence.

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我比姐姐小三岁。" traditional="我比姐姐小三歲。" pinyin="wǒ bǐ jiějie xiǎo sān suì." jyutping="ngo5 bei2 ze2 ze2 siu2 saam1 seoi3." english="I’m three years younger than my older sister." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="这条路比那条路长两公里。" traditional="這條路比那條路長兩公里。" pinyin="zhè tiáo lù bǐ nà tiáo lù cháng liǎng gōnglǐ." jyutping="ze5 tiu4 lou6 bei2 naa5 tiu4 lou6 coeng4 loeng5 gung1 lei5." english="This road is two kilometres longer than that one." />

<Hint type="hint">遍 counts a whole run-through. The number after an adjective answers “by how much?” It doesn’t replace 比 when you’re naming both things being compared.</Hint>

<Hint type="insight">In a real conversation, soften a repeat request with 不好意思: 不好意思，可以再说一遍吗？ (“Sorry, could you say that one more time?”)</Hint>
