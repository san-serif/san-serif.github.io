Connect a purpose with the effort behind it, or concede a point before giving the main claim. These patterns help organize a complex argument: a writer may grant a point, describe a purpose, or connect an action with its intended result. Find the main claim after the concession before deciding the overall direction.

### Patterns in this lesson

- **はおろか** — Let alone, Much less, Needless to say, Not to mention, Not only but also
- **といわず** — Not only A or B but every-; A, B and every-; Not only A or B but all over;  A, B both, whether A or B
- **にもまして** — Even -er than, Even more than, More than anything, More than anybody, More than ever
- **次第です** — Because the reason is, Therefore, The present situation is like this, This is how (it is), Those are the circumstances, This is why
- **というところ** — I would say (about), I guess (about), It could be said that, About, At best
- **１～たりとも～ない** — Not even one, Not a single, No (at all)
- **ときたら** — When it comes to, That or this … of mine, That or this darn …, That or this blasted …
- **びる** — -like, Looking, Feeling, With an air of, To become
- **にしたところで** — Even, Even for, Even from the perspective of, Even in the case of
- **に即して** — In line with, Based on, In accordance with
- **ないまでも** — May not … but (at least), Even though not, Even if not
- **をよそに** — Ignoring, Disregarding, Despite
- **まじき** — Unbecoming of, Unworthy of, Not proper for, Should not, Must not, Cannot

<JSentenceBlock furigana="費用[ひよう]がかかるとはいえ、安全性[あんぜんせい]を優先[ゆうせん]すべきです。" romaji="Hiyou ga kakaru to wa ie, anzensei o yuusen subeki desu." english="Although it costs money, safety should take priority." />

とはいえ acknowledges the cost but keeps the recommendation as the main claim. It is a concession, not a reversal.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="文章[ぶんしょう]の文脈[ぶんみゃく]から、筆者[ひっしゃ]の意図[いと]を読[よ]み取[と]りましょう。" romaji="Bunshou no bunmyaku kara, hissha no ito o yomitorimashou." english="Use the context to identify the writer’s intention." />

In an opinion piece, mark the concession separately from the writer’s conclusion; the main point often follows it.

<Hint type="insight">In a proposal, a sentence may concede cost or inconvenience before stating why a plan should still go ahead. とはいえ is a useful cue that the main claim is still coming.</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">Bracket the concession and underline the main clause. The writer’s central recommendation usually appears after the concession.</Hint>
