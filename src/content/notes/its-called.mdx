이라고 해요 (after a consonant) or 라고 해요 (after a vowel) frames something as "called" or "named" X, a step removed from the plain copula 이에요/예요. It's less "this is X" and more "this is what X is called."

<SentenceBlock formal="저는 안나라고 합니다." formalRom="jeoneun annarago hamnida." polite="저는 안나라고 해요." politeRom="jeoneun annarago haeyo." casual="나는 안나라고 해." casualRom="naneun annarago hae." english="I am called Anna." />

<SentenceBlock formal="이건 한국어로 뭐라고 합니까?" formalRom="igeon hangugeoro mworago hamnikka?" polite="이건 한국어로 뭐라고 해요?" politeRom="igeon hangugeoro mworago haeyo?" casual="이건 한국어로 뭐라고 해?" casualRom="igeon hangugeoro mworago hae?" english="What is this called in Korean?" />

<Hint>이거 한국어로 뭐라고 해요? is arguably the single most useful sentence in this whole curriculum. It's how you ask for a word you don't have yet, mid-conversation, pointing at something real and getting the vocabulary on the spot instead of looking it up later.</Hint>

<SentenceBlock formal="그건 한복이라고 합니다." formalRom="geugeon hanbogirago hamnida." polite="그건 한복이라고 해요." politeRom="geugeon hanbogirago haeyo." casual="그건 한복이라고 해." casualRom="geugeon hanbogirago hae." english="That is called hanbok." />

<SentenceBlock formal="니하오를 한국어로 뭐라고 합니까?" formalRom="nihaoreul hangugeoro mworago hamnikka?" polite="니하오를 한국어로 뭐라고 해요?" politeRom="nihaoreul hangugeoro mworago haeyo?" casual="니하오를 한국어로 뭐라고 해?" casualRom="nihaoreul hangugeoro mworago hae?" english="How do you say “ni hao” in Korean?" />

<SentenceBlock formal="안녕하세요라고 합니다." formalRom="annyeonghaseyorago hamnida." polite="안녕하세요라고 해요." politeRom="annyeonghaseyorago haeyo." casual="안녕하세요라고 해." casualRom="annyeonghaseyorago hae." english="It's called “annyeonghaseyo.”" />

<SentenceBlock formal="한국말로 당근이라고 합니다." formalRom="hangungmallo danggeunirago hamnida." polite="한국말로 당근이라고 해요." politeRom="hangungmallo danggeunirago haeyo." casual="한국말로 당근이라고 해." casualRom="hangungmallo danggeunirago hae." english="In Korean, it's called carrot." />

<Hint>한국어 and 한국말 both mean "Korean language," but they're not perfectly interchangeable. 한국어 feels a touch more formal or written; 한국말 (말 meaning "speech") leans more conversational, closer to "Korean talk" than "the Korean language" as an academic subject.</Hint>
