You’re about to make a suggestion, but first explain the situation: 시간이 없는데 택시를 탈까요? -는데/-은데/-ㄴ데 gives the listener context before the main point. Depending on the sentence, it can set up a question, contrast, or next move.

<SentenceBlock formal="비가 오는데 택시를 탈까요?" formalRom="biga oneunde taeksireul talkkayo?" polite="비가 오는데 택시를 탈까요?" politeRom="biga oneunde taeksireul talkkayo?" casual="비가 오는데 택시를 탈까?" casualRom="biga oneunde taeksireul talkka?" english="It's raining; shall we take a taxi?" />

<SentenceBlock formal="날씨가 좋은데 산에 갈까요?" formalRom="nalssiga joeunde sane galkkayo?" polite="날씨가 좋은데 산에 갈까요?" politeRom="nalssiga joeunde sane galkkayo?" casual="날씨가 좋은데 산에 갈까?" casualRom="nalssiga joeunde sane galkka?" english="The weather is nice; shall we go to the mountain?" />

<Hint type="hint">Adjectives use their own endings: 싸다 → 싼데, 좋다 → 좋은데. Action verbs use -는데 regardless of the stem ending: 가다 → 가는데, 먹다 → 먹는데. 있다 and 없다 also take -는데.</Hint>

<SentenceBlock formal="머리가 조금 아픈데 약이 있습니까?" formalRom="meoriga jogeum apeunde yagi itseumnikka?" polite="머리가 조금 아픈데 약이 있어요?" politeRom="meoriga jogeum apeunde yagi isseoyo?" casual="머리가 조금 아픈데 약 있어?" casualRom="meoriga jogeum apeunde yak isseo?" english="My head hurts a little; do you have any medicine?" />

<Hint type="insight">In real life, -는데 is a useful way to make a request feel less abrupt: state the situation first, then let the listener infer what you need. 머리가 아픈데요… (“My head hurts…”) can be a gentle opening before asking for medicine or help.</Hint>
