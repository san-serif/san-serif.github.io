You and your housemate are dividing the chores. First finish the washing-up, then clean the floor. “I’ll do this, so please do that” turns two jobs into an agreement.

### Finish the First Step

Verb stem + -고 나서 places the next action after the first has been completed: 씻고 나서, 먹고 나서. It makes completion clearer than a simple -고 list. [-(으)ㄴ 후에](/notes/then-what) can often describe the same sequence.

<SentenceBlock formal="청소하고 나서 같이 쉽니다." formalRom="cheongsohago naseo gachi swimnida." polite="청소하고 나서 같이 쉬어요." politeRom="cheongsohago naseo gachi swieoyo." casual="청소하고 나서 같이 쉬어." casualRom="cheongsohago naseo gachi swieo." english="We rest together after cleaning." />

### Make Your Part the Basis for a Request

-(으)ㄹ 테니까 combines a plan or expectation with a reason for what follows. With a first-person commitment: 제가 청소할 테니까…. With a prediction: 비가 올 테니까…. Use -ㄹ after a vowel or ㄹ and -을 after another consonant.

<SentenceBlock formal="제가 거실을 청소할 테니까 주방을 부탁합니다." formalRom="jega geosireul cheongsohal tenikka jubangeul butakhamnida." polite="제가 거실을 청소할 테니까 주방을 부탁해요." politeRom="jega geosireul cheongsohal tenikka jubangeul butakhaeyo." casual="내가 거실을 청소할 테니까 주방을 부탁해." casualRom="naega geosireul cheongsohal tenikka jubangeul butakhae." english="I’ll clean the living room, so please take care of the kitchen." />

<SentenceBlock formal="비가 올 테니까 우산을 가져가십시오." formalRom="biga ol tenikka usaneul gajyeogasipsio." polite="비가 올 테니까 우산을 가져가세요." politeRom="biga ol tenikka usaneul gajyeogaseyo." casual="비가 올 테니까 우산을 가져가." casualRom="biga ol tenikka usaneul gajyeoga." english="It will probably rain, so take an umbrella." />

<Hint type="hint">With a promise, -ㄹ 테니까 invites the listener to act on your commitment. It does not guarantee that every prediction made with the same ending is certain.</Hint>
