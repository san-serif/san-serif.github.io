These vivid forms describe striking states. まみれ means covered or smeared with something undesirable, while だらけ means full of something, often mistakes or other unwanted things. ずくめ describes a series or situation filled with one quality. めく means “to seem or have the air of,” while adjective stem + げ describes an appearance or impression, as in 不安げな顔, “an anxious-looking face.” んばかりに describes an action so intense it is almost as if something else were happening. ったらない intensifies an evaluation (“incredibly...”), and さぞ expresses the speaker's inference about how intense someone else's experience must be.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="雨[あめ]の中[なか]を歩[ある]いて、服[ふく]は泥[どろ]まみれになりました。" romaji="Ame no naka o aruite, fuku wa doromamire ni narimashita." english="After walking in the rain, my clothes were covered in mud." />

まみれ follows a noun and usually describes an undesirable substance covering something: 泥まみれ, “covered in mud”; 汗まみれ, “soaked in sweat.”

<JSentenceBlock furigana="この作文[さくぶん]は間違[まちが]いだらけですが、内容[ないよう]は面白[おもしろ]いです。" romaji="Kono sakubun wa machigai darake desu ga, naiyou wa omoshiroi desu." english="This essay is full of mistakes, but the content is interesting." />

だらけ follows a noun and means “full of,” commonly with an undesirable thing or flaw. まみれ focuses more on a covering or substance clinging to a surface.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="今年[ことし]は嬉[うれ]しい知[し]らせずくめでした。" romaji="Kotoshi wa ureshii shirase zukume deshita." english="This year was full of good news." />

ずくめ describes a whole run or state made up of the same kind of thing, often positive but not always. めく attaches to nouns to describe a quality something seems to have: 謎めいた表情, “a mysterious-looking expression.”

<JSentenceBlock furigana="彼女[かのじょ]は感動[かんどう]のあまり、泣[な]き出[だ]さんばかりでした。" romaji="Kanojo wa kandou no amari, nakidasan bakari deshita." english="She was so moved she looked as if she might burst into tears." />

んばかりに makes an action seem just on the verge of happening. ったらない is colloquial emphasis, as in 忙しいったらない, “I'm incredibly busy.” さぞ expresses an inference about another person's feelings: さぞ疲れたでしょう, “you must have been very tired.”

<Hint type="insight">まみれ usually suggests something clinging to a surface and is often messy. ずくめ describes the overall character of a period or set of events. さぞ is empathetic or inferential, while ったらない is the speaker's own emphatic reaction.</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">Covered in an unwelcome substance: まみれ. A run full of one quality: ずくめ. Has the air of: めく. Almost as if: んばかり. Extremely (colloquial): ったらない. “Must have been”: さぞ.</Hint>
