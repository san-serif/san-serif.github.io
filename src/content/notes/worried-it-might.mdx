You leave early because you are worried about traffic. Your friend suggests taking a coat because it will probably be cold later. Both sentences look ahead, but only one makes worry the reason.

### A Worry Prompts an Action

Use -ㄹ까 봐 after a vowel or ㄹ and -을까 봐 after another consonant: 늦을까 봐, 잊을까 봐, 비가 올까 봐. It introduces a feared possibility, often followed by worry or a precaution. -ㄹ까 봐서 is also possible.

### An Expectation Sets Up the Next Point

-(으)ㄹ 텐데 presents an expectation or supposition as background for a question, concern, or suggestion: 추울 텐데…. It can also concern an assumed past difficulty: 어려웠을 텐데…. It does not have to express fear.

<SentenceBlock formal="밤에는 추울 텐데 외투를 가져가십시오." formalRom="bameneun chuul tende oetureul gajyeogasipsio." polite="밤에는 추울 텐데 외투를 가져가세요." politeRom="bameneun chuul tende oetureul gajyeogaseyo." casual="밤에는 추울 텐데 외투를 가져가." casualRom="bameneun chuul tende oetureul gajyeoga." english="It will probably be cold at night, so take a coat." />

<SentenceBlock formal="혼자 준비하기 어려웠을 텐데 어떻게 했습니까?" formalRom="honja junbihagi eoryeowosseul tende eotteoke haetseumnikka?" polite="혼자 준비하기 어려웠을 텐데 어떻게 했어요?" politeRom="honja junbihagi eoryeowosseul tende eotteoke haesseoyo?" casual="혼자 준비하기 어려웠을 텐데 어떻게 했어?" casualRom="honja junbihagi eoryeowosseul tende eotteoke haesseo?" english="It must have been hard to prepare alone; how did you manage?" />

<Hint type="hint">[-ㄹ 테니까](/notes/finish-first-ill-handle-it) makes a plan or prediction the reason for what follows. -ㄹ 텐데 sets an expectation in the background, often leaving room for concern, contrast, or discussion.</Hint>
