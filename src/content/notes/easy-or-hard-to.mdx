やすい (easy to) and にくい (hard to) attach to a verb's stem and turn it into an い-adjective describing how easy or difficult that action is to do.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="漢字[かんじ]は覚[おぼ]えにくいです。" romaji="Kanji wa oboenikui desu." english="Kanji are hard to memorize." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="クラスメートは話[はな]しやすいです。" romaji="Kurasumeeto wa hanashiyasui desu." english="My classmates are easy to talk to." />

<Hint>覚える becomes 覚えやすい or 覚えにくい, 話す becomes 話しやすい or 話しにくい, both built on the same verb stem you've used for たい, すぎる, and ながら. Once attached, they conjugate exactly like any other い-adjective, やすくない, やすかった, にくくなかった.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="ペンとボールペンと、どちらの方[ほう]が書[か]きやすいと思[おも]いますか。" romaji="Pen to boorupen to, dochira no hou ga kakiyasui to omoimasu ka." english="Between a pen and a ballpoint pen, which do you think is easier to write with?" />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="この花[はな]とあの花[はな]と、どちらの方[ほう]が育[そだ]てやすいと思[おも]いますか。" romaji="Kono hana to ano hana to, dochira no hou ga sodateyasui to omoimasu ka." english="Between this flower and that one, which do you think is easier to grow?" />

<Hint>どちらの方が〜と思いますか, built on やすい or にくい, is a natural way to ask someone to compare two options based on ease or difficulty, common when shopping or choosing between similar things.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="壊[こわ]れにくい傘[かさ]はありませんか。" romaji="Kowarenikui kasa wa arimasen ka." english="Do you have an umbrella that doesn't break easily?" />

<Hint>Xありませんか built on にくい is a common way to ask a shop clerk for a durable or convenient version of something, "isn't there one that's hard to break," effectively asking for the sturdiest option.</Hint>
