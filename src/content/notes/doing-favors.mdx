あげる, くれる, and もらう, which you already know for giving and receiving things, attach after a verb's て-form to describe doing or receiving a favor, an action given rather than an object.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="由紀[ゆき]さんは大輔[だいすけ]さんに宿題[しゅくだい]を教[おし]えてあげました。" romaji="Yuki-san wa Daisuke-san ni shukudai o oshiete agemashita." english="Yuki taught Daisuke his homework (as a favor)." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="大輔[だいすけ]さんは由紀[ゆき]さんに宿題[しゅくだい]を教[おし]えてもらいました。" romaji="Daisuke-san wa Yuki-san ni shukudai o oshiete moraimashita." english="Daisuke had Yuki teach him his homework." />

<Hint>These two sentences describe the exact same event from opposite sides, exactly like plain あげる and もらう did with objects. てあげる puts the giver of the favor as the subject; てもらう puts the receiver as the subject, with に marking whoever did the actual favor.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="田中[たなか]さんが予約[よやく]してくれました。" romaji="Tanaka-san ga yoyaku shite kuremashita." english="Tanaka made the reservation for me." />

<Hint>てくれる always centers the speaker, or someone close to the speaker, as the one benefiting, which is why 予約してくれました naturally implies "for me" even without saying so directly. Compare 予約してあげました, which would instead describe the speaker doing a favor for someone else.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="沙耶[さや]さんは直樹[なおき]さんを病院[びょういん]に連[つ]れて行[い]ってあげました。" romaji="Saya-san wa Naoki-san o byouin ni tsurete itte agemashita." english="Saya took Naoki to the hospital (as a favor)." />

<Hint>Careful with あげる toward someone above you socially, saying してあげました can sound like you're bragging about the favor. くれる and もらう carry no such risk, which is why they're used more freely across all levels of politeness.</Hint>
