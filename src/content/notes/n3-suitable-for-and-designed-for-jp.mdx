向き and 向け both connect something to a user or purpose, but the direction differs. Noun + 向き means “suited to” or “a good fit for” that person or use. Noun + 向け means “aimed at” or “intended for” a target audience. A product can be向け a certain group without being especially向き for every member of that group.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="短[みじか]い記事[きじ]は、通勤中[つうきんちゅう]に読[よ]むのに向[む]いています。" romaji="Mijikai kiji wa, tsuukin-chuu ni yomu no ni muite imasu." english="Short articles are well suited to reading during a commute." />

向き describes suitability or fit. It often appears as 向いている and can be used for people, tools, activities, or environments.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="この本[ほん]は初級者向[しょきゅうしゃむ]けに、短[みじか]い文[ぶん]で書[か]かれています。" romaji="Kono hon wa shokyuusha-muke ni, mijikai bun de kakarete imasu." english="This book is written in short sentences for beginners." />

向け identifies the intended audience or market. It can modify a noun directly (初心者向けの本) or appear with に to describe the intended design. Unlike 向き, it does not necessarily claim the product is a perfect fit for that group.

に合わせて means “in accordance with / to match,” describing an adjustment made to fit a person, schedule, or condition: 相手に合わせて話し方を変える, “change how you speak to suit the other person.”

<Hint type="insight">A beginner's workbook is 初心者向け because it was designed for that audience. A clear, step-by-step explanation may also be 初心者向き because it is suitable for beginners. The two often overlap, but 向け focuses on intent and 向き on fit.</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">Intended audience: X向け. Suitable fit: X向き / Xに向いている. Use 向け for marketing, courses, and products; use 向き for suitability or aptitude.</Hint>
