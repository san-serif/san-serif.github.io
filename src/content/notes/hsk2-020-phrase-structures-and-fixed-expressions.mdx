You’ve been studying Chinese for a few months, and you notice you’re understanding more each week. Use 越来越 before an adjective or verb phrase for a change that continues to grow. For a casual list that doesn’t need every item, finish with 什么的 (“and things like that”).

<CSentenceBlock simplified="天气越来越热了。" traditional="天氣越來越熱了。" pinyin="tiānqì yuèláiyuè rè le." jyutping="tin1 hei3 jyut6 loi4 jyut6 jit6 liu5." english="The weather is getting hotter and hotter." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="他汉语说得越来越好了。" traditional="他漢語說得越來越好了。" pinyin="tā Hànyǔ shuō de yuèláiyuè hǎo le." jyutping="taa1 hon3 jyu5 syut3 dak1 jyut6 loi4 jyut6 hou2 liu5." english="His Chinese is getting better and better." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我买了水果、面包什么的。" traditional="我買了水果、麵包什麼的。" pinyin="wǒ mǎi le shuǐguǒ, miànbāo shénme de." jyutping="ngo5 maai5 liu5 seoi2 gwo2, min6 baau1 sam6 mo1 dik1." english="I bought fruit, bread, and things like that." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="周末我们去公园、看电影什么的。" traditional="週末我們去公園、看電影什麼的。" pinyin="zhōumò wǒmen qù gōngyuán, kàn diànyǐng shénme de." jyutping="zau1 mut6 ngo5 mun4 heoi3 gung1 jyun4, hon3 din6 jing2 sam6 mo1 dik1." english="At the weekend we go to the park, see a film, and things like that." />

<Hint type="hint">越来越 describes an ongoing change: it’s becoming more and more a certain way. 什么的 comes after examples in a list, so the listener understands there are other similar things you haven’t named.</Hint>
