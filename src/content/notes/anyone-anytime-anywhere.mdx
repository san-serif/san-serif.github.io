Attach でも to a question word, 誰, どこ, いつ, 何, and it stops being a question. 誰でも means "anyone," どこでも means "anywhere," 何でも means "anything," the answer becomes every possible answer at once.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="何[なん]でもいいです。" romaji="Nan demo ii desu." english="Anything is fine." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="今[いま]はどこでも写真[しゃしん]が撮[と]れます。" romaji="Ima wa doko demo shashin ga toremasu." english="These days you can take photos anywhere." />

<Hint>This is the same でも that means "but" elsewhere, doing a completely different job here. Context tells them apart instantly: でも alone at the start of a sentence means "but," でも glued onto a question word means "any-." The two never get confused in practice despite looking identical.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="子供[こども]でも買[か]えるから、この本[ほん]は人気[にんき]があります。" romaji="Kodomo demo kaeru kara, kono hon wa ninki ga arimasu." english="This book is popular because even a child can buy it." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="今[いま]は誰[だれ]とでも知[し]り合[あ]えます。" romaji="Ima wa dare to demo shiriaemasu." english="These days you can get to know anyone." />

<Hint>でも also attaches directly to an ordinary noun to mean "even," not just to question words. 子供でも (even a child) makes a claim stronger by naming the least likely case, if even a child can do it, anyone can. Other particles slot in before でも without any trouble: 誰とでも is "with anyone," とdoing its usual job right underneath.</Hint>
