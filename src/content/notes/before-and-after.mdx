You check the forecast before work and walk the dog after dinner. 前に takes the dictionary form; あとで takes the past た-form. Together, they make the sequence easy to follow.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="新聞[しんぶん]を読[よ]む前[まえ]に着替[きが]えます。" romaji="Shinbun o yomu mae ni kigaemasu." english="I get dressed before reading the newspaper." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="花[はな]に水[みず]をやった後[あと]で犬[いぬ]の散歩[さんぽ]をします。" romaji="Hana ni mizu o yatta ato de inu no sanpo o shimasu." english="I water the flowers, then walk the dog." />

<Hint type="hint">前に always takes the dictionary form even in a past-tense sentence, 食べる前に行きました, "I went before eating," never 食べた前に. あとで works the opposite way, always the past た-form, even when the whole sentence describes a habit or the future, 食べた後で寝ます.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="会社[かいしゃ]に行[い]く前[まえ]に天気予報[てんきよほう]を見[み]ます。" romaji="Kaisha ni iku mae ni tenki yohou o mimasu." english="I check the weather forecast before going to the office." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="会社[かいしゃ]に着[つ]いた後[あと]でメールをチェックします。" romaji="Kaisha ni tsuita ato de meeru o chekku shimasu." english="I check my email after arriving at the office." />

<Hint type="hint">Attached to a noun instead of a verb, both just need の between them, 授業の前に, パーティーの後で, "before class," "after the party," a much simpler pattern than the verb forms above.</Hint>

In formal announcements, noun + に先立[さきだ]ち means “prior to / before,” usually for an important event: 開会に先立ち, “before the opening.” Noun + 後[ご]の + noun describes the period or state after an event, as in 退院後の生活, “life after discharge.”

<JSentenceBlock furigana="開会[かいかい]に先立[さきだ]ち、主催者[しゅさいしゃ]から説明[せつめい]があります。" romaji="Kaikai ni sakidachi, shusai-sha kara setsumei ga arimasu." english="Before the opening, the organizers will give an explanation." />

一旦[いったん] means “once” or “for now” and marks a pause or first step before what follows: 一旦ここで休んで、あとで再開しましょう, “Let's pause here for now and resume later.”

<Hint type="insight">に先立ち is common in formal schedules and event notices. 一旦 is useful in everyday planning when you want to pause, check something, or finish one step before moving on.</Hint>
