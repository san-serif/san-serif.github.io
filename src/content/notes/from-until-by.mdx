から marks a starting point, まで marks an ending point, and together they bracket a range exactly like "from" and "until" do in English.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="六時[ろくじ]から七時[しちじ]までテニスをします。" romaji="Rokuji kara shichiji made tenisu o shimasu." english="I play tennis from six to seven." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="東京[とうきょう]から京都[きょうと]まで新幹線[しんかんせん]で行[い]きます。" romaji="Toukyou kara Kyouto made shinkansen de ikimasu." english="I go from Tokyo to Kyoto by shinkansen." />

<Hint>から・まで works on both time and place with no change in shape, unlike English switching between "from 6 to 7" and "from Tokyo to Kyoto" without a second thought. Japanese uses the identical pair either way.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="電話[でんわ]するまで家[いえ]にいてください。" romaji="Denwa suru made ie ni ite kudasai." english="Please stay home until I call." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="明日[あした]までに宿題[しゅくだい]を出[だ]してください。" romaji="Ashita made ni shukudai o dashite kudasai." english="Please turn in your homework by tomorrow." />

<Hint>まで means the action continues all the way up to that point, staying home doesn't stop early. までに means the action happens once, somewhere before that point, the homework could be turned in today, it just can't be later than tomorrow. Mixing them up is a common trap: 明日まで宿題を出してください would mean "keep turning in homework continuously until tomorrow," which doesn't make sense for a one-time action like handing something in.</Hint>
