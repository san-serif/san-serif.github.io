You meet a friend after the holidays. “How have you been?” can be a casual question, “Let’s eat” can be a casual suggestion, and “You look well” can stay an impression rather than a claim about how they feel.

### Ask and Suggest Casually

Verb stem + -니? makes a familiar question: 가니?, 먹니? Past questions use -았/었니? Adjectives can use -(으)니?, with shorter -니? forms also heard. Verb stem + -자 suggests doing something together: 먹자, 걷자. These endings belong with close friends or someone younger in an established relationship.

<SentenceBlock polite="방학 잘 보냈어요?" politeRom="banghak jal bonaesseoyo?" casual="방학 잘 보냈니?" casualRom="banghak jal bonaenni?" english="Did you have a good holiday?" />

<SentenceBlock polite="우리 같이 점심 먹어요." politeRom="uri gachi jeomsim meogeoyo." casual="우리 같이 점심 먹자." casualRom="uri gachi jeomsim meokja." english="Let’s have lunch together." />

### Give a Visual Impression

Adjective -아/어 form + 보이다 means look or seem that way: 피곤해 보이다, 좋아 보이다. Irregular adjectives follow their -아/어 forms: 바쁘다 → 바빠 보여요, 춥다 → 추워 보여요. For a noun comparison, use noun + 처럼 보여요.

<SentenceBlock polite="오늘 기분이 좋아 보여요." politeRom="oneul gibuni joa boyeoyo." casual="오늘 기분이 좋아 보여." casualRom="oneul gibuni joa boyeo." english="You look happy today." />

<SentenceBlock polite="이 소파는 편안해 보여요." politeRom="i sopaneun pyeonanhae boyeoyo." casual="이 소파는 편안해 보여." casualRom="i sopaneun pyeonanhae boyeo." english="This sofa looks comfortable." />

<Hint type="hint">맛있어 보여요 is your impression from appearance. 맛있어요 gives the judgement directly. With someone’s health or feelings, the impression form lets them tell you how things actually are.</Hint>
