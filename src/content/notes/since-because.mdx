ので attaches to the plain form and gives a reason, much like から, but softer and less assertive, common when explaining yourself politely or asking permission.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="病院[びょういん]に行[い]くので、今日[きょう]、休[やす]んでもいいでしょうか。" romaji="Byouin ni iku node, kyou, yasunde mo ii deshou ka." english="Since I have to go to the hospital, would it be alright if I took today off?" />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="頭[あたま]が痛[いた]いので、今日[きょう]、休[やす]んでもいいでしょうか。" romaji="Atama ga itai node, kyou, yasunde mo ii deshou ka." english="Since I have a headache, would it be alright if I took today off?" />

<Hint>から states a reason as a fact, sometimes bluntly, while ので frames it as a shared, reasonable explanation, which is why it shows up so often right before a polite request like てもいいでしょうか. の-adjectives and nouns take な before ので, just as they do before の itself: 好きなので, 今日なので.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="会社[かいしゃ]まで1時間[いちじかん]掛[か]かるので、疲[つか]れます。" romaji="Kaisha made ichijikan kakaru node, tsukaremasu." english="Since it takes an hour to get to the office, I get tired." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="すみません、月曜日[げつようび]にテストがあるので、ちょっと。。。" romaji="Sumimasen, getsuyoubi ni tesuto ga aru node, chotto..." english="Sorry, since I have a test on Monday, it's a bit..." />

<Hint>Trailing off after ので, leaving ちょっと。。。 unfinished, is a very natural way to decline an invitation without spelling out a flat no, letting the reason imply the refusal on its own.</Hint>
