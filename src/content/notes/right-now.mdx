-고 있다 is the same -고 from [날씨가 어때요?](/notes/hows-the-weather)'s "summer is hot and winter is cold," now paired with 있다. Literally it reads as "doing [verb] and existing in that state," which is exactly what a progressive tense describes.

<SentenceBlock formal="지금 뭐 하고 있습니까?" formalRom="jigeum mwo hago isseumnikka?" polite="지금 뭐 하고 있어요?" politeRom="jigeum mwo hago isseoyo?" casual="지금 뭐 하고 있어?" casualRom="jigeum mwo hago isseo?" english="What are you doing right now?" />

<SentenceBlock formal="요리하고 있습니다." formalRom="yorihago isseumnida." polite="요리하고 있어요." politeRom="yorihago isseoyo." casual="요리하고 있어." casualRom="yorihago isseo." english="I am cooking." />

<Hint>저는 요리해요 states a general fact, I cook, as a hobby or a habit. 저는 요리하고 있어요 means it's happening this exact moment. English draws the same line between "I cook" and "I'm cooking"; Korean just makes the grammar do more of the work.</Hint>

<SentenceBlock formal="음악을 듣고 있습니다." formalRom="eumageul deutgo isseumnida." polite="음악을 듣고 있어요." politeRom="eumageul deutgo isseoyo." casual="음악을 듣고 있어." casualRom="eumageul deutgo isseo." english="I am listening to music." />

<SentenceBlock formal="친구를 기다리고 있습니다." formalRom="chingureul gidarigo isseumnida." polite="친구를 기다리고 있어요." politeRom="chingureul gidarigo isseoyo." casual="친구를 기다리고 있어." casualRom="chingureul gidarigo isseo." english="I am waiting for a friend." />

<SentenceBlock formal="누가 메시지를 보내고 있습니까?" formalRom="nuga mesijireul bonaego isseumnikka?" polite="누가 메시지를 보내고 있어요?" politeRom="nuga mesijireul bonaego isseoyo?" casual="누가 메시지를 보내고 있어?" casualRom="nuga mesijireul bonaego isseo?" english="Who is sending a message?" />

<SentenceBlock formal="마리 씨가 메시지를 보내고 있습니다." formalRom="mari ssiga mesijireul bonaego isseumnida." polite="마리 씨가 메시지를 보내고 있어요." politeRom="mari ssiga mesijireul bonaego isseoyo." casual="마리 씨가 메시지를 보내고 있어." casualRom="mari ssiga mesijireul bonaego isseo." english="Marie is sending a message." />

<Hint>Wear-verbs from [입다 vs 신다](/notes/dressed-for-it) get genuinely ambiguous with -고 있다. 티셔츠를 입고 있어요 can mean "I'm putting a t-shirt on right now" or simply "I'm wearing a t-shirt" as a state, and only context tells you which. That's because clothing verbs describe both the act of putting something on and the resulting state of having it on.</Hint>
