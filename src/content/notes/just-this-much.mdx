だけ attaches directly to a noun, quantity, or particle and marks a plain limit, "only this much, nothing more." Unlike some other "only" words you'll meet later, だけ carries no judgment at all, it's just a neutral fact about quantity.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="野菜[やさい]だけ食[た]べました。" romaji="Yasai dake tabemashita." english="I only ate vegetables." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="日本語[にほんご]が少[すこ]しだけわかります。" romaji="Nihongo ga sukoshi dake wakarimasu." english="I only understand a little Japanese." />

<Hint>だけ works with either a positive or negative verb, whichever the sentence needs, and it just states a limit without implying complaint or surprise the way some other "only" expressions do later on.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="友達[ともだち]が一人[ひとり]だけいます。" romaji="Tomodachi ga hitori dake imasu." english="I have exactly one friend." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="ビールを一本[いっぽん]だけ飲[の]みました。" romaji="Biiru o ippon dake nomimashita." english="I only drank one beer." />

<Hint>だけ can also attach after a particle, 田中さんにだけ会いました, "I only met with Tanaka," stacking neatly onto に, と, or から without changing their meaning, it just narrows the scope down to that one thing.</Hint>
