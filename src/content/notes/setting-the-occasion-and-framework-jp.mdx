These formal patterns show how an event is framed: に際して marks an important occasion, の下で names the conditions or authority surrounding an action, を中心に identifies a focus, を巡って introduces a contested topic, and に沿って means following a plan or guideline. They share a formal tone but do different jobs.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="開店[かいてん]に際[さい]して、近所[きんじょ]の皆[みな]さんに挨拶[あいさつ]しました。" romaji="Kaiten ni saishite, kinjo no minasan ni aisatsu shimashita." english="On the occasion of opening the shop, we greeted everyone in the neighborhood." />

に際して is used at the point of an important event or undertaking. It commonly follows a noun, or a dictionary-form verb, and sounds more formal than 時に. It introduces what someone does in preparation for or in connection with that occasion.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="専門家[せんもんか]を中心[ちゅうしん]に、対策[たいさく]チームが作[つく]られました。" romaji="Senmonka o chuushin ni, taisaku chiimu ga tsukuraremashita." english="A response team was formed with experts at its center." />

を中心に places a person, place, or activity at the center of a group or effort. の下で marks the conditions, rules, or person under whose authority something happens: 新しい制度の下で働く, “work under the new system.”

<Hint type="insight">In announcements, に際して often appears with a milestone such as a move, opening, or start of service. In meetings and reports, を中心に tells readers where the main activity is happening, while を巡って signals a subject that people are discussing or disputing.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="安全[あんぜん]の基準[きじゅん]に沿[そ]って、設備[せつび]を点検[てんけん]します。" romaji="Anzen no kijun ni sotte, setsubi o tenken shimasu." english="We inspect the equipment in accordance with the safety standards." />

に沿って means to follow a route, policy, plan, or set of guidelines. を巡って instead puts a topic at the center of discussion or conflict: 予算を巡って意見が分かれた, “opinions differed over the budget.”

<Hint type="hint">Choose by the relationship: an occasion に際して; conditions or authority の下で; central focus を中心に; topic under discussion を巡って; a plan or guideline に沿って. に沿って can be literal, as in following a road, or figurative, as in following a policy.</Hint>
