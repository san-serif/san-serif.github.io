Your friend says they’re heading to the concert, and you check, “You’re going too?” In conversation, Chinese can turn an ordinary statement into a yes-or-no question with a rising, questioning voice. The words stay in statement order; the situation and intonation tell your listener you’re asking.

<CSentenceBlock simplified="你也去看演唱会？" traditional="你也去看演唱會？" pinyin="nǐ yě qù kàn yǎnchànghuì?" jyutping="nei5 jaa5 heoi3 hon3 jin2 coeng3 wui6?" english="You’re going to the concert too?" />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="他已经到了？" traditional="他已經到了？" pinyin="tā yǐjīng dào le?" jyutping="taa1 ji5 ging1 dou3 liu5?" english="He’s already arrived?" />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="这么便宜？" traditional="這麼便宜？" pinyin="zhème piányi?" jyutping="ze2 mo1 pin4 ji4?" english="It’s that cheap?" />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="你没告诉她？" traditional="你沒告訴她？" pinyin="nǐ méi gàosu tā?" jyutping="nei5 mut6 gou3 sou3 taa1?" english="You didn’t tell her?" />

<Hint type="hint">Use this in a conversation when you’re surprised or checking what you just heard. For a neutral, carefully formed question, use 吗 or a question word. The same statement with a different voice can sound like a real question or an echo of surprise.</Hint>
