ようと思う attaches to a verb's volitional form, the "let's" form you met in ましょう's plain equivalent, plus と思う, and expresses a decision or intention, "I'm thinking I'll do X."

<JSentenceBlock furigana="水曜日[すいようび]は、車[くるま]を洗[あら]おうと思[おも]う。" romaji="Suiyoubi wa, kuruma o araou to omou." english="On Wednesday, I'm thinking I'll wash the car." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="木曜日[もくようび]は、ジムで走[はし]ろうと思[おも]う。" romaji="Mokuyoubi wa, jimu de hashirou to omou." english="On Thursday, I'm thinking I'll go run at the gym." />

<Hint>洗う becomes 洗おう, 走る becomes 走ろう, する becomes しよう, the volitional form built the same way as the plain equivalent of ましょう. と思う on top of it frames the plan as something you've just decided, in-the-moment, rather than a long-settled commitment.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="火曜日[かようび]は、両親[りょうしん]に電話[でんわ]をかけようと思[おも]う。" romaji="Kayoubi wa, ryoushin ni denwa o kakeyou to omou." english="On Tuesday, I'm thinking I'll call my parents." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="神戸[こうべ]を観光[かんこう]しようと思[おも]っています。" romaji="Koube o kankou shiyou to omotte imasu." english="I'm planning to go sightseeing in Kobe." />

<Hint>つもり states a settled plan, often one you've held for a while. ようと思う leans more spontaneous, "I've just decided," and ようと思っています, with ている, softens it into an ongoing intention you've been considering rather than a decision made on the spot.</Hint>
