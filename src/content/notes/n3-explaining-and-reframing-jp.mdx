These expressions explain or refine meaning. というのは introduces a definition or reason for a preceding term or statement. ということだ can report what someone said or draw a conclusion (“that means…”). つまり, 要するに, and 即ち restate the point more simply; 即ち is more formal. したがって introduces a logical consequence (“therefore”), especially in formal explanation; そうすると and そこで can connect an action or cause to what followed. その結果 introduces an outcome. とおり means “as / in accordance with” a stated model or instruction. というのは事実だ emphasizes that a statement is factual. 前者 and 後者 refer back to the first and second items in a comparison. というより corrects or refines a label: “rather than X, it's more like Y.” The existing N4 lesson on という covers the basic quotation and naming pattern; this lesson focuses on discourse use.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="留学[りゅうがく]とは、外国[がいこく]で学[まな]ぶということです。" romaji="Ryuugaku to wa, gaikoku de manabu to iu koto desu." english="Study abroad means studying in another country." />

というのは can introduce what a word or prior statement means. It is often followed by ことだ or a plain explanation. ということだ can also pass along information: 先生は来ないということだ, “I hear the teacher isn't coming.” Context distinguishes reported speech from a conclusion.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="電車[でんしゃ]が止[と]まっています。つまり、到着[とうちゃく]が遅[おく]れるということです。" romaji="Densha ga tomatte imasu. Tsumari, touchaku ga okureru to iu koto desu." english="The train has stopped. In other words, arrival will be delayed." />

つまり explicitly summarizes an inference or restates an explanation in a clearer form. というより revises a description: 親切というより、おせっかいだ, “rather than kind, they're meddlesome.”

<JSentenceBlock furigana="彼[かれ]は上司[じょうし]というより、相談相手[そうだんあいて]のような人[ひと]です。" romaji="Kare wa joushi to iu yori, soudan aite no you na hito desu." english="Rather than just a supervisor, he's like someone I can consult." />

というより does not simply negate the first term. It signals that the second description is more accurate or revealing.

<Hint type="insight">In conversation, つまり can check shared understanding: “So, if I understand correctly...” というより can gently adjust a word choice without insisting the first description was completely wrong.</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">Define or explain a term: というのは. Report or conclude: ということだ. Summarize: つまり. Replace an imprecise label with a better one: というより.</Hint>
