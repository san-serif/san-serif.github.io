Someone has left the window open, so you mention its current state. Later, your friend asks whether you’ve ever visited Hong Kong. Chinese uses different markers for these ideas: 着 keeps a state in view, while 过 tells about past experience.

<CSentenceBlock simplified="窗户开着，外面很冷。" traditional="窗戶開著，外面很冷。" pinyin="chuānghu kāi zhe, wàimiàn hěn lěng." jyutping="coeng1 wu6 hoi1 zoek6, ngoi6 min6 han2 laang5." english="The window is open, and it’s cold outside." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="她穿着一件红色外套。" traditional="她穿著一件紅色外套。" pinyin="tā chuān zhe yí jiàn hóngsè wàitào." jyutping="taa1 cyun1 zoek6 jat1 gin6 hung4 sik1 ngoi6 tou3." english="She’s wearing a red coat." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我去过香港两次。" traditional="我去過香港兩次。" pinyin="wǒ qù guo Xiānggǎng liǎng cì." jyutping="ngo5 heoi3 gwo3 Hoeng1 gong2 loeng5 ci3." english="I’ve been to Hong Kong twice." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="你吃过北京烤鸭吗？" traditional="你吃過北京烤鴨嗎？" pinyin="nǐ chī guo Běijīng kǎoyā ma?" jyutping="nei5 hek3 gwo3 Bak1 ging1 haau1 aap3 maa3?" english="Have you ever tried Beijing roast duck?" />

<Hint type="hint">着 describes the state you can observe now; it doesn’t usually tell you when that state began. 过 asks whether an experience has happened at least once before. It commonly follows the verb and can pair with a frequency such as 两次.</Hint>
