A friend offers you kimchi, and you decide to try it: 김치를 먹어 봐요. Attach -아/어 보다 to frame an action as an experiment. In the past, 먹어 봤어요? asks whether someone has tried it before.

<SentenceBlock formal="제주도에 가 봤습니다." formalRom="jejudo-e ga bwatseumnida." polite="제주도에 가 봤어요." politeRom="jejudo-e ga bwasseoyo." casual="제주도에 가 봤어." casualRom="jejudo-e ga bwasseo." english="I've been to Jeju Island." />

<SentenceBlock formal="비빔밥을 먹어 봤습니까?" formalRom="bibimbabeul meogeo bwatseumnikka?" polite="비빔밥을 먹어 봤어요?" politeRom="bibimbabeul meogeo bwasseoyo?" casual="비빔밥 먹어 봤어?" casualRom="bibimbap meogeo bwasseo?" english="Have you tried bibimbap?" />

<Hint type="hint">보세요 can mean “please look” when 보다 stands alone, but 먹어 보세요 means “try eating.” Here 보다 is attached to another verb and signals trying it out, rather than literally looking.</Hint>

<SentenceBlock formal="이 노래를 한번 들어 보십시오." formalRom="i noraereul hanbeon deureo bosipsio." polite="이 노래를 한번 들어 보세요." politeRom="i noraereul hanbeon deureo boseyo." casual="이 노래 한번 들어 봐." casualRom="i norae hanbeon deureo bwa." english="Try listening to this song." />

<Hint type="insight">In real life, 한번 (“once”) often appears with a suggestion to make it sound easy and low-pressure: 한번 먹어 보세요, “Give it a try.” It doesn't always mean exactly one time.</Hint>
