〜ている attaches to a verb's て-form and does more work than English's "-ing." It covers an action happening right now, a habit, and a state that's been true since some point in the past, all with the same ending.

### Happening Right Now

<JSentenceBlock furigana="大輔[だいすけ]さんは宿題[しゅくだい]をしています。" romaji="Daisuke-san wa shukudai o shiteimasu." english="Daisuke is doing homework." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="沙耶[さや]さんは音楽[おんがく]を聞[き]いています。" romaji="Saya-san wa ongaku o kiiteimasu." english="Saya is listening to music." />

### A Habit

<JSentenceBlock furigana="毎日[まいにち]、朝[あさ]ご飯[はん]を食[た]べています。" romaji="Mainichi, asagohan o tabeteimasu." english="I eat breakfast every day." />

<Hint>Nothing in this sentence marks it as happening this exact second, 毎日 (every day) is what pushes the meaning from "right now" to "as a rule." Same ending, different meaning, entirely dependent on context.</Hint>

### A Lasting State

<JSentenceBlock furigana="車[くるま]の会社[かいしゃ]に勤[つと]めています。" romaji="Kuruma no kaisha ni tsutometeimasu." english="I work at a car company." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="結婚[けっこん]しています。" romaji="Kekkon shiteimasu." english="I'm married." />

<Hint>結婚しています doesn't mean "I am marrying right now," it means the marrying already happened and the resulting state is still true. Verbs describing a one-time change, 結婚する (to marry), 住む (to live somewhere), 知る (to find out) all shift to ている to describe the state that change left behind, not the instant it occurred.</Hint>

### Describing Someone

<JSentenceBlock furigana="あの黒[くろ]い帽子[ぼうし]を被[かぶ]っている人[ひと]は誰[だれ]ですか。" romaji="Ano kuroi boushi o kabutteiru hito wa dare desu ka." english="Who's the person wearing that black hat?" />

<Hint>被っている sits directly in front of 人 as a modifier, exactly the same modifying position -ㄴ/은 or -는 would take in Korean, or a relative clause in English ("the person who is wearing"). This is one of the most common ways to describe someone by what they're wearing or holding at the moment.</Hint>
