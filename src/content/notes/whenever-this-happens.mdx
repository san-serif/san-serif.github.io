と attaches to the plain form and links a trigger to a result that reliably follows, "whenever X happens, Y happens." Unlike たら, which can describe a one-time hypothetical, と describes something closer to a natural law, a habit, or an automatic mechanism.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="お酒[さけ]を飲[の]みすぎるといつも気分[きぶん]が悪[わる]くなります。" romaji="Osake o nomisugiru to itsumo kibun ga waruku narimasu." english="Whenever I drink too much, I always feel sick." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="６時[ろくじ]になると、外[そと]の電気[でんき]が点[つ]きます。" romaji="Rokuji ni naru to, soto no denki ga tsukimasu." english="When it turns 6:00, the outside light comes on." />

<Hint>と can't be used for a one-time invitation or suggestion the way たら can, 6時になると always describes something automatic or habitual, a light on a timer, a rule that always holds, never a single, freely chosen event.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="玄関[げんかん]のドアを閉[し]めると、鍵[かぎ]が掛[か]かります。" romaji="Genkan no doa o shimeru to, kagi ga kakarimasu." english="When you close the front door, it locks automatically." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="このボタンを押[お]すと、選択[せんたく]が始[はじ]まります。" romaji="Kono botan o osu to, sentaku ga hajimarimasu." english="When you press this button, the wash cycle starts." />

<Hint>This mechanical, automatic sense is why と shows up constantly in instructions and manuals, "press this, and that happens," a direct, reliable cause and effect with no room for exceptions.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="パソコンを使[つか]いすぎると、目[め]が痛[いた]くなります。" romaji="Pasokon o tsukaisugiru to, me ga itaku narimasu." english="If I use the computer too much, my eyes start to hurt." />

<Hint>と also works well for personal patterns you can count on, not just machines, お酒を飲むと顔が赤くなる, "whenever I drink, my face turns red," a reliable cause-and-effect relationship about yourself.</Hint>
