You spent all afternoon cleaning, and the room is messy again: 白白忙了一场 (“all that work for nothing”). The result went the other way than expected: 反倒. And if the facts are plain, 分明 puts that certainty into the sentence. These adverbs carry the speaker’s reaction to what happened.

### Show That an Effort Was Wasted

白白 comes before an action or result that brought no benefit. It often appears with 等、浪费 or 失去.

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我们白白等了两个小时，最后还是没见到他。" traditional="我們白白等了兩個小時，最後還是沒見到他。" pinyin="wǒmen báibái děng le liǎng ge xiǎoshí, zuìhòu háishi méi jiàndào tā." jyutping="ngo5 mun4 baak6 baak6 dang2 liu5 loeng5 go3 siu2 si4, zeoi3 hau6 waan4 si6 mut6 gin3 dou3 taa1" english="We waited two hours for nothing and still did not see him." />

<Hint type="hint">白白 describes a wasted effort or loss; here it does not mean “white.”</Hint>

### Point Out the Unexpected Opposite

反倒 introduces a result that runs against what the listener might expect from the first clause. It often means “instead” or “on the contrary.”

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我帮了他，他反倒怪我。" traditional="我幫了他，他反倒怪我。" pinyin="wǒ bāngle tā, tā fǎndào guài wǒ." jyutping="ngo5 bong1 liu5 taa1, taa1 faan2 dou2 gwaai3 ngo5." english="I helped him, but he blamed me instead." />

<Hint type="hint">反倒 marks a surprising turn, not just any next action.</Hint>

### Emphasize What Is Plain to See

分明 means “clearly” or “obviously.” It can modify something the speaker clearly perceived or stress that a fact is unmistakable.

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我分明听见有人叫我。" traditional="我分明聽見有人叫我。" pinyin="wǒ fēnmíng tīngjiàn yǒu rén jiào wǒ." jyutping="ngo5 fan1 ming4 teng1 gin3 jau5 jan4 giu3 ngo5." english="I clearly heard someone call me." />

<Hint type="hint">分明 often carries insistence: the evidence is clear, even if someone else denies it.</Hint>

<Hint type="insight">白白 points to effort with no payoff; 反倒 marks a result that goes against expectation; 分明 tells the listener the evidence seems unmistakable to the speaker.</Hint>
