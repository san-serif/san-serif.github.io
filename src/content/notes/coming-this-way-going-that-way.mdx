Attached to a verb's て-form, くる and いく describe a change unfolding over time, not just physical movement. てくる points the change toward now, something that's been building up to this moment. ていく points it away from now, something that will keep going from here.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="物価[ぶっか]が高[たか]くなってきました。" romaji="Bukka ga takaku natte kimashita." english="Prices have been getting higher (up to now)." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="これからもっと高[たか]くなっていくでしょう。" romaji="Kore kara motto takaku natte iku deshou." english="They'll probably keep getting higher from here on." />

<Hint>Both sentences describe the same trend, rising prices, but てきました looks backward from now, while ていくでしょう looks forward from now. Neither verb is about walking anywhere, くる and いく have shifted entirely into marking the direction of change through time.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="大学[だいがく]に入[はい]ってから、ずっと色々[いろいろ]な本[ほん]を読[よ]んできました。" romaji="Daigaku ni haitte kara, zutto iroiro na hon o yonde kimashita." english="I've been reading all kinds of books ever since I started university." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="卒業[そつぎょう]してからも、色々[いろいろ]な本[ほん]を読[よ]んでいこうと思[おも]います。" romaji="Sotsugyou shite kara mo, iroiro na hon o yonde ikou to omoimasu." english="I plan to keep reading all kinds of books even after I graduate." />

<Hint>てきました reports a habit built up to the present, from university through now. ていこうと思います projects that same habit forward, from now through graduation and beyond, pairing naturally with ようと思う, which names an intention.</Hint>
