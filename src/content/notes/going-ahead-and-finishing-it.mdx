てしまう attaches to a verb's て-form and adds a sense of completion, "go ahead and finish it," "end up doing it." Depending on context, it can sound like determination to wrap something up, or regret over something that already happened and can't be undone.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="資料[しりょう]を集[あつ]めてしまいましょう。" romaji="Shiryou o atsumete shimaimashou." english="Let's go ahead and get all the materials gathered." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="レポートを仕上[しあ]げてしまいましょう。" romaji="Repooto o shiagete shimaimashou." english="Let's go ahead and finish the report completely." />

<Hint>Paired with ましょう, てしまう pushes toward getting something fully done, not halfway, closer to "let's just get this over with" than a plain ましょう alone would sound. The nuance is finality, no loose ends left.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="友達[ともだち]から借[か]りた本[ほん]を無[な]くしてしまいました。" romaji="Tomodachi kara karita hon o nakushite shimaimashita." english="I ended up losing a book I borrowed from a friend." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="大切[たいせつ]な写真[しゃしん]を消[け]してしまいました。" romaji="Taisetsu na shashin o keshite shimaimashita." english="I accidentally ended up deleting an important photo." />

<Hint>Used with a past-tense mishap, てしまいました almost always carries regret, "I went and did it," "it happened and now it's done." Casual speech often contracts てしまう down to ちゃう, なくしちゃった, a form you'll hear constantly in spoken Japanese.</Hint>
