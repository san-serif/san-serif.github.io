The N4 lesson on ても introduced “even if.” At N3, the same idea becomes more emphatic: どんなに and いくら push a condition to an extreme (“no matter how much”), while たとえ explicitly asks the listener to imagine a case (“even if”). The result is presented as unchanged despite that condition.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="どんなに忙[いそが]しくても、一日[いちにち]に一度[いちど]は運動[うんどう]します。" romaji="Donna ni isogashikute mo, ichinichi ni ichido wa undou shimasu." english="No matter how busy I am, I exercise at least once a day." />

どんなに means “no matter how” and emphasizes degree. It commonly appears with an adjective or adverb before ても: どんなに高くても, “no matter how expensive”; どんなに遅くても, “no matter how late.”

<JSentenceBlock furigana="いくら考[かんが]えても、答[こた]えが分[わ]かりません。" romaji="Ikura kangaete mo, kotae ga wakarimasen." english="No matter how much I think about it, I can't figure out the answer." />

いくら often emphasizes amount or effort: how much you try, ask, or spend. Verbs and い-adjectives take ても (いくら考えても, いくら安くても); nouns and な-adjectives take でも (いくらお金があっても, いくら便利でも). The second clause tells us that the amount or degree doesn't change the outcome.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="たとえ失敗[しっぱい]しても、もう一度[いちど]挑戦[ちょうせん]します。" romaji="Tatoe shippai shite mo, mou ichido chousen shimasu." english="Even if I fail, I'll try one more time." />

たとえ marks the condition as hypothetical or deliberately imagined. It often pairs with ても and makes a statement sound resolute: the speaker has considered the possibility and says the result will still stand.

<Hint type="insight">In real life, たとえ〜ても is useful for expressing a firm decision or reassurance: たとえ時間がかかっても、自分でやります, “Even if it takes time, I'll do it myself.” どんなに and いくら are more natural when you are emphasizing degree or repeated effort.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="必要[ひつよう]な書類[しょるい]さえあれば、申請[しんせい]できます。" romaji="Hitsuyou na shorui sae areba, shinsei dekimasu." english="As long as you have the necessary documents, you can apply." />

さえ〜ば is the other side of the condition: it names the one minimum requirement that is enough for the result. Here, having the necessary documents is sufficient. With a verb, use さえすれば or さえ〜ば: 連絡さえすれば, “as long as you contact me.”

<Hint type="hint">Think of these as two different moves. どんなに〜ても, いくら〜ても, and たとえ〜ても say “even this extreme condition won't change the result.” さえ〜ば says “this one condition is enough to make the result possible.” The familiar plain ても works without an intensifier when no extra emphasis is needed.</Hint>
