You and your friend both like tea, but neither likes coffee. も can mark both sides of a positive sentence or both sides of a negative one: the same particle, with the verb polarity setting the reading.

The expanded も～ば～も form also means “both…and”: 経験もあれば知識もある, “they have both experience and knowledge.” It uses the ば conditional form as a balanced link, and the second も completes the pair.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="直樹[なおき]さんは寿司[すし]も刺身[さしみ]も好[す]きです。" romaji="Naoki-san wa sushi mo sashimi mo suki desu." english="Naoki likes both sushi and sashimi." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="沙耶[さや]さんはテニスもゴルフもします。" romaji="Saya-san wa tenisu mo gorufu mo shimasu." english="Saya plays both tennis and golf." />

<Hint type="hint">寿司も刺身も replaces the plain object marker を entirely, listing two items on completely equal footing, distinct from と, which lists items as a fixed group, "sushi and sashimi" as one set, while も〜も treats each one separately, "sushi, and sashimi too."</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="特[とく]に美味[おい]しくも不味[まず]くもないですね。" romaji="Toku ni oishiku mo mazuku mo nai desu ne." english="It's not especially delicious or bad, really." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="特[とく]に賑[にぎ]やかでも静[しず]かでもないですね。" romaji="Toku ni nigiyaka demo shizuka demo nai desu ne." english="It's not especially lively or quiet." />

<Hint type="hint">い-adjectives drop い for くも, 美味しい becomes 美味しくも; な-adjectives and nouns take でも, 賑やかでも. Paired with ない at the end and 特に (particularly) up front, this becomes a natural way to describe something as thoroughly unremarkable, neither one extreme nor the other.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="新幹線[しんかんせん]でもバスでもないです。車[くるま]のはずです。" romaji="Shinkansen demo basu demo nai desu. Kuruma no hazu desu." english="It's neither the bullet train nor the bus. It must be the car." />

<Hint type="hint">Xでも Yでもない rules out two possibilities at once, "it's neither X nor Y," often followed by a confident conclusion about what it must be instead, pairing naturally with はず, which you already know.</Hint>

To add another fact across sentences, また works like “moreover / also.” And for a more formal “is also” with nouns or な-adjectives, use でもある; in a balanced list, use でもあり before the second item.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="この町[まち]は歴史[れきし]があり、また自然[しぜん]も豊[ゆた]かです。" romaji="Kono machi wa rekishi ga ari, mata shizen mo yutaka desu." english="This town has a rich history, and it also has abundant nature." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="この町[まち]は観光地[かんこうち]でもあり、学生[がくせい]の町[まち]でもあります。" romaji="Kono machi wa kankouchi demo ari, gakusei no machi demo arimasu." english="This town is both a tourist destination and a student town." />

<Hint type="insight">また is handy when you want to add a second point in a presentation or explanation. でもある is more compact and formal when naming two roles or qualities of the same person or thing.</Hint>
