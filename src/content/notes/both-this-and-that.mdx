Attaching も to two nouns in a row, in place of the particles they'd normally take, links them together evenly, "both X and Y." With a positive verb it means "both"; with a negative verb it means "neither."

<JSentenceBlock furigana="直樹[なおき]さんは寿司[すし]も刺身[さしみ]も好[す]きです。" romaji="Naoki-san wa sushi mo sashimi mo suki desu." english="Naoki likes both sushi and sashimi." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="沙耶[さや]さんはテニスもゴルフもします。" romaji="Saya-san wa tenisu mo gorufu mo shimasu." english="Saya plays both tennis and golf." />

<Hint>寿司も刺身も replaces the plain object marker を entirely, listing two items on completely equal footing, distinct from と, which lists items as a fixed group, "sushi and sashimi" as one set, while も〜も treats each one separately, "sushi, and sashimi too."</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="特[とく]に美味[おい]しくも不味[まず]くもないですね。" romaji="Toku ni oishiku mo mazuku mo nai desu ne." english="It's not especially delicious or bad, really." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="特[とく]に賑[にぎ]やかでも静[しず]かでもないですね。" romaji="Toku ni nigiyaka demo shizuka demo nai desu ne." english="It's not especially lively or quiet." />

<Hint>い-adjectives drop い for くも, 美味しい becomes 美味しくも; な-adjectives and nouns take でも, 賑やかでも. Paired with ない at the end and 特に (particularly) up front, this becomes a natural way to describe something as thoroughly unremarkable, neither one extreme nor the other.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="新幹線[しんかんせん]でもバスでもないです。車[くるま]のはずです。" romaji="Shinkansen demo basu demo nai desu. Kuruma no hazu desu." english="It's neither the bullet train nor the bus. It must be the car." />

<Hint>Xでも Yでもない rules out two possibilities at once, "it's neither X nor Y," often followed by a confident conclusion about what it must be instead, pairing naturally with はず, which you already know.</Hint>
