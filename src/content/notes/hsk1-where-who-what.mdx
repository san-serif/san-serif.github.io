You’re looking for a friend at a busy station. Ask 谁 when the person is missing, 什么 when you need the thing, and 哪儿 or 哪里 when the place is unclear. Keep the question word in the spot where the answer would go; Chinese doesn’t move it to the front.

### Asking Where

<CSentenceBlock simplified="你住在哪里？" traditional="你住在哪裡？" pinyin="Nǐ zhù zài nǎlǐ?" jyutping="nei5 zyu6 zoi6 naa5 leoi5?" english="Where do you live?" />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="学校在哪儿？" traditional="學校在哪兒？" pinyin="Xuéxiào zài nǎr?" jyutping="hok6 haau6 zoi6 naa5 ji4?" english="Where is the school?" />

哪儿 and 哪里 both mean “where.” 在 identifies a location; it can appear before a place in a question or a statement.

### Asking Who and What

<CSentenceBlock simplified="谁在教室里？" traditional="誰在教室裡？" pinyin="Shéi zài jiàoshì lǐ?" jyutping="seoi4 zoi6 gaau3 sat1 leoi5?" english="Who is in the classroom?" />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="这是什么？" traditional="這是什麼？" pinyin="Zhè shì shénme?" jyutping="ze5 si6 sam6 mo1?" english="What is this?" />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="你想喝什么？" traditional="你想喝什麼？" pinyin="Nǐ xiǎng hē shénme?" jyutping="nei5 soeng2 hot3 sam6 mo1?" english="What would you like to drink?" />

### Saying Where Something Is

Position words such as 上、下、里、外、前、后、左、右 usually follow the place or object they describe. Add 边 or 面 to make many of them into location phrases.

<CSentenceBlock simplified="书在桌子上。" traditional="書在桌子上。" pinyin="Shū zài zhuōzi shàng." jyutping="syu1 zoi6 zoek3 zi2 soeng6." english="The book is on the table." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="猫在椅子下面。" traditional="貓在椅子下面。" pinyin="Māo zài yǐzi xiàmiàn." jyutping="maau1 zoi6 ji2 zi2 haa6 min6." english="The cat is under the chair." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="商店在学校左边。" traditional="商店在學校左邊。" pinyin="Shāngdiàn zài xuéxiào zuǒbian." jyutping="soeng1 dim3 zoi6 hok6 haau6 zo2 bin1." english="The shop is to the left of the school." />

<Hint type="hint">Keep the question word in the answer slot: 你住在哪里？ asks “You live where?” in the same order as the statement 你住在北京. A question word does not need 吗.</Hint>

<Hint type="insight">In everyday Mandarin, 哪儿 is especially common in northern speech, while 哪里 is used broadly and often preferred in writing or in regions where 儿化 is less common. You can use either to ask “where”; choose the form that matches the variety you are learning.</Hint>
