The shoes looked right in the shop. After trying them at home, you discovered they were too small. -아/어 보니까 gives the result of trying; -(으)려면 asks what you need to do next.

### What Trying Revealed

Use the -아/어 form plus 보니까: 입어 보니까, 써 보니까. The next clause gives a fact, impression, or feeling learned through the experience. The discovery is often expressed in the past.

<SentenceBlock formal="직접 써 보니까 생각보다 편했습니다." formalRom="jikjeop sseo bonikka saenggakboda pyeonhaetseumnida." polite="직접 써 보니까 생각보다 편했어요." politeRom="jikjeop sseo bonikka saenggakboda pyeonhaesseoyo." casual="직접 써 보니까 생각보다 편했어." casualRom="jikjeop sseo bonikka saenggakboda pyeonhaesseo." english="When I tried using it myself, it was more convenient than I expected." />

### If You Intend to Do It

Attach -려면 after a vowel or ㄹ and -으려면 after another consonant: 바꾸려면, 만들려면, 받으려면. It introduces an intended action and the condition, advice, or procedure needed for it.

<SentenceBlock formal="환불받으려면 영수증이 필요합니다." formalRom="hwanbulbadeuryeomyeon yeongsujeungi piryohamnida." polite="환불받으려면 영수증이 필요해요." politeRom="hwanbulbadeuryeomyeon yeongsujeungi piryohaeyo." casual="환불받으려면 영수증이 필요해." casualRom="hwanbulbadeuryeomyeon yeongsujeungi piryohae." english="You need a receipt to get a refund." />

<SentenceBlock formal="신발을 바꾸려면 어디로 가야 합니까?" formalRom="sinbareul bakkuryeomyeon eodiro gaya hamnikka?" polite="신발을 바꾸려면 어디로 가야 해요?" politeRom="sinbareul bakkuryeomyeon eodiro gaya haeyo?" casual="신발을 바꾸려면 어디로 가야 해?" casualRom="sinbareul bakkuryeomyeon eodiro gaya hae?" english="Where should I go to exchange the shoes?" />

<Hint type="hint">Use -려면 for someone’s intended action. A weather condition needs ordinary -(으)면: 비가 오면. Rain has no intention to carry out a plan.</Hint>
