In Seoul, "What do you do?" isn't just small talk. It's a way to figure out how much respect you owe the person in front of you.

### Essential Greetings

<SentenceBlock formal="안녕히 계십시오" formalRom="annyeonghi gyesipsio" polite="안녕히 계세요" politeRom="annyeonghi gyeseyo" casual="잘 있어" casualRom="jal isseo" english="Goodbye (to someone staying)." />

<Hint>You'll almost never hear 안녕하십니까 outside a news broadcast, a military base, or a very stiff corporate greeting. In daily life Koreans move straight from casual 안녕 to polite 안녕하세요 and mostly skip formal register altogether. Learn it to spot it, not because you'll need it at a cafe.</Hint>

### How to Introduce Yourself

이다 (to be) is the single most-used word in the language, and its shape depends entirely on the sound right before it. If the noun ends in a consonant (has **받침**, batchim), attach **이에요**. If it ends in a vowel, attach **예요** instead, since Korean avoids stacking two vowel sounds back to back.

* Use **이에요** for consonants (e.g., 사람 + 이에요 → **사람이에요**).
* Use **예요** for vowels (e.g., 뭐 + 예요 → **뭐예요**).

<SentenceBlock formal="성함이 어떻게 되십니까?" formalRom="seonghami eotteoke doesimnikka?" polite="이름이 뭐예요?" politeRom="ireumi mwoyeyo?" casual="이름이 뭐야?" casualRom="ireumi mwoya?" english="What is your name?" />

<SentenceBlock formal="직업이 무엇입니까?" formalRom="jigeobi mueosimnikka?" polite="직업이 뭐예요?" politeRom="jigeobi mwoyeyo?" casual="직업이 뭐야?" casualRom="jigeobi mwoya?" english="What is your job?" />

<SentenceBlock formal="어느 나라 사람입니까?" formalRom="eoneu nara saramimnikka?" polite="어느 나라 사람이에요?" politeRom="eoneu nara saramieyo?" casual="어느 나라 사람이야?" casualRom="eoneu nara saramiya?" english="Where are you from?" />

<Hint>Once the context is obvious, Korean drops the subject entirely. If you've already established you're talking about yourself, saying 저는 in every single sentence sounds as unnatural as an English speaker starting every reply with "As for me." Natives just say 학생이에요, not 저는 학생이에요, unless they're contrasting themselves with someone else.</Hint>
