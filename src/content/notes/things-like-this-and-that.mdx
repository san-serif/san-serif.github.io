とか attaches after nouns or plain-form verbs and lists a few casual examples, "things like X and Y." It's looser and more conversational than と, which lists a complete, exact set.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="ミステリーとかホラー小説[しょうせつ]をよく読[よ]みます。" romaji="Misuterii toka horaa shousetsu o yoku yomimasu." english="I often read stuff like mysteries and horror novels." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="ジムとか本屋[ほんや]とかCDショップによく行[い]きます。" romaji="Jimu toka hon'ya toka shii dii shoppu ni yoku ikimasu." english="I often go places like the gym, the bookstore, or a CD shop." />

<Hint>と implies a closed, complete list, "A and B, that's everything." とか implies an open one, "things like A, B, and probably more," which is why it fits so naturally into casual conversation, where you're rarely being exhaustive.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="拓也[たくや]さんは休[やす]みの日[ひ]に、ジムに行[い]くとか泳[およ]ぐとかします。" romaji="Takuya-san wa yasumi no hi ni, jimu ni iku toka oyogu toka shimasu." english="On days off, Takuya does things like go to the gym or swim." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="拓也[たくや]さんは休[やす]みの日[ひ]に、友達[ともだち]と出[で]かけるとか掃除[そうじ]をするとかします。" romaji="Takuya-san wa yasumi no hi ni, tomodachi to dekakeru toka souji o suru toka shimasu." english="On days off, Takuya does things like go out with friends or clean." />

<Hint>とか works on verbs too, always paired with する at the end, Xとか、Yとかする, the verb version of たり〜たりする, a slightly more casual-sounding alternative doing much the same job.</Hint>
