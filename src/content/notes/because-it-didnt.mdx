<JapaneseLessonHead />

なくて attaches to a verb, adjective, or noun's negative stem and gives the reason for a feeling or result that follows, usually something troubling, disappointing, or, less often, a relief.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="空港[くうこう]でスーツケースが出[で]て来[こ]なくて、困[こま]りました。" romaji="Kuukou de suutsukeesu ga dete konakute, komarimashita." english="My suitcase didn't come out at the airport, and I had trouble." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="携帯[けいたい]が使[つか]えなくて、困[こま]りました。" romaji="Keitai ga tsukaenakute, komarimashita." english="My phone wasn't working, and I had trouble." />

<Hint>なくて links the missing thing directly to the feeling it caused, much like なので or のに, except the first clause here is specifically negative. 困りました almost always follows it, naming the trouble that the absence created.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="雨[あめ]が降[ふ]らなくて、よかったです。" romaji="Ame ga furanakute, yokatta desu." english="It didn't rain, and that was a relief." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="人[ひと]がいなくて、静[しず]かでした。" romaji="Hito ga inakute, shizuka deshita." english="There weren't any people, and it was quiet." />

<Hint>Not every なくて sentence is bad news, よかった after it flips the whole thing into relief, "I'm glad this didn't happen." The grammar itself is neutral, it's whatever follows, 困りました or よかったです, that decides the tone.</Hint>
