You’re describing a queue: your friend is second, and there are about a dozen people ahead. Chinese marks an order with 第 before the number. To give a rough count, 多 goes after the number phrase, with its position depending on the measure word.

<CSentenceBlock simplified="这是我第一次来北京。" traditional="這是我第一次來北京。" pinyin="zhè shì wǒ dì yī cì lái Běijīng." jyutping="ze5 si6 ngo5 dai6 jat1 ci3 loi4 Bak1 ging1." english="This is my first time in Beijing." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="她住在第三层。" traditional="她住在第三層。" pinyin="tā zhù zài dì sān céng." jyutping="taa1 zyu6 zoi6 dai6 saam1 cang4." english="She lives on the third floor." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="前面有十多个人。" traditional="前面有十多個人。" pinyin="qiánmiàn yǒu shí duō ge rén." jyutping="cin4 min6 jau5 sap6 do1 go3 jan4." english="There are more than ten people ahead." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我等了一个多小时。" traditional="我等了一個多小時。" pinyin="wǒ děng le yí ge duō xiǎoshí." jyutping="ngo5 dang2 liu5 jat1 go3 do1 siu2 si4." english="I waited for a little over an hour." />

<Hint type="hint">第 comes before the number and turns it into an ordinal: 第一 (“first”). With a whole number plus a measure word, 多 usually follows the measure word (十多个); with units of time or money, it can follow the number (一个多小时).</Hint>
