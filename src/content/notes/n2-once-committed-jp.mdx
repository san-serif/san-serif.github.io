以上, 上は, and からには can all mean “now that” or “since,” when a prior decision or fact creates a responsibility. They set up a consequence, often an intention, obligation, or expectation. This is separate from 以上 meaning “more than,” which uses the same kanji but a different grammar and sentence shape.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="引[ひ]き受[う]けた以上[いじょう]、最後[さいご]まで責任[せきにん]を持[も]ちます。" romaji="Hikiuketa ijou, saigo made sekinin o mochimasu." english="Now that I've taken it on, I'll take responsibility to the end." />

Verb plain form + 以上(は) treats the preceding fact as a premise that entails a commitment or reasonable consequence. It often appears in formal statements and can sound resolute.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="代表[だいひょう]を務[つと]める上[うえ]は、皆[みな]の意見[いけん]を聞[き]くつもりです。" romaji="Daihyou o tsutomeru ue wa, minna no iken o kiku tsumori desu." english="Since I'm serving as the representative, I intend to listen to everyone's views." />

上は follows a decision or role and introduces what follows from it. からには similarly emphasizes the speaker's determination or obligation: やるからには最後までやる, “if I'm going to do it, I'll see it through.” 手前 can mean “given one's position / because one is obligated,” as in 責任者である手前、説明しなければならない, “given that I'm responsible, I have to explain.”

てこそ means “only by doing X” or “not until X,” emphasizing that the result depends on that experience or action: 努力してこそ結果が出る, “only through effort do results appear.”

<Hint type="insight">These forms are useful in promises and statements of resolve: 引き受けたからには責任を持つ, “since I accepted, I'll take responsibility.” They sound stronger than simply stating a future plan because the premise is presented as binding.</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">A premise creating duty or resolve: Vた以上(は), Vる上は, Vるからには. The “more than” reading of 以上 appears with quantities, such as 三年以上, and is not this pattern.</Hint>
