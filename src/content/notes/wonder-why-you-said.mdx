You are unsure how to behave at an unfamiliar event. A friend mentions that another classmate has been before, and you ask to confirm what you heard. Embedded questions organise what you know; -다면서요? checks a reported claim.

### Put a Question Inside a Sentence

Action verbs take -는지, present adjectives -(으)ㄴ지, and nouns -인지 before 알다 or 모르다. Past events use -았/었는지 and future or uncertain choices often use -(으)ㄹ지. Keep a question word when needed: 어디에 있는지, 무엇을 먹을지.

<SentenceBlock polite="언제 인사해야 하는지 잘 모르겠어요." politeRom="eonje insahaeya haneunji jal moreugesseoyo." casual="언제 인사해야 하는지 잘 모르겠어." casualRom="eonje insahaeya haneunji jal moreugesseo." english="I’m not sure when I should greet people." />

<SentenceBlock polite="이 표현이 자연스러운지 알고 싶어요." politeRom="i pyohyeoni jayeonseureounji algo sipeoyo." casual="이 표현이 자연스러운지 알고 싶어." casualRom="i pyohyeoni jayeonseureounji algo sipeo." english="I want to know whether this expression is natural." />

### Check What You Heard

Use -ㄴ다면서요? after a vowel action stem, -는다면서요? after a consonant action stem, -다면서요? after adjectives, and -(이)라면서요? after nouns. Past statements use -았/었다면서요? It often means “I hear that…, is that right?”

<SentenceBlock polite="한국에서 공부했다면서요?" politeRom="hangugeseo gongbuhaetdamyeonseoyo?" casual="한국에서 공부했다면서?" casualRom="hangugeseo gongbuhaetdamyeonseo?" english="I hear you studied in Korea. Is that right?" />

<SentenceBlock polite="내일 행사에 간다면서요?" politeRom="naeil haengsae gandamyeonseoyo?" casual="내일 행사에 간다면서?" casualRom="naeil haengsae gandamyeonseo?" english="I hear you’re going to the event tomorrow. Is that right?" />

<Hint type="hint">The same ending can challenge a claim when the facts seem inconsistent: 간다면서요? can sound like “But you said you were going!” Use a friendly tone when you are simply confirming news.</Hint>
