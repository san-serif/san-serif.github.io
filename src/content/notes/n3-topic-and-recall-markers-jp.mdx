ところで shifts to a new topic (“by the way”). といえば brings up something associated with a word or topic (“speaking of…”). そういえば introduces a related memory that has just occurred to the speaker (“come to think of it”). から言うと marks the standpoint from which a judgment is made. なんか and なんて can soften or dismiss a topic, but tone can become belittling. っけ appears in casual questions when the speaker is trying to remember or confirm something. それぞれ means “each” or “respectively.” では・それでは and casual じゃあ can close one stage of a conversation and lead into the next; さて similarly signals a fresh step, often “now then.” 一体 adds emphasis to a question such as 一体どうしたの, “what on earth happened?”

<JSentenceBlock furigana="ところで、昨日[きのう]話[はな]していた店[みせ]はどこでしたっけ。" romaji="Tokoro de, kinou hanashite ita mise wa doko deshita kke." english="By the way, where was that shop we talked about yesterday?" />

ところで introduces a new topic, often after the previous one has wrapped up. Sentence-final っけ shows the speaker is retrieving or checking information they partly remember: 明日は休みだっけ, “Was tomorrow a day off?”

<JSentenceBlock furigana="京都[きょうと]といえば、古[ふる]い町並[まちな]みを思[おも]い出[だ]します。" romaji="Kyouto to ieba, furui machinami o omoidasu." english="Speaking of Kyoto, I think of its old streetscapes." />

といえば links a topic with a familiar association. から言うと identifies a perspective: 私の経験から言うと, “speaking from my experience.”

<JSentenceBlock furigana="簡単[かんたん]なんかじゃないよ。" romaji="Kantan nanka ja nai yo." english="It's not easy at all." />

なんか / なんて can make an example sound casual or diminish its importance, as in 私なんかまだまだです, “someone like me still has a long way to go.” Use carefully, since it can sound dismissive toward a person or thing.

<Hint type="insight">ところで is a conversation-management tool for switching topics. といえば follows an association. っけ is a private memory check; it is usually too casual for a formal question to a stranger.</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">New topic: ところで. Association: Xといえば. Viewpoint: Xから言うと. Casual “something like / someone like”: なんか・なんて. Checking memory: っけ.</Hint>
