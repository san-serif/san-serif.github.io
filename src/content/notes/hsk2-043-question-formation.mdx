The usual question words—什么时候 (“when?”), 为什么 (“why?”), 怎么样 (“how is it?”)—fit into an ordinary sentence. If you’re making a suggestion, finish with 好吗 or 行吗 to check whether it suits the other person. And when everyone knows who you mean, noun + 呢 is a quick “what about…?”

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我们明天见面，好吗？" traditional="我們明天見面，好嗎？" pinyin="wǒmen míngtiān jiànmiàn, hǎo ma?" jyutping="ngo5 mun4 ming4 tin1 gin3 min6, hou2 maa3?" english="Shall we meet tomorrow? Does that work?" />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="你什么时候有空？" traditional="你什麼時候有空？" pinyin="nǐ shénme shíhou yǒu kòng?" jyutping="nei5 sam6 mo1 si4 hau6 jau5 hung3?" english="When are you free?" />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="你为什么不吃早饭？" traditional="你為什麼不吃早飯？" pinyin="nǐ wèishénme bù chī zǎofàn?" jyutping="nei5 wai6 sam6 mo1 bat1 hek3 zou2 faan6?" english="Why don’t you eat breakfast?" />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="你的朋友呢？" traditional="你的朋友呢？" pinyin="nǐ de péngyou ne?" jyutping="nei5 dik1 pang4 jau5 ne1?" english="What about your friend?" />

<Hint type="hint">吗 makes a neutral yes/no question. 好吗 asks whether a suggestion is okay. 呢 often points back to a person or thing already mentioned: 我很好，你呢？ (“I’m fine. How about you?”).</Hint>
