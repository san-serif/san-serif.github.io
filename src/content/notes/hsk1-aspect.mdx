Dinner is over, so you tell your friend 我吃了饭. A moment later, the weather changes: 天冷了. The verb itself hasn’t changed tense; 了 presents the meal as complete in the first sentence and the cold as a new situation in the second. For an action still underway, put 在 or 正在 before the verb.

### Examples

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我吃了饭。" traditional="我吃了飯。" pinyin="Wǒ chī le fàn." jyutping="ngo5 sik6 zo2 faan6." english="I’ve eaten." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="天冷了。" traditional="天冷了。" pinyin="Tiān lěng le." jyutping="tin1 laang5 laa3." english="It has become cold." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="他正在看书呢。" traditional="他正在看書呢。" pinyin="Tā zhèngzài kàn shū ne." jyutping="taa1 zing3 zoi6 hon3 syu1 ne1." english="He is reading right now." />

<Hint type="hint">了 does not simply mean “past.” Compare an event marker after the verb with sentence-final 了, which often signals a change.</Hint>
