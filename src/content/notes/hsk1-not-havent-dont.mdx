Your friend turns down coffee: 我不喝咖啡. Later, you ask whether they ate breakfast and hear 还没有. One “not” covers a habit; another says an event hasn’t happened. And if the bus is about to leave, 别着急 is a way to tell someone not to panic. Choose the form for the job it’s doing.

### Examples

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我不喝咖啡。" traditional="我不喝咖啡。" pinyin="Wǒ bù hē kāfēi." jyutping="ngo5 bat1 hot3 gaa3 fe1." english="I don’t drink coffee." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="他没有吃早饭。" traditional="他沒有吃早飯。" pinyin="Tā méiyǒu chī zǎofàn." jyutping="taa1 mut6 jau5 sik6 zou2 faan6." english="He hasn’t eaten breakfast." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="别着急。" traditional="別著急。" pinyin="Bié zháojí." jyutping="bit6 zoek6 gap1." english="Don’t worry." />

<Hint type="hint">A completed action is commonly negated with 没(有), not 不: 我没去 means “I didn’t go.”</Hint>
