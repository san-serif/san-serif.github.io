たり attaches to a verb's past た-form, and used twice with する at the end, たり〜たりする lists a few representative activities without claiming they're the complete list or that they happened in that exact order.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="週末[しゅうまつ]、よく友達[ともだち]と遊[あそ]んだり一緒[いっしょ]にご飯[はん]を食[た]べたりする。" romaji="Shuumatsu, yoku tomodachi to asondari issho ni gohan o tabetari suru." english="On weekends I often do things like hang out with friends and eat together." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="留学中[りゅうがくちゅう]、よく漢字[かんじ]を覚[おぼ]えたり、レポートを書[か]いたりしましたね。" romaji="Ryuugakuchuu, yoku kanji o oboetari, repooto o kaitari shimashita ne." english="While studying abroad, we often did things like memorize kanji and write reports, didn't we?" />

<Hint>Compare this to て, which links actions in a fixed sequence, and し, which stacks up reasons. たり〜たりする deliberately loosens both, "things like this and that," implying a fuller, messier reality behind just the two examples given.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="毎日[まいにち]泳[およ]いだり走[はし]ったりしています。" romaji="Mainichi oyoidari hashittari shite imasu." english="Every day I do things like swim and run." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="行[い]ったり来[き]たりする。" romaji="Ittari kitari suru." english="To go back and forth." />

<Hint>行ったり来たりする is a set phrase built on this same pattern, "to go back and forth," using two opposite verbs to describe repeated motion in both directions rather than a representative sample of activities.</Hint>
