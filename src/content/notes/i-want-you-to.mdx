<JapaneseLessonHead />

てほしい attaches to a verb's て-form and expresses a wish about someone else's action, what you want them to do, not what you yourself want to do.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="直樹[なおき]にもっと野菜[やさい]を食[た]べてほしいです。" romaji="Naoki ni motto yasai o tabete hoshii desu." english="I want Naoki to eat more vegetables." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="沙耶[さや]に早[はや]く来[き]てほしいです。" romaji="Saya ni hayaku kite hoshii desu." english="I want Saya to come quickly." />

<Hint>The person you want something from is marked with に, not は or が. This lines up with たい, which you already know describes wanting to do something yourself, while てほしい flips the direction: someone else is the one doing the action, and に marks who that someone else is.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="子供[こども]に、いい大学[だいがく]に入[はい]ってほしいです。" romaji="Kodomo ni, ii daigaku ni haitte hoshii desu." english="I want my children to get into a good university." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="あまり心配[しんぱい]してほしくないです。" romaji="Amari shinpai shite hoshikunai desu." english="I don't want you to worry too much." />

<Hint>ほしい conjugates like any other い-adjective attached at the end, ほしくない for the negative, ほしかった for the past, ほしくなかった for the past negative, exactly the pattern you already learned for 欲しい itself.</Hint>
