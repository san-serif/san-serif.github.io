てもいい attaches to a verb's て-form to mean "may, it's okay to." てはいけない attaches the same way to mean "must not, it's forbidden." Same て-form both directions, opposite permission.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="先生[せんせい]、辞書[じしょ]を使[つか]ってもいいですか。" romaji="Sensei, jisho o tsukatte mo ii desu ka." english="Teacher, may I use a dictionary?" />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="宿題[しゅくだい]はあしたでもいいですか。" romaji="Shukudai wa ashita demo ii desu ka." english="Is it okay if the homework is tomorrow?" />

<Hint>てもいい literally reads "even if [you] do this, it's good," も carrying its "even" meaning from Anyone, Anytime, Anywhere straight into this construction. Asking permission with か on the end is the standard, polite way to check before doing something.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="宿題[しゅくだい]をメールで出[だ]してはいけません。" romaji="Shukudai o meeru de dashite wa ikemasen." english="You must not turn in homework by email." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="週末[しゅうまつ]も働[はたら]かなくてはいけませんか。" romaji="Shuumatsu mo hatarakanakute wa ikemasen ka." english="Do I have to work on weekends too?" />

<Hint>てはいけない is a flat prohibition, stronger than simply saying you don't want to do something. なくてはいけない (the negative te-form plus はいけない) flips the meaning to obligation instead, "not doing this isn't allowed," which lands as "you have to." Negating a negative to state a requirement is a pattern that shows up constantly in Japanese.</Hint>
