こと attaches after a plain-form clause and turns the whole thing into a noun, "the act of doing X," "the fact that Y." Once wrapped in こと, that clause can be a sentence's subject, object, or predicate just like any ordinary noun.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="私[わたし]の趣味[しゅみ]はピアノを弾[ひ]くことです。" romaji="Watashi no shumi wa piano o hiku koto desu." english="My hobby is playing piano." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="この町[まち]の問題[もんだい]は空気[くうき]がきれいじゃないことです。" romaji="Kono machi no mondai wa kuuki ga kirei ja nai koto desu." english="This town's problem is that the air isn't clean." />

<Hint>ピアノを弾くこと packages the whole action "playing piano" into a single noun, letting it slot directly after です, exactly the way ことができる wraps a verb before できる. This is the same こと, doing the same nominalizing job in a broader range of positions.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="日本人[にほんじん]の友達[ともだち]ができたことが嬉[うれ]しかったです。" romaji="Nihonjin no tomodachi ga dekita koto ga ureshikatta desu." english="The fact that I made Japanese friends made me happy." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="新幹線[しんかんせん]が速[はや]くて便利[べんり]なことに感動[かんどう]しました。" romaji="Shinkansen ga hayakute benri na koto ni kandou shimashita." english="I was impressed by how fast and convenient the bullet train was." />

<Hint>Wrapping a whole fact in こと lets you attach a feeling to it directly, ことが嬉しい, "the fact that ~ made me happy," ことに感動する, "was moved by the fact that ~," a natural way to react to an entire situation rather than a single object.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="来週[らいしゅう]の試験[しけん]のことで心配[しんぱい]です。" romaji="Raishuu no shiken no koto de shinpai desu." english="I'm worried about next week's exam." />

<Hint>Attached directly to a noun instead of a verb, XのことでYou get a slightly softer, more roundabout way of referring to a topic, 試験のことで心配, "worried, regarding the matter of the exam," than simply 試験が心配, "worried about the exam."</Hint>
