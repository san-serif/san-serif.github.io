一番 means "number one," and sitting in front of an adjective it works as a superlative, "the most." Pair it with 〜の中で (among ~) to name the group being compared.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="三人[さんにん]の中[なか]で、山田[やまだ]さんが一番[いちばん]やさしいです。" romaji="Sannin no naka de, Yamada-san ga ichiban yasashii desu." english="Of the three, Yamada is the kindest." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="この町[まち]で一番[いちばん]人気[にんき]があるレストランはどこですか。" romaji="Kono machi de ichiban ninki ga aru resutoran wa doko desu ka." english="Which restaurant in this town is the most popular?" />

<Hint>〜の中で names the pool being compared, three people, this town, your family, and 一番 always sits right before the adjective doing the ranking. Unlike English, which can drop the group ("the kindest"), Japanese leans on 〜の中で constantly to make clear what the comparison is actually against.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="外国[がいこく]料理[りょうり]の中[なか]で、何[なに]を一番[いちばん]よく食[た]べますか。" romaji="Gaikoku ryouri no naka de, nani o ichiban yoku tabemasu ka." english="Out of foreign cuisines, what do you eat the most?" />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="今[いま]までの旅行[りょこう]で一番[いちばん]美味[おい]しかった料理[りょうり]は何[なん]ですか。" romaji="Ima made no ryokou de ichiban oishikatta ryouri wa nan desu ka." english="What's the most delicious food you've had on your travels so far?" />

<Hint>一番 works on past-tense adjectives exactly the same way, 一番美味しかった (was the most delicious) just conjugates the adjective normally and leaves 一番 untouched in front of it. The superlative marker never changes shape, only the thing it's ranking does.</Hint>
