These adverbs describe how a change unfolds. やがて points to something that happens eventually; いつの間にか says it happened without the speaker noticing; 次第[しだい]に describes gradual progress; たちまち means a change happened very quickly.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="やがて春[はる]が訪[おとず]れ、町[まち]に花[はな]が咲[さ]き始[はじ]めます。" romaji="Yagate haru ga otozure, machi ni hana ga saki-hajimemasu." english="Before long, spring will arrive and flowers will start blooming in town." />

やがて often looks ahead to an expected event that will happen after some time. It is common in narration and careful explanations.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="気[き]がつくと、いつの間[ま]にか外[そと]が暗[くら]くなっていました。" romaji="Ki ga tsuku to, itsu no ma ni ka soto ga kuraku natte imashita." english="When I noticed, it had gotten dark outside before I knew it." />

いつの間にか means that a state changed while the speaker was not aware of when it happened. It often expresses mild surprise.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="新[あたら]しい制度[せいど]は次第[しだい]に広[ひろ]まっています。" romaji="Atarashii seido wa shidai ni hiromatte imasu." english="The new system is gradually becoming widespread." />

次第に describes a steady, progressive change. It often appears in news, business, or analytical writing.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="その知らせは、たちまち町中[まちじゅう]に広[ひろ]まりました。" romaji="Sono shirase wa, tachimachi machijuu ni hiromarimashita." english="The news spread through town in no time." />

たちまち describes a result that happened in a very short time, often faster than expected.

<Hint type="insight">A report might say a service is 次第に普及している, “gradually becoming popular.” In a story, いつの間にか can show that time passed unnoticed; たちまち emphasizes how quickly news or a change spread.</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">やがて = after a while; いつの間にか = without noticing when; 次第に = gradually; たちまち = very quickly. The key difference is the speed and the speaker's awareness of the change.</Hint>
