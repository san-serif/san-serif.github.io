だけでなく attaches after a noun, adjective, or plain verb form and means "not just this," always paired with も later in the sentence to add the extra item, "not just X, but Y too."

<JSentenceBlock furigana="私[わたし]の日本語[にほんご]のクラスでは、簡単[かんたん]な漢字[かんじ]だけでなく、難[むずか]しい漢字[かんじ]も練習[れんしゅう]します。" romaji="Watashi no nihongo no kurasu de wa, kantan na kanji dake denaku, muzukashii kanji mo renshuu shimasu." english="In my Japanese class, we practice not just easy kanji, but difficult ones too." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="私[わたし]の日本語[にほんご]のクラスでは、教科書[きょうかしょ]だけでなく、日本語[にほんご]の新聞[しんぶん]を読[よ]みます。" romaji="Watashi no nihongo no kurasu de wa, kyoukasho dake denaku, nihongo no shinbun o yomimasu." english="In my Japanese class, we read not just the textbook, but Japanese newspapers too." />

<Hint type="hint">だけ, which you already know, sets a limit, "only this." だけでなく actively denies that limit, "not only this," always followed by も to name what else belongs in the picture, together forming a single, deliberate correction to a too-narrow assumption.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="この冷蔵庫[れいぞうこ]はドアが右[みぎ]からも左[ひだり]からも開[あ]けられるだけでなく、氷[こおり]もたくさん作[つく]れます。" romaji="Kono reizouko wa doa ga migi kara mo hidari kara mo akerareru dake denaku, koori mo takusan tsukuremasu." english="This fridge's door opens from both the right and left, and it can make plenty of ice too." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="はい、駅[えき]から近[ちか]いだけじゃなくて、新[あたら]しいですよ。" romaji="Hai, eki kara chikai dake janakute, atarashii desu yo." english="Yes, and it's not just close to the station, it's new too." />

<Hint type="insight">In casual speech, だけでなく often shortens to だけじゃなくて, functionally identical but a bit more relaxed, common when listing several selling points of something, an apartment, a product, a plan.</Hint>

In formal speech and writing, のみならず and ばかりでなく also mean “not only… but also…” and often pair with も or でも. はもとより treats the first item as an expected case, then adds another. および is a formal “and” that joins nouns in notices and documents; it doesn't carry the same emphasis as だけでなく.

もまた means “also / likewise” and often repeats an earlier point in a slightly more formal tone: 社長もまた同じ意見です, “The president shares the same opinion as well.”

おまけに means “on top of that / to make matters worse.” It adds one more point, often a further inconvenience or complaint.

さらに and に加えて are neutral “furthermore / in addition.” They add another fact without necessarily implying that the added point is a problem. はもちろん means “not to mention / of course,” and introduces something that should naturally be included. だけでなく～も explicitly says “not only X but also Y,” in everyday speech; the formal patterns elsewhere in this lesson serve similar purposes.

なお can also add a point in formal writing, often introducing supplementary information after a main statement. It is a concise notice-style “in addition / furthermore.”

For other additive links, しかも means “moreover” and often introduces an especially notable extra fact. Noun or clause + 上に adds another point, often with the same positive or negative direction. その上 works as a sentence connector meaning “in addition.” These forms add information without the paired “not only…but also” structure of だけでなく or のみならず.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="このホテルは駅[えき]から遠[とお]いです。おまけに、部屋[へや]も狭[せま]いです。" romaji="Kono hoteru wa eki kara tooi desu. Omake ni, heya mo semai desu." english="This hotel is far from the station. On top of that, the rooms are small." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="このサービスは国内[こくない]のみならず、海外[かいがい]でも利用[りよう]できます。" romaji="Kono saabisu wa kokunai nomi narazu, kaigai demo riyou dekimasu." english="This service can be used not only in Japan but overseas as well." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="安全[あんぜん]はもとより、使[つか]いやすさも大切[たいせつ]です。" romaji="Anzen wa moto yori, tsukaiyasusa mo taisetsu desu." english="Safety goes without saying; ease of use is important too." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="申込書[もうしこみしょ]および必要書類[ひつようしょるい]を提出[ていしゅつ]してください。" romaji="Moushikomisho oyobi hitsuyou shorui o teishutsu shite kudasai." english="Please submit the application form and the required documents." />

<Hint type="hint">Use だけでなく in ordinary conversation. のみならず and および are common in formal notices and reports; はもとより works when the first item is obvious or already accepted, so the sentence can move on to an additional point.</Hint>
