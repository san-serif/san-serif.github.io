あまり paired with a negative verb means "not very" or "not much," softening a denial instead of stating it flatly. It almost never appears without a negative ending nearby.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="鈴木[すずき]さんはあまりお酒[さけ]を飲[の]みません。" romaji="Suzuki-san wa amari osake o nomimasen." english="Suzuki doesn't drink much." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="あまり運動[うんどう]しないほうがいいですよ。" romaji="Amari undou shinai hou ga ii desu yo." english="You'd better not exercise too much." />

<Hint>あまり高くない reads as "not very expensive," gentler than a flat "cheap." It's the negative-leaning cousin of とても (very): とても高い is "very expensive," あまり高くない is "not very expensive," and the two rarely swap roles.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="いいえ、ぜんぜんすいていません。" romaji="Iie, zenzen suiteimasen." english="No, it's not empty at all." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="いいえ、ぜんぜん近[ちか]くなかったです。" romaji="Iie, zenzen chikakunakatta desu." english="No, it wasn't close at all." />

<Hint>ぜんぜん is あまり's stronger sibling, pushing the same negative all the way to "not at all" instead of just "not very." Both need a negative verb to make sense; ぜんぜん alone, without the negative, only survives in very casual speech meaning something closer to "totally," a newer, looser usage separate from this pairing.</Hint>
