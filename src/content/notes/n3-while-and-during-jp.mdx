中, 間に, うちに, and 最中に all locate one event relative to a period, but their focus differs. 中 means “during” or “in the middle of.” 間に places a shorter event inside a defined span. うちに emphasizes doing something while a favorable state or opportunity still lasts. ないうちに means “before X happens.” 最中に highlights that another event interrupts the very middle of an activity. まま describes leaving something in a state without changing it. かけ marks something as partly done or just beginning; っぱなし describes leaving an action or resulting state unattended. にかけて marks a span that extends across a period or area. たびに means “every time” an event occurs. 中を can describe moving through a difficult or active setting: 雨の中を歩く, “walk through the rain.”

<JSentenceBlock furigana="日本[にほん]にいる間[あいだ]に、行[い]きたかった場所[ばしょ]を訪[たず]ねました。" romaji="Nihon ni iru aida ni, ikitakatta basho o tazunemashita." english="While I was in Japan, I visited places I had wanted to see." />

間に marks a bounded period and says an event happened within it. うちに is used when the speaker wants to act before a condition changes: 暗くならないうちに帰ろう, “let's go home before it gets dark.”

<JSentenceBlock furigana="会議[かいぎ]の最中[さいちゅう]に、火災報知器[かさいほうちき]が鳴[な]りました。" romaji="Kaigi no saichuu ni, kasai houchiki ga narimashita." english="In the middle of the meeting, the fire alarm sounded." />

最中に emphasizes that something happens at the busiest or most active moment. Verbている + 中 means an action is in progress; Noun + 中 can mean “during” or “currently,” depending on the noun.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="窓[まど]を開[あ]けたまま、出[で]かけてしまいました。" romaji="Mado o aketa mama, dekakete shimaimashita." english="I went out and left the window open." />

まま says that a state remains unchanged while something else happens. It often points out an unintended or noteworthy state, such as leaving a light on or going to sleep with the TV running.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="電気[でんき]をつけっぱなしで出[で]てしまいました。" romaji="Denki o tsukeppanashi de dete shimaimashita." english="I left with the lights on." />

っぱなし often criticizes or notes leaving something in an undesirable state. It overlaps with まま, but focuses on an action being left undone or continuing without attention.

Vます stem + かけ describes something partly completed or just underway: 読みかけの本, “a book I started reading.” にかけて follows two endpoints to mark an approximate span: 夜から朝にかけて, “from night through morning.”

Verb dictionary form + たびに marks an event that happens every time another action occurs: この道を通るたびに, “every time I take this road.” ついでに means doing one more task while already taking the opportunity to do something else: 銀行に行くついでに郵便局にも寄る, “while going to the bank, stop by the post office too.”

と同時に means “at the same time,” either for two simultaneous events or for a change that accompanies another. 直ちに means “immediately” in formal instructions, while おきに marks a repeated interval: 一日おきに, “every other day.”

<Hint type="insight">When a chance may disappear, うちに sounds purposeful: 若いうちにいろいろ経験する, “gain different experiences while you're young.” 間に simply locates an event within a period. 最中に often introduces an interruption.</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">Event within a span: 間に. Act before a state changes: うちに. At the exact middle of an activity: 最中に. Ongoing activity: Vている中. Leave a state unchanged: まま.</Hint>
