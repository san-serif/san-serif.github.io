[일, 이, 삼](/notes/counting-won) gave you the months. This note finishes the calendar: days of the week, full dates, and 언제 (when), the question word that starts half the scheduling conversations you'll ever have in Korean.

### Days of the Week

Every day of the week ends in 요일. Only the first syllable changes.

<Hint>Those first syllables aren't random. They're the same five elements plus sun and moon used across East Asian calendars: 월 (moon), 화 (fire), 수 (water), 목 (wood), 금 (metal/gold), 토 (earth), 일 (sun). Japanese weekday names run on the exact same seven, which is why 월요일 and getsuyōbi both mean "moon day."</Hint>

### When Is It?

언제 (when) works exactly like the other question words you already know, 뭐, 누구, 어디. Drop it in where the answer goes and everything else stays put.

<SentenceBlock formal="생일이 언제입니까?" formalRom="saengiri eonjeimnikka?" polite="생일이 언제예요?" politeRom="saengiri eonjeyeyo?" casual="생일이 언제야?" casualRom="saengiri eonjeya?" english="When is your birthday?" />

<SentenceBlock formal="제 생일은 8월 19일입니다." formalRom="je saengireun parwol sipguirimnida." polite="제 생일은 8월 19일이에요." politeRom="je saengireun parwol sipguirieyo." casual="내 생일은 8월 19일이야." casualRom="nae saengireun parwol sipguiriya." english="My birthday is August 19." />

<Hint>생일이 언제예요? assumes one calendar, but plenty of Korean families track two: 양력 (the solar calendar most of the world uses) and 음력 (the lunar calendar). Many older Koreans still celebrate their lunar birthday, which shifts against the solar calendar every year, so don't be surprised if someone gives you a date that "moves."</Hint>
