On the shelf, you spot white sneakers and a red umbrella: 하얀 운동화, 빨간 우산. Many Korean adjectives ending in ㅎ drop it before a noun and add ㄴ. Before a vowel-starting ending, the stem changes too: 하얗다 becomes 하얘요; 빨갛다 becomes 빨개요.

<SentenceBlock formal="이 핸드폰 디자인이 어떻습니까?" formalRom="i haendeupon dijaini eotteotseumnikka?" polite="이 핸드폰 디자인이 어때요?" politeRom="i haendeupon dijaini eottaeyo?" casual="이 핸드폰 디자인 어때?" casualRom="i haendeupon dijain eottae?" english="How is this phone's design?" />

<SentenceBlock formal="미나 씨, 얼굴이 빨갛습니다." formalRom="mina ssi, eolguri ppalgatseumnida." polite="미나 씨, 얼굴이 빨개요." politeRom="mina ssi, eolguri ppalgwayo." casual="미나, 얼굴이 빨개." casualRom="mina, eolguri ppalgwae." english="Mina, your face is red." />

<Hint type="hint">Not every ㅎ is irregular. 좋다 keeps its ㅎ: 좋아요, 좋은 날씨. Verbs such as 놓다 and 넣다 also keep it. Check the dictionary form rather than assuming every word with ㅎ changes.</Hint>

<SentenceBlock formal="저 노란 우산을 주십시오." formalRom="jeo noran usaneul jusipsio." polite="저 노란 우산 주세요." politeRom="jeo noran usan juseyo." casual="저 노란 우산 줘." casualRom="jeo noran usan jwo." english="Please give me that yellow umbrella." />

<Hint type="insight">In real life, colors are often used in the noun-modifying form when identifying a specific item: 빨간색 가방 (“the red bag”), 파란 우산 (“the blue umbrella”). For colors, you will often hear both the adjective form and the color noun ending in 색.</Hint>
