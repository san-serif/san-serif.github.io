られる (attached to a る-verb's stem) or れる (attached to an う-verb's negative stem) turns a verb passive, shifting the sentence's focus onto the person something is done to, marked with に for who did it.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="兄[あに]は毎朝[まいあさ]6時[ろくじ]に母[はは]に起[お]こされます。" romaji="Ani wa maiasa rokuji ni haha ni okosaremasu." english="My older brother gets woken up by our mother at 6:00 every morning." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="弟[おとうと]は時々[ときどき]兄[あに]に揶揄[から]われます。" romaji="Otouto wa tokidoki ani ni karawaremasu." english="My younger brother sometimes gets teased by our older brother." />

<Hint>起こす becomes 起こされる, 揶揄う becomes 揶揄われる. The person acted upon becomes the subject, は or が, and the person doing the action takes に, exactly flipping the roles from the plain, active sentence.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="私[わたし]はよく大輔[だいすけ]さんに夜中[よなか]に友達[ともだち]を連[つ]れて来[こ]られます。" romaji="Watashi wa yoku Daisuke-san ni yonaka ni tomodachi o tsurete koraremasu." english="Daisuke keeps bringing friends over in the middle of the night on me." />

<Hint>This second kind, sometimes called the adversity passive, describes something happening that inconveniences the speaker, even though the speaker isn't literally the object of the verb. 連れて来る has no direct object marked with を belonging to the speaker, yet the passive still frames it as something done to them, which is why it always carries a note of "and that's a hassle for me."</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="隣[となり]のトトロという映画[えいが]は宮崎駿[みやざきはやお]によって作[つく]られました。" romaji="Tonari no Totoro to iu eiga wa Miyazaki Hayao ni yotte tsukuraremashita." english="The movie My Neighbor Totoro was made by Hayao Miyazaki." />

<Hint>によって replaces に when crediting the creator of something, a book, a film, a building, standard in formal or written descriptions of who made what, distinct from the plain に used for everyday passive sentences.</Hint>
