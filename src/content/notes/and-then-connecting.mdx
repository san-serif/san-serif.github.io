て doesn't only chain verbs together. い-adjectives, な-adjectives, and nouns all have their own て-shaped connector, letting you string two descriptions into one flowing sentence instead of two short ones.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="私[わたし]の町[まち]は静[しず]かで綺麗[きれい]です。" romaji="Watashi no machi wa shizuka de kirei desu." english="My town is quiet and pretty." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="私[わたし]の町[まち]は危[あぶ]なくて怖[こわ]いです。" romaji="Watashi no machi wa abunakute kowai desu." english="My town is dangerous and scary." />

<Hint>Each word type takes a different shape: な-adjectives and nouns drop だ and add で (静かで, 高校生で), while い-adjectives swap the final い for くて (危ない becomes 危なくて). Same job, three slightly different mechanics depending on what's being connected.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="私[わたし]の弟[おとうと]は高校生[こうこうせい]でサッカーが好[す]きです。" romaji="Watashi no otouto wa koukousei de sakkaa ga suki desu." english="My younger brother is a high schooler and likes soccer." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="私[わたし]の祖母[そぼ]は面白[おもしろ]くて優[やさ]しいです。" romaji="Watashi no sobo wa omoshirokute yasashii desu." english="My grandmother is funny and kind." />

<Hint>高校生で isn't the same で as the tool/means で from By, At, With. This で is the noun's own connecting shape, standing in for だ (is) the way くて stands in for an い-adjective's plain ending. Two completely different jobs sharing one sound.</Hint>
