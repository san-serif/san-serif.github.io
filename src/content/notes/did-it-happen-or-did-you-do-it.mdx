Many Japanese verbs come in pairs describing the same event from two angles: a transitive verb for someone doing it on purpose, and an intransitive verb for it simply happening, with no one named as the cause.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="リンさんは電気[でんき]を消[け]しました。" romaji="Rin-san wa denki o keshimashita." english="Lin turned off the light." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="電気[でんき]が消[き]えました。" romaji="Denki ga kiemashita." english="The light went out." />

<Hint>消す (transitive, someone turns something off) and 消える (intransitive, something goes out on its own) describe the same result, a light no longer being on, but only one names who did it. を marks the object of the transitive verb; が marks the subject of the intransitive one, and the two verbs are never interchangeable despite looking related.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="窓[まど]が開[あ]いていました。" romaji="Mado ga aiteimashita." english="The window was open." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="財布[さいふ]が落[お]ちていました。" romaji="Saifu ga ochiteimashita." english="The wallet was on the ground (had fallen)." />

<Hint>Attach ている to the intransitive half and you get a neutral resultant state, no blame or intention implied, unlike てある which always points at someone's deliberate action. 窓が開いています just says the window is open; who opened it, or why, isn't part of the sentence at all.</Hint>
