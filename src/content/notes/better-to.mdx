ほうがいい attaches after a verb's plain past た-form for a recommendation, "it'd be better to," or after the plain negative ない-form for the opposite, "it'd be better not to."

<JSentenceBlock furigana="少[すこ]し休[やす]んだほうがいいですよ。" romaji="Sukoshi yasunda hou ga ii desu yo." english="You'd better rest a little." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="遅[おそ]くまで仕事[しごと]をしないほうがいいですよ。" romaji="Osoku made shigoto o shinai hou ga ii desu yo." english="You'd better not work until late." />

<Hint>The positive form always uses the past た-form, even though the advice is about the future, 休んだほうがいい, never 休むほうがいい. The negative form uses the plain ない-form as normal, しないほうがいい, no past tense involved there.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="なるべく自分[じぶん]で料理[りょうり]したほうがいいと思[おも]います。" romaji="Narubeku jibun de ryouri shita hou ga ii to omoimasu." english="I think it'd be better to cook for yourself as much as possible." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="夜[よる]遅[おそ]く食[た]べないほうがいいと思[おも]います。" romaji="Yoru osoku tabenai hou ga ii to omoimasu." english="I think it'd be better not to eat late at night." />

<Hint>と思います softens ほうがいい even further, turning a recommendation into "I think it'd be better," which reads as a personal opinion rather than a directive, useful whenever you're giving advice to someone you don't want to sound bossy toward.</Hint>
