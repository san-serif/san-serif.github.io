Past tense slots 았 or 었 in between the stem and the ending you already know, using the exact same vowel-harmony rule from [을/를 해요](/notes/what-are-you-doing): stems with ㅏ or ㅗ take 았, everything else takes 었. Swap -어요 for -았어요/-었어요 and you've moved the whole sentence into the past.

<Hint>The polite and casual past differ by exactly one syllable, 요, the same relationship as every present-tense pair you've already learned. If you can hear 먹었어요 versus 먹었어, you already know the whole pattern. Nothing new to learn there, just apply the trick you already have.</Hint>

Every 하다 verb breaks the vowel-harmony rule the same irregular way: 하다's past is always 했, never 하았. This matters constantly, since 공부하다, 운동하다, 요리하다, 일하다, every noun-plus-하다 verb from [을/를 해요](/notes/what-are-you-doing), follows it.

<SentenceBlock formal="어제 한국어를 공부했습니다." formalRom="eoje hangugeoreul gongbuhaetseumnida." polite="어제 한국어를 공부했어요." politeRom="eoje hangugeoreul gongbuhaesseoyo." casual="어제 한국어를 공부했어." casualRom="eoje hangugeoreul gongbuhaesseo." english="I studied Korean yesterday." />

<SentenceBlock formal="어제 수영을 했습니다." formalRom="eoje suyeongeul haetseumnida." polite="어제 수영을 했어요." politeRom="eoje suyeongeul haesseoyo." casual="어제 수영을 했어." casualRom="eoje suyeongeul haesseo." english="I swam yesterday." />

<Hint>했어요 shows up so often that it's tempting to bolt it onto everything, but it only ever means "did," never "was" or "went." 되다 (to become), 가다 (to go), 이다 (to be) all keep their own separate past-tense stems, 됐어요, 갔어요, 이었어요/였어요. 하다's irregularity doesn't spread to other verbs.</Hint>
