<JapaneseLessonHead />

This そうだ attaches to an adjective's stem or a verb's stem, dropping the ending, and describes an impression based on appearance, "looks like," "seems like." Unlike the reporting そうだ, it's built entirely on what you can see or sense right now, not something you heard.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="教科書[きょうかしょ]は難[むずか]しそうです。" romaji="Kyoukasho wa muzukashisou desu." english="The textbook looks difficult." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="この餃子[ぎょうざ]は美味[おい]しそうですね。" romaji="Kono gyouza wa oishisou desu ne." english="These dumplings look delicious." />

<Hint>い-adjectives drop the final い before adding そう, 難しい becomes 難しそう, 美味しい becomes 美味しそう. い itself, oddly, becomes よさそう instead of いそう, an irregular exception worth just memorizing.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="先生[せんせい]は厳[きび]しくなさそうです。" romaji="Sensei wa kibishikunasasou desu." english="The teacher doesn't look strict." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="この赤[あか]ちゃんは泣[な]きそうですね。" romaji="Kono akachan wa nakisou desu ne." english="This baby looks like it's about to cry." />

<Hint>The negative inserts なさ before そう, 厳しくない becomes 厳しくなさそう, adding an extra syllable that's easy to drop by accident. Attached to a verb's stem instead of an adjective, そう shifts toward "on the verge of," 泣きそう, "looks about to cry," 落ちそう, "looks about to fall."</Hint>
