At a market, you ask the price; later, you write the date in your calendar and arrange a meeting time. Chinese prices put the number before the currency unit, dates run from year to month to day, and clock time puts 点 after the hour. Once you know that order, the numbers do most of the work.

### Examples

<CSentenceBlock simplified="这个苹果三块钱。" traditional="這個蘋果三塊錢。" pinyin="Zhège píngguǒ sān kuài qián." jyutping="ze5 go3 ping4 gwo2 saam1 faai3 cin2." english="This apple costs three yuan." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="今天是星期一。" traditional="今天是星期一。" pinyin="Jīntiān shì xīngqīyī." jyutping="gam1 tin1 si6 sing1 kei4 jat1." english="Today is Monday." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="现在三点半。" traditional="現在三點半。" pinyin="Xiànzài sān diǎn bàn." jyutping="jin6 zoi6 saam1 dim2 bun3." english="It’s half past three." />

<Hint type="hint">Say the larger date unit first: year, month, day. In a clock time, 点 marks the hour; 半 means thirty minutes past.</Hint>
