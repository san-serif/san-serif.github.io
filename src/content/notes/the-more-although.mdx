The more you use a language, the more familiar it becomes. A report about that change may also need to contrast two trends. -(으)ㄹ수록 links related changes; -(으)나 sets one fact against another in a written voice.

### One Change Goes With Another

Use -ㄹ수록 after a vowel or ㄹ and -을수록 after another consonant: 갈수록, 알수록, 읽을수록. The second clause changes in relation to the first. You can reinforce it with -(으)면: 읽으면 읽을수록.

### A Contrast in Written Language

Use -나 after a vowel and -으나 after most consonants: 크나, 작으나. A ㄹ stem drops ㄹ before ㄴ: 살다 → 사나. Nouns take -(이)나. This connector often does the work of -지만 in reports or formal writing.

<SentenceBlock formal="온라인 수업은 편리하나 직접 대화할 기회가 적습니다." formalRom="ollain sueobeun pyeollihana jikjeop daehwahal gihoega jeokseumnida." polite="온라인 수업은 편리하나 직접 대화할 기회가 적어요." politeRom="ollain sueobeun pyeollihana jikjeop daehwahal gihoega jeogeoyo." casual="온라인 수업은 편리하나 직접 대화할 기회가 적어." casualRom="ollain sueobeun pyeollihana jikjeop daehwahal gihoega jeogeo." english="Online classes are convenient, but there are fewer opportunities to talk in person." />

<SentenceBlock formal="여러 번 시도했으나 성공하지 못했습니다." formalRom="yeoreo beon sidohaesseuna seonggonghaji mothaetseumnida." polite="여러 번 시도했으나 성공하지 못했어요." politeRom="yeoreo beon sidohaesseuna seonggonghaji mothaesseoyo." casual="여러 번 시도했으나 성공하지 못했어." casualRom="yeoreo beon sidohaesseuna seonggonghaji mothaesseo." english="I tried several times but did not succeed." />

<Hint type="hint">The connector -(으)나 gives a contrast, as in 편리하나, “although convenient.” Noun + (이)나 can also list alternatives, while quantity + (이)나 can emphasise a large amount. The form’s job depends on what it attaches to and the sentence around it.</Hint>
