This lesson groups ways of setting scope. 抜きで explicitly leaves something out; やら～やら gives a non-exhaustive list of examples, often things that feel like a jumble; にしても～にしても treats alternatives alike; 一応 signals a provisional or minimal action; and なりとも means “at least” or “even a little,” in a formal request.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="堅苦[かたくる]しい挨拶[あいさつ]は抜[ぬ]きで、今日[きょう]は楽[たの]しく話[はな]しましょう。" romaji="Katakurushii aisatsu wa nuki de, kyou wa tanoshiku hanashimashou." english="Let's skip the formal greetings and enjoy talking today." />

Noun + 抜きで means “without” or “leaving out” that item. It can be literal, as in 砂糖抜き, “without sugar,” or figurative, as in 前置き抜きで, “without a preamble.”

<JSentenceBlock furigana="引[ひ]っ越[こ]しの準備[じゅんび]で、箱[はこ]やら書類[しょるい]やらが部屋[へや]に積[つ]んであります。" romaji="Hikkoshi no junbi de, hako yara shorui yara ga heya ni tsunde arimasu." english="For the move, boxes and papers and all sorts of things are piled in the room." />

やら～やら lists representative items, often suggesting there is more to the list or that things are mixed together. にしても～にしても means “whether A or B,” with the same conclusion applying to both: 雨にしても雪にしても出発する, “whether rain or snow, we'll leave.”

<JSentenceBlock furigana="一応[いちおう]担当者[たんとうしゃ]に確認[かくにん]しましたが、まだ決定[けってい]ではありません。" romaji="Ichiou tantousha ni kakunin shimashita ga, mada kettei de wa arimasen." english="I checked with the person in charge for now, but it isn't final yet." />

一応 can mean “for now,” “just in case,” or “at least,” depending on context. なりとも is a formal way to ask for even a small amount: ご意見なりともお聞かせください, “please share even a brief opinion.”

<Hint type="insight">A host can say 前置き抜きで始めましょう, “let's start without a long preamble.” A colleague might say 一応確認しました to report a basic check while leaving the result provisional. The surrounding context determines whether 一応 sounds reassuring or tentative.</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">抜きで removes something; やら～やら samples a varied list; にしても～にしても covers alternatives equally; 一応 marks a basic or provisional step; なりとも politely asks for even a little.</Hint>
