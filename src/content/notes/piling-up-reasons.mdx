し attaches to the plain form and lists reasons or facts, one after another, building toward a conclusion. Unlike て or から, し implies there could be even more reasons than the ones actually stated.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="静[しず]かだし、家賃[やちん]も安[やす]いし、とてもいいです。" romaji="Shizuka da shi, yachin mo yasui shi, totemo ii desu." english="It's quiet, and the rent is cheap too, so it's really good." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="好[す]きな仕事[しごと]だし、同僚[どうりょう]も親切[しんせつ]だし、毎日[まいにち]楽[たの]しんでいます。" romaji="Suki na shigoto da shi, douryou mo shinsetsu da shi, mainichi tanoshinde imasu." english="It's work I like, and my coworkers are kind too, so I enjoy every day." />

<Hint>Each clause ending in し adds one more supporting fact, and the pattern itself suggests the list isn't exhaustive, "quiet, and the rent's cheap, and honestly there's more I could say." から gives a single clear reason; し piles several up loosely.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="寒[さむ]かったし、あまり魚[さかな]がいなかったし、面白[おもしろ]くなかったです。" romaji="Samukatta shi, amari sakana ga inakatta shi, omoshirokunakatta desu." english="It was cold, and there weren't many fish either, so it wasn't fun." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="えっ、今晩[こんばん]ですか。今晩[こんばん]は予定[よてい]があるし、ちょっと。。。" romaji="E, konban desu ka. Konban wa yotei ga aru shi, chotto..." english="Huh, tonight? I've got plans tonight, so it's a bit..." />

<Hint>Trailing off after a single し, leaving ちょっと。。。 unfinished, softens a refusal the same way ので does, implying there's a reason without spelling out every detail, only politer, since the listener can guess there's more behind it.</Hint>
