Repeat a verb and the action often sounds brief, light, or tentative. With a one-syllable verb, you can repeat it directly (看看), put 一 between the two parts (看一看), or place 了 after the first part (看了看). Two-syllable verbs commonly repeat as ABAB (讨论讨论). Adjectives use a different pattern: AABB can make a description more vivid.

### A Short Try

<CSentenceBlock simplified="你先试一试这个办法。" traditional="你先試一試這個辦法。" pinyin="Nǐ xiān shì yi shì zhège bànfǎ." jyutping="nei5 sin1 si3 jat1 si3 ze5 go3 baan6 faat3." english="Try this method first." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我想了想，还是决定去。" traditional="我想了想，還是決定去。" pinyin="Wǒ xiǎng le xiǎng, háishi juédìng qù." jyutping="ngo5 soeng2 zo2 soeng2, waan4 si6 kyut3 ding6 heoi3." english="I thought it over and still decided to go." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我们应该讨论讨论这个问题。" traditional="我們應該討論討論這個問題。" pinyin="Wǒmen yīnggāi tǎolùn tǎolùn zhège wèntí." jyutping="ngo5 mun4 jing1 goi1 tou2 leon6 tou2 leon6 ze5 go3 man6 tai4." english="We should discuss this question for a bit." />

A一A often makes a suggestion feel easy to accept: 试一试 is “give it a try.” A了A presents a short action as completed: 想了想 means someone thought it over briefly.

<Hint type="hint">Reduplication itself does not mark past tense. In 想了想, 了 sits inside the repeated verb to frame a brief completed action. In 我想一想, the same verb is an intention: “Let me think for a moment.”</Hint>

### Describing a State

<CSentenceBlock simplified="她高高兴兴地回家了。" traditional="她高高興興地回家了。" pinyin="Tā gāogāoxìngxìng de huí jiā le." jyutping="taa1 gou1 gou1 hing3 hing3 dei6 wui4 gaa1 liu5." english="She went home happily." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="房间收拾得干干净净。" traditional="房間收拾得乾乾淨淨。" pinyin="Fángjiān shōushi de gāngānjìngjìng." jyutping="fong4 gaan1 sau1 sap6 dak1 gon1 gon1 zeng6 zeng6." english="The room was tidied up until it was spotless." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="你慢慢说，我听着呢。" traditional="你慢慢說，我聽著呢。" pinyin="Nǐ mànmàn shuō, wǒ tīngzhe ne." jyutping="nei5 maan6 maan6 syut3, ngo5 teng1 zoek6 ne1." english="Take your time; I’m listening." />

The adjective pattern paints a fuller picture than a plain adjective. 高高兴兴 describes someone as cheerful throughout the action; 干干净净 emphasizes how thoroughly clean the room is. 慢慢 is the common AA form of 慢, “slow,” used here to describe how someone speaks.

<Hint type="insight">看看 and 听听 often soften an invitation or suggestion: 你看看这个 (“Have a look at this”) feels lighter than a direct command to look. English does something similar with “take a look” and “have a listen.”</Hint>
