が glues two clauses together with a contrast between them, attaching straight to the plain form of whatever comes before it, no separate connecting word needed.

### が: But

<JSentenceBlock furigana="私[わたし]は友達[ともだち]が少[すく]ないが、弟[おとうと]は友達[ともだち]が多[おお]いです。" romaji="Watashi wa tomodachi ga sukunai ga, otouto wa tomodachi ga ooi desu." english="I have few friends, but my brother has a lot." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="私[わたし]は車[くるま]の運転[うんてん]ができるが、弟[おとうと]はできないです。" romaji="Watashi wa kuruma no unten ga dekiru ga, otouto wa dekinai desu." english="I can drive, but my brother can't." />

<Hint>が attaches directly to a plain-form verb or i-adjective with nothing in between. な-adjectives and nouns need だ first (苦手だが, not 苦手が), the same だ that shows up everywhere else plain-form sentences need it.</Hint>

### が: Softening a Request

<JSentenceBlock furigana="コンビニに行[い]くんですが、何[なに]かいりますか。" romaji="Conbini ni iku n desu ga, nani ka irimasu ka." english="I'm heading to the convenience store, do you need anything?" />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="明日[あした]日本語[にほんご]の試験[しけん]があるんですが、漢字[かんじ]が覚[おぼ]えられないんです。" romaji="Ashita nihongo no shiken ga aru n desu ga, kanji ga oboerarenai n desu." english="I have a Japanese exam tomorrow, and I can't remember the kanji." />

<Hint>This が isn't contrasting anything, there's no "but" in either translation above. Paired with んです, が just cushions the sentence before a request or a follow-up, giving the listener a beat to catch up before you ask for something. Dropping it entirely, 何かいりますか alone, isn't wrong, it just sounds slightly more abrupt.</Hint>
