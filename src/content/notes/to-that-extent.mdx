ほど measures how far something goes. Attached after a clause, it paints a vivid degree through exaggeration, "to the point that." Attached after a noun with a negative verb, it compares two things, "not as much as."

<JSentenceBlock furigana="死[し]ぬほど苦[くる]しいです。" romaji="Shinu hodo kurushii desu." english="It's so painful I could die." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="言葉[ことば]が出[で]ないほど嬉[うれ]しいです。" romaji="Kotoba ga denai hodo ureshii desu." english="I'm so happy I'm at a loss for words." />

<Hint>These sentences aren't literal, no one is actually dying or losing the ability to speak, ほど here paints a picture, describing a feeling by naming an extreme, vivid result it would be strong enough to cause.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="田中[たなか]さんほど運動[うんどう]しません。" romaji="Tanaka-san hodo undou shimasen." english="I don't exercise as much as Tanaka does." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="日本語[にほんご]ほど難[むずか]しくないです。" romaji="Nihongo hodo muzukashikunai desu." english="It's not as difficult as Japanese." />

<Hint>This second use always needs a negative verb, just like しか, and always compares two things directly, X ほど [negative], "not as [adjective/verb] as X." Without the negative, ほど reverts to its extreme-degree meaning instead.</Hint>
