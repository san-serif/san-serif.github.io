“Getting up early is hard.” “I enjoy cooking.” “The book I bought is on the table.” In Chinese, an action or a whole description can work as the subject, the object of a verb, or a phrase that tells us which noun we mean.







<CSentenceBlock simplified="早起让人一天都有精神。" traditional="早起讓人一天都有精神。" pinyin="zǎoqǐ ràng rén yì tiān dōu yǒu jīngshén." jyutping="zou2 hei2 joeng6 jan4 jat1 tin1 dou1 jau5 zing1 san4." english="Getting up early gives you energy for the whole day." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我喜欢周末在家做饭。" traditional="我喜歡週末在家做飯。" pinyin="wǒ xǐhuan zhōumò zài jiā zuòfàn." jyutping="ngo5 hei2 fun1 zau1 mut6 zoi6 gaa1 zou6 faan6." english="I like cooking at home on weekends." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="我想读的那本书已经到了。" traditional="我想讀的那本書已經到了。" pinyin="wǒ xiǎng dú de nà běn shū yǐjīng dào le." jyutping="ngo5 soeng2 duk6 dik1 naa5 bun2 syu1 ji5 ging1 dou3 liu5." english="The book I wanted to read has arrived." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="她认真准备的报告让大家很佩服。" traditional="她認真準備的報告讓大家很佩服。" pinyin="tā rènzhēn zhǔnbèi de bàogào ràng dàjiā hěn pèifu." jyutping="taa1 jing6 zan1 zeon2 bei6 dik1 bou3 gou3 joeng6 daai6 gaa1 han2 pui6 fuk6." english="Everyone admired the report she prepared so carefully." />

<CSentenceBlock simplified="跑步对身体有好处。" traditional="跑步對身體有好處。" pinyin="pǎobù duì shēntǐ yǒu hǎochu." jyutping="paau2 bou6 deoi3 san1 tai2 jau5 hou2 cyu3." english="Running is good for your health." />

<Hint type="hint">A verb phrase can stand where a noun would: 早起 is the subject in the first sentence, and 做饭 is the object of 喜欢 in the second. In the last two, the 的 phrase comes before the noun it describes: 那本书, 她准备的报告.</Hint>
