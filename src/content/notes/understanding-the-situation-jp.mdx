何しろ foregrounds a strong reason (“after all,” “because”), 何より ranks something as especially important, and ことだから lets the listener infer a likely action from someone's known character or situation. なにやら is a vague “something or other,” often used when the speaker cannot identify the details.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="何[なに]しろ初[はじ]めての経験[けいけん]ですから、少[すこ]し時間[じかん]がかかります。" romaji="Nanishiro hajimete no keiken desu kara, sukoshi jikan ga kakarimasu." english="After all, it's a first experience, so it takes a little time." />

何しろ introduces the reason the speaker wants the listener to keep in mind. It often appears with から or のだから and can sound persuasive or explanatory.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="責任感[せきにんかん]の強[つよ]い田中[たなか]さんのことだから、最後[さいご]までやり遂[と]げるでしょう。" romaji="Sekininkan no tsuyoi Tanaka-san no koto dakara, saigo made yaritogeru deshou." english="Knowing the responsible Tanaka, they'll probably see it through to the end." />

Person + のことだから invokes what is known about that person to make a prediction. It typically assumes the listener shares that knowledge. 何より means “more than anything,” while なにやら describes something indistinct: なにやら音がする, “there's some sort of sound.”

<Hint type="insight">何しろ can help explain a delay without sounding defensive when the reason is relevant. のことだから is warmer and more personal: it can express confidence in someone's usual reliability, or concern about a habit everyone knows.</Hint>

<Hint type="hint">何しろ presents a reason; 何より emphasizes what matters most; Xのことだから predicts from a known trait; なにやら leaves the thing unidentified.</Hint>
