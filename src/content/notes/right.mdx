-지요 attaches directly to any stem, no vowel harmony, and turns a statement into "right?" or "isn't it?", inviting the listener to agree. It layers on top of anything you already know how to say, including past tense and -(으)ㄹ 거예요 futures.

<SentenceBlock polite="오늘 날씨가 좋지요?" politeRom="oneul nalssiga jochiyo?" casual="오늘 날씨가 좋지?" casualRom="oneul nalssiga jochi?" english="The weather is nice today, right?" />

<Hint>지요 very often contracts to 죠 in real speech: 좋죠? instead of 좋지요?, the same kind of shortening as English "gonna" for "going to." Both are correct; 죠 is what you'll actually hear far more often.</Hint>

<SentenceBlock polite="한국 음식은 맛있지요?" politeRom="hanguk eumsigeun masitjiyo?" casual="한국 음식은 맛있지?" casualRom="hanguk eumsigeun masitji?" english="Korean food is delicious, right?" />

<SentenceBlock polite="어제 날씨가 안 좋았지요?" politeRom="eoje nalssiga an joatjiyo?" casual="어제 날씨가 안 좋았지?" casualRom="eoje nalssiga an joatji?" english="The weather was bad yesterday, right?" />

<SentenceBlock polite="내일은 회사에 갈 거지요?" politeRom="naeireun hoesae gal geojiyo?" casual="내일은 회사에 갈 거지?" casualRom="naeireun hoesae gal geoji?" english="You'll go to work tomorrow, right?" />

<SentenceBlock polite="우리 토요일에 만나기로 했지요?" politeRom="uri toyoire mannagiro haetjiyo?" casual="우리 토요일에 만나기로 했지?" casualRom="uri toyoire mannagiro haetji?" english="We agreed to meet on Saturday, right?" />

<Hint>Korean reaches for -지요/-죠 far more often than English reaches for "right?" or "isn't it?". It's standard conversational oil, less a genuine question and more a way of keeping the exchange moving and checking you're both on the same page.</Hint>

<SentenceBlock polite="안나 씨는 회사원이지요?" politeRom="anna ssineun hoesawonijiyo?" casual="안나 씨는 회사원이지?" casualRom="anna ssineun hoesawoniji?" english="Anna is an office worker, right?" />

<SentenceBlock polite="한국은 사계절이 있지요?" politeRom="hangugeun sagyejeori itjiyo?" casual="한국은 사계절이 있지?" casualRom="hangugeun sagyejeori itji?" english="Korea has four seasons, right?" />
