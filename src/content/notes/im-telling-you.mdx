よ tacks onto the end of a sentence to flag that you're telling the listener something they don't already know, the opposite of ね, which checks something you both already share.

<JSentenceBlock furigana="この店[みせ]は安[やす]いですよ。" romaji="Kono mise wa yasui desu yo." english="This shop is cheap, you know." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="明日[あした]クイズがありますよ。" romaji="Ashita kuizu ga arimasu yo." english="There's a quiz tomorrow, just so you know." />

<Hint>ね invites agreement on something shared; よ hands over new information the other person doesn't have yet. Mixing them up is a subtle but real mistake: saying 安いですね about a price only you know suggests you expect the listener to already agree, when they have no way to.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="あ、そこは危[あぶ]ないよ。" romaji="A, soko wa abunai yo." english="Oh, that's dangerous there!" />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="もう１２時[じゅうにじ]よ。" romaji="Mou juuniji yo." english="It's already midnight!" />

<Hint>よ can read as helpful or pushy purely based on tone and how often it's used, stacking it onto every sentence starts to sound like nagging. Softer speech, especially from women in many contexts, favors わよ or just よ said gently; a flat, clipped よ can land as blunt or irritated regardless of the words themselves.</Hint>
