ところだ attaches after a verb and marks a precise point on its timeline, depending on which form comes before it: dictionary form for "about to," ている for "in the middle of," and past た-form for "just finished."

<JSentenceBlock furigana="今[いま]からレポートを書[か]くところです。" romaji="Ima kara repooto o kaku tokoro desu." english="I'm just about to write the report." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="今[いま]、宿題[しゅくだい]の読[よ]み物[もの]を読[よ]んでいるところです。" romaji="Ima, shukudai no yomimono o yonde iru tokoro desu." english="I'm in the middle of reading the homework material right now." />

<Hint>書くところ, "about to write," describes a moment right before starting. 書いているところ, "in the middle of writing," describes the action actively in progress. Only one syllable of difference, but the timing described is completely different.</Hint>

<JSentenceBlock furigana="さっきお風呂[ふろ]に入[はい]ったところです。" romaji="Sakki ofuro ni haitta tokoro desu." english="I just got out of the bath a moment ago." />

<JSentenceBlock furigana="さっき歯[は]を磨[みが]いたところです。" romaji="Sakki ha o migaita tokoro desu." english="I just brushed my teeth a moment ago." />

<Hint>入ったところ, built on the past た-form, means the action just wrapped up, very recently, often paired with さっき (just now) to emphasize how fresh it is. This is a tighter, more immediate claim than ことがある's past-experience meaning, which can describe something from years ago.</Hint>
